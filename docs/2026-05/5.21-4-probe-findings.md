# Value-head probe findings (Mode A + Mode B)

**Date:** 2026-05-21
**Branch:** `main`
**Context:** Frozen-trunk probes of `epoch_005.pt` to test whether the dead-ReLU collapse from [`5.21-1`](./5.21-1-debugging-value-head.md) is a head-architecture problem or a representation problem upstream of the head.

## Headline

The trunk encodes only a *very weak* placement signal — the cleanest linear probe (`gap_linear`) edges past the always-1st baseline by +2.3 percentage points on val top-1 and never beats the marginal-entropy floor on CE. Spatial-head probes overfit before producing readable signal in this setup. The 5.21-1 framing of "dead-ReLU as architectural attractor" needs a small but important nuance: the attractor is *trained-state*, not architecture-intrinsic.

## Mode A reframe of the dead-ReLU

Mode A overfits a stratified 60-frame val batch with each head variant. Both `status_quo` (architecture matching `epoch_005.pt`'s value head) and `no_relu` reach loss < 0.005 in 100 steps — they have no trouble fitting 60 examples from a fresh init.

This refines [`5.21-1`](./5.21-1-debugging-value-head.md) §"Why the head ended up here". The structural fragility list there still stands — single-channel bottleneck, ReLU upstream of the linear classifier — but the *pathology* requires the corpus-scale optimization trajectory to reach. A freshly-initialized head with the same architecture, optimized against 60 stratified frames, does not fall into the all-negative-pre-activation basin. The basin is real and deep on the loss landscape over 1.5M correlated training frames; it is not a property reachable from any initialization on any data.

Operationally: this means **fixing the architecture alone may not be sufficient** even at corpus scale. The trained head sat in a bad basin; a different head architecture might just sit in a different bad basin given the same upstream signal pressure. Mode B partly tests that.

## Mode B results

**Setup.** Frozen trunk from `epoch_005.pt`. Fresh head variants instantiated per run. Sub-split val perspectives 80/20 at the perspective level (101 perspectives → 81 train_probe / 20 val_probe). Stream trunk features (frozen) to a CPU-resident cache once; mini-batch the head training over the cache. AdamW lr=3e-4, weight_decay=1e-4, 5 epochs, batch_size=128. Reproduce: `training/scripts/value_head_probe.py --mode probe` (full invocation in the run dir's `args.json`).

**Run dir.** `training/data/probes/20260521-110841-value-probe/` — contains `output.log` (full stdout), `probe_summary.json` (per-epoch curves), `probe_curves.png`, `args.json`. (Gitignored; numbers reproduced below.)

**Val_probe class distribution.** Frame counts `[2912, 2690, 1877, 0, 486, 1219, 0, 0]` — 9,184 frames total. Random sub-sample of 20 perspectives missed 4th, 7th, and 8th place entirely. Resulting baselines for *this* sub-split:

- Marginal entropy: **1.4719 nats** (not the full-val 1.5195 the probe script hardcodes — see open gaps below)
- Always-1st top-1: **0.317** (the 1st-place fraction)

**Final-epoch numbers across variants:**

| variant | params | val CE | Δ marginal (1.47) | val top-1 | Δ always-1st (0.317) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| status_quo | 9,353 | 5.61 | +4.14 | 0.226 | −0.091 |
| no_relu | 9,353 | 4.47 | +3.00 | 0.260 | −0.057 |
| gelu | 9,353 | 3.95 | +2.48 | 0.199 | −0.118 |
| wide_conv | 74,768 | 6.04 | +4.57 | 0.283 | −0.034 |
| **gap_linear** | **1,032** | **1.57** | **+0.10** | **0.340** | **+0.023** |

**Read.** The four spatial-preserving heads all show the same memorization signature: train loss collapses to ≤0.007 by epoch 5, val CE soars to 3.9-6.0 (worse than uniform), val top-1 drops *below* the always-1st baseline (the heads are confidently wrong often enough that argmax is worse than the dumb baseline). Effective sample size is closer to 81 (distinct perspectives) than 31,444 (raw frames) due to within-perspective frame correlation — 9k+ params is more than enough to memorize 81 trajectory signatures.

`gap_linear` (1,032 params, `GlobalAvgPool → Linear(128, 8)` — the textbook linear-probe shape) lacks the capacity to memorize and is forced to find a generalizable mapping. It lands at val CE 1.57 (just above the marginal floor) and val top-1 0.340 (just above the always-1st floor). The +2.3 pp top-1 edge is the only positive signal in the run.

A fuller interpretive walkthrough of these numbers lives in [`docs/teaching/interpreting-bc-runs.md`](../teaching/interpreting-bc-runs.md).

## Implication for Phase 4

Taking the Mode A and Mode B results together — provisionally, pending the methodology gaps in the next section — the picture is:

- The dead-ReLU collapse is real but is reached via the corpus optimization trajectory, not the head's architecture alone. Fresh-init variants of the same shape don't fall in given a small enough batch.
- The trunk after 5 epochs of policy+pass-only gradient (per [`5.21-1`](./5.21-1-debugging-value-head.md), the value head's gradient was ≈0 from early epoch 1) carries only a barely-positive placement signal under a linear probe. Sufficient to give a linear classifier a hair above always-1st on top-1; not sufficient to beat the marginal floor on CE.

The Phase-4 implication is that **head-level fixes (remove ReLU, swap activation, widen conv, even the `option C` PyramidModule extension stubbed at `training/bc/model.py:473-482`) may be insufficient on their own**. If the trunk is the bottleneck, swapping head architecture leaves the upstream representation unchanged. The natural Phase-4 sequence is:

1. Fix the value head structurally so its gradient flows.
2. Retrain (or warm-start from `epoch_005.pt` and continue training) with the working value gradient pushing the trunk to learn placement-relevant features.
3. Evaluate whether the trunk now encodes placement signal that a probe can extract.

The reinit-and-warm-start probe (Tier 1 #1 in [`5.21-1`](./5.21-1-debugging-value-head.md)) is the natural intermediate test of this hypothesis — it differs from our frozen-trunk Mode B by letting the trunk keep updating via the now-flowing value gradient plus the existing policy+pass gradients. Untested.

## Open methodology gaps

The current Mode B is interpretable as a directional read, not a quantitative one. Before believing the numbers more precisely, four fixes:

1. **Per-run baseline.** `value_head_probe.py` hardcodes `VAL_MARGINAL_ENTROPY = 1.5195` (the full val-set marginal from [`5.21-1`](./5.21-1-debugging-value-head.md)). The sub-split val_probe has a different class distribution and a different floor (1.47 in this run). Compute per-run from `val_targets`, not from the constant. The "Δ marginal" column in the table above already uses the corrected 1.47; the script's printed verdict does not.
2. **Best-epoch reporting.** Final-epoch val is the worst-case for overfit-prone variants. Track + report best-epoch val loss and top-1 alongside the final-epoch numbers. For most spatial variants in this run, best val was at epoch 1 — still poor, but a different-shaped result.
3. **Regularization knobs.** No CLI surface for weight decay or dropout beyond the AdamW default `wd=1e-4`. Spatial heads under aggressive weight decay or with dropout on the flatten output may match `gap_linear`'s generalization. The result table can't tell us this yet. Adding `--probe-weight-decay`, `--probe-dropout`, or (cheapest) `--probe-epochs 1` as a soft early-stop fixes the question.
4. **Class-stratified sub-split.** Random 20% perspective sub-sample dropped 4th, 7th, and 8th place entirely. Class-stratified perspective sampling would ensure each placement class appears in val_probe at meaningful counts.

None of these are blocking for the directional read above, but the next pass on this question should address (1) and (2) at minimum.

## Engineering: MPS INT_MAX gotcha

The first Mode B attempt crashed with `RuntimeError: MPSGraph does not support tensor dims larger than INT_MAX` when computing the head forward over a 1.65 GB MPS-resident `train_feats` tensor (31,444 × 128 × 32 × 32 float32). Slicing the parent into 128-row mini-batches did **not** avoid it — MPS graph compilation references the parent tensor's dimensions, not just the slice's.

Workaround: cache features to CPU, move mini-batches to MPS at training time. On M1 unified memory the CPU↔MPS copy is bookkeeping, not real transfer, so the workaround is free locally. Code lives in `value_head_probe.py` `stream_trunk_features` (CPU-side cat) + `evaluate_head` / `train_head_minibatched` (per-minibatch `.to(device)`). Saved as a memory entry under `feedback_mps_int_max_cpu_cache` for the next ML-heavy session.

This will reliably bite again on any cache-heavy MPS work — Phase 4 cloud-vs-local decisions, future probe extensions, anything else that wants a large frozen-feature cache.
