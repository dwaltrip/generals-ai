# Greedy FFA Bot — Design Sketch (v1)

**Date:** 2026.05.24

**Purpose:** Design a relatively simple, hand-coded greedy bot to serve as a **fixed evaluation opponent** for a neural-net FFA bot. This doc captures the architecture, the named heuristics at each layer, the resolved design decisions, and the tuning levers. It is intended as a handoff reference for implementation.

**Companion docs:** `generals-io-game-mechanics.md` (rules), `5.02-8-ffa-strategic-intuitions-interview.md` (strong-human strategic intuition — see note in §1 on why most of it is deliberately *out of scope* for this bot).

---

## 1. Framing: what a fixed eval opponent needs

The eval bot's job is almost the inverse of the NN's job. The strategic-intuitions interview describes the sophisticated FFA reasoning we want the *NN* to eventually discover — relative-strength reads, conflict detection, "find the uncontested 4th player," dynamic attention reallocation. Baking that into the eval opponent is a mistake: it muddies what the benchmark measures (beating the bot would partly mean out-politicking a hand-coded politician) and makes the bot illegible and hard to reason about.

A fixed eval opponent wants three properties:

1. **Expands competently** — so it can't be trivially out-economy'd.
2. **Defends its general** — so the NN can't "win" via a single naive rush. This is the property people skip and the one that matters most: without it, the NN learns one cheese line, win rate pins at ~100%, and the metric tells you nothing. **The single biggest failure mode of a hand-coded eval opponent is that the policy learns to exploit its specific blind spots rather than learning to play. Undefended generals are the fattest blind spot.**
3. **Makes occasional attacks** — so it isn't purely passive.

That competence target is roughly "a competent beginner human." Beating it consistently means the NN has learned basic economy plus basic combat — a meaningful bar, even though it doesn't test sophisticated FFA politics.

**The exploitability test** (used throughout to decide whether a heuristic belongs in the bot): *Can the opponent trip the trigger cheaply and unilaterally, and does tripping it reward real skill or cheese?* A weakness that can only be exploited by learning real strategy is a **good** weakness for a benchmark. A weakness that's a free, local, repeatable lever (e.g. "show a frontier → bot flinches → herd it around the map") is a **bad** weakness.

## 2. Architecture overview

### 2.1 The core tension

The bot emits exactly **one move per timestep**, but every productive action in generals — taking a city, breaking a defended position, killing a general — requires consolidating army across many timesteps before the payoff move lands. How a bot resolves that tension is the whole design.

### 2.2 The chosen shape: target + route

All of the bot's "modes" (defend / expand / take-city / attack) are the **same operation with a different target**:

- expand → route to nearest empty land
- city → route to the city
- attack → route to enemy general / frontier
- defend → route to an intercept point or the general

So the entire decision each cycle collapses to two functions:

- **`pick_target()`** — a fixed priority ladder that returns a target (and a commit amount).
- **`route_to(target)`** — emit one move that advances army toward it.

This is a state machine in disguise — the "mode" is just which branch of `pick_target` fired — but **there is no transition logic to write, because re-running `pick_target` each cycle *is* the transition.** That's what makes it lower-complexity than an explicit state machine while playing about as well.

### 2.3 Two cadences

- **Planning** happens occasionally — when there's no active plan, the plan is exhausted/spent, or an interrupt fires — and produces a target plus a plan toward it.
- **Acting** happens every timestep and just emits the next move from the current plan.

Logically `pick_target` re-evaluates each tick; the implementation may cache the active plan and invalidate it on interrupt / spent / exhausted (see `spent_push`, §6).

## 3. The decision ladder (`pick_target`)

Evaluated top-down each tick; the first gate to fire sets the target. Read "down" as *fall through to lower priority*, "fires" as *this gate sets the target and we stop*.

| Priority | Gate | Fires when | Target |
|----------|------|------------|--------|
| 1 (interrupt) | **Defend** | `IncursionThreat` | `InterceptTile`, or the general (by severity) |
| 2 (interrupt) | **Kill-shot** | `KillShotFeasible` | the revealed enemy general |
| 3 | **Expand** | `LocalLandValue` high | `ExpandTarget` (nearest empty) |
| 4 (fallback) | **Attack** | always (fallback) | `AttackTarget` (`PrimaryTarget`'s frontier) |

`City` is a deferred 5th gate that slots between Expand and Attack (see §7.2). De-escalation is *not* a gate — it's the absence of conversion (see §5.4).

Defend and Kill-shot are **interrupts**: they may preempt an in-progress plan. Expand and Attack are the default flow.

## 4. Layer model

The bot is organized in four layers (0–3). Essentially all behavioral character lives in **Layer 2 thresholds**; Layer 1's ordering is fixed and Layer 3 is shared mechanism.

### Layer 0 — world model (recomputed each tick, before any gate)

The perceive step. The bot's only real state:

- **`ThreatWindow`** — sliding buffer of the last `N` ticks of the largest visible enemy stack, as `(pos, army, dist_to_general)`. The single stateful structure; only the Defend gate consumes it.
- **`ContactSet`** — opponents ever seen. The on/off switch for the field-wide reads: empty early game (threat/target machinery inert), populated mid-game (live). Matches the interview's contact-gating.
- **`RelativeStrength(p)`**, **`CityCountEst(p)`** — scoreboard reads over `ContactSet`; city count from the peacetime growth-rate inference (growth − 1 = city count).
- **`ConflictFlag(p)`** — land-delta correlation: is `p` already fighting a third party.
- **`PrimaryThreat`** = argmax `ThreatScore`; **`PrimaryTarget`** = argmax `TargetScore`. Two derived rankings sharing inputs but treating relative strength with **opposite sign** (see §5.4) — which is why they're two scores, not one weighted sum.

### Layer 1 — `pick_target` (the gate ladder, §3)

Output: `(target, commit_amount)`. Re-run every tick.

### Layer 2 — medium heuristics composing each gate

See §5 for the per-gate detail. Each gate is a thin composite over Layer 0 plus a couple of its own metrics.

### Layer 3 — shared low-level machinery

See §6. The fixed scaffolding the gates defer to; the reason the bot is small.

## 5. Per-gate detail

### 5.1 Defend (interrupt)

**Trigger is a territory-incursion read, not a general-at-risk read.** The binary question is *"is an opponent on a sustained attack path driving into the heart of my territory?"* General-safety is the most severe special case, not the definition — defense earns its keep on two fronts, **survival** and **land-denial** (their land near me becomes their army near me), and a deep strike threatens both while a perimeter graze threatens neither enough to react.

```
IncursionThreat = Closing AND Eating
```

- **`Closing`** — `dist_to_general` is trending down over the window: distance decreased in ≥ `M` of the last `N` steps (robust form, not a raw endpoint compare — a mountain reroute can briefly flatten distance while the army still bores inward). Distance is **BFS graph distance** over known-passable tiles, not Manhattan (Manhattan lies around mountain walls, exactly where intrusions funnel).
- **`Eating`** — within radius `R` of the threat's current position, the count of tiles I owned *at window-start* that are *now* enemy-owned is ≥ `eat_min`. Anchored on past-ownership so the metric doesn't cannibalize itself as the army advances. This trail of just-flipped tiles also gives a cheap directional substrate.

**Why there's no separate "threatening army size" gate:** a sustained, successful inward capture *reveals* a threatening army — if it keeps eating turn after turn without being stopped, it is by definition winning every fight it picks, so it's already large relative to local defense. Size is revealed, not tested. And because a strike army bleeds as it advances (leaves 1 behind per tile, fights each garrison), "still large *and* still closing" is the deep-strike fingerprint; "shrinking or turning away" is the all-clear.

**Response target — chosen by one severity gate:**

```
CanReachGeneral(stack) = stack_army > general_garrison + PathBleed
```
where `PathBleed` = sum of my army on the BFS shortest path from the threat to my general.

- **High severity** (`CanReachGeneral` true) → target = the **general** tile. The safe-but-passive rally; used *only* here. (It satisfies survival, fully abandons land-denial, and is exploitable — a good opponent reads that you won't contest and eats freely — so we restrict it to the genuine survival case.)
- **Otherwise** → target = **`InterceptTile`**, the tile `lookahead` steps from the invader *along the shortest known path from invader → general*. Defining the intercept as a point on that path means BFS bends it around mountains for free, and bakes in the accepted assumption that "inward" ≈ "toward general." Intercepting does **both** jobs and makes the inward-first mechanic work for you: you become the defender at the fortified tile, with defender's advantage on ties.

**Commit amount:** gather until the army arriving at the target ≥ `ThreatWindow` stack army + `margin`. Since the eater bleeds as it comes, its current size already overstates what you'll collide with, so match-plus-small-margin is conservative-enough without stripping the frontier.

**Exit is free:** `pick_target` re-runs each tick; `IncursionThreat` goes false when the army turns, shrinks, or the collision resolves. Watch for **thrash** at the threshold (defend/expand flicker) — fix only if observed, with hysteresis (fire at a higher bar than you release at).

**Two correct behaviors fall out for free:** an enemy army parked in its own land never trips this (not closing, not eating); the no-contact early game trivially returns false (the bot just expands until someone comes at it).

### 5.2 Kill-shot (interrupt)

A revealed enemy general is the highest-value, most time-sensitive prize in the game (capturing it is +N land in one move via inheritance, and it's a *perishable resource* — once anyone takes it, it becomes a city and the whole-estate prize is gone permanently). That profile — rare, highest-value, time-sensitive — is the profile of defense, so it gets the same treatment: **a preempting interrupt, not a term in a weighted sum.** This is how the "focus your target vs. grab the free kill" tension resolves — it was never a blend, it's a priority ordering.

```
KillShotFeasible(g) = a revealed enemy general g exists
                      AND gatherable_stack >= g.army + PathBleed + Margin(travel_time)
```

- **`Margin(travel_time)`** scales with **gather-tree depth / path length** (which BFS already gives you). "Longer travel → more reinforcement" collapses the entire reinforcement model into this one distance-scaled term — no defender modeling needed.
- **Competence ceiling (by design):** the static estimate *undershoots* against an alert defender, because the moment your stack is adjacent they gain vision and reinforce while you gather. So the bot reliably finishes **undefended or distracted** generals (the realistic win against an over-extended opponent) and won't out-micro an active defense. Clean, legible ceiling.
- **Free bonus, optional:** `ConflictFlag` is already computed for `TargetScore`, so relaxing the kill-shot margin when the target is busy fighting a third party is a one-line add. Skippable for v1.

### 5.3 Expand (default)

Fires on **`LocalLandValue`** — high when near, worthwhile empty land remains; low when only distant scraps are left. Target **`ExpandTarget`** = nearest reachable neutral empty tile (0 garrison, always captures), sourced from **`LargestFrontierStack`** rather than crawling thin 2-army tiles (the "leave 1 behind" rule strands an army on every thin move, so move accumulated army). Nearest-empty fans outward and fills the local pocket densely — good land-per-move efficiency.

- The diffuse-fill vs. far-tendril distinction collapses: a tendril is just what "nearest empty" *becomes* once the local pocket is eaten, so the transition is board-driven, not a phase clock.
- **No dedicated information-gathering expeditions in v1.** Vision/general-safety scouting is folded into a directional tiebreak: when choosing among equal-nearest empty tiles, bias toward the unwatched corridors near the general (the lanes a deep strike would use — same geometry the Defend detector watches). The ≥10-apart general rule gives a guaranteed dead-zone prior. Far-field scouting is a knob.
- **Aggression via direction, not a new branch:** when `LocalLandValue` goes low (only far scraps remain), bias `ExpandTarget` toward `PrimaryTarget`'s frontier instead of distant neutral tiles. The bot flows into contact on its own, which generates kill-shot opportunities. This is the **passivity dial** that prevents the turtle-blob failure mode.

### 5.4 Attack (fallback) + the threat/target model

Target **`AttackTarget`** = `PrimaryTarget`'s nearest visible frontier (or its revealed general). Attacking the nearest frontier doubles as general-hunting (vision flows toward them), so there's no separate hunt logic.

**Two scores from a shared input set** (not one weighted sum), because relative strength points *opposite* ways for the two questions — the players you most want to attack are the ones least likely to attack you:

Shared inputs (all cheap; vision + always-visible scoreboard): recent harm, proximity (BFS dist from their nearest force to my general), relative strength (with `CityCountEst` as a leading indicator), `ConflictFlag`.

- **`ThreatScore(p)`** — "who do I avoid opening a front against, and who biases my defense." High when: recently attacked me, close to my general, *similar* strength. Stronger-than-me also reads high. **Chosen sign:** stronger *and* busy fighting someone else reads as *lower* threat (distracted = I'm safer).
- **`TargetScore(p)`** — "who can I profitably kill." High when: close, *weak* relative to me, not already a high threat. Two **gates** (not weights) do the real work:
  - *stronger-than-me* → target ≈ 0 (don't initiate), unless already in contact-conflict with me (then it's self-defense, `ThreatScore` owns it).
  - *stronger + busy elsewhere* → suppressed *further*, not raised — "wait until I'm stronger, don't provoke."
  - *weak + not yet strong* → urgency bump — the closing-window "kill them before they grow" instinct, the same gate sign-flipped.

**De-escalation is the weak form: "don't escalate," not "actively flee."** On contact with a non-`PrimaryTarget`, the spent-push → re-evaluate behavior *is* the de-escalation — the bot declines to open a front but holds position. **A holding bot can't be herded; only a fleeing one can.** The strong form (route away to signal peace) is the more human play and stays a knob. Field-wide `ThreatScore`/`TargetScore` conditions pass the exploitability test (you can't flip your standing with one local action); the residual exploit (stay non-threatening, then strike) is exactly the FFA skill we *want* the NN to learn — a good weakness.

**Contact reclassification (expand/attack are one motion):** a stack pushing a fog target that gains vision of an enemy tile checks `my_stack_army > visible_enemy_frontier_army`. If yes, keep pushing (the fog-push *is* an attack now; `pick_target`'s attack branch picks it up). If no, the push is spent — stop, re-evaluate. No redirect-away, no peacefulness signaling in v1.

**Stickiness:** `PrimaryTarget` is recomputed each tick for *scoring*, but an in-flight attack gather does **not** churn when two targets score closely — reuse the Defend hysteresis as a milder-weighted version. Once attack commits, the plan is sticky until it completes, goes spent, or a higher gate preempts it. (A gather is a multi-turn investment; churning mid-gather wastes it.)

**Abort & recall reuse existing machinery:** an attack that stops capturing toward its target (stack no longer larger than what's in front of it) is `spent` — stop, re-evaluate. Committing the stack strips home, so the Defend interrupt recalls it if a counter-rush appears. Keeping a home garrison so it isn't *fully* stripped is the parked defensive-reserve knob (§7.1).

## 6. Shared low-level machinery (Layer 3)

Every gate defers here; nothing in this layer changes between difficulty tiers.

- **`bfs_dist(a, b)` / `path(a, b)`** — graph distance / shortest path over known-passable tiles (respecting mountains and known cities). One routine serves the Defend distance metric, `PathBleed`, the intercept point, and travel-time — all reads off the same BFS.
- **`gather_tree(target)`** — BFS from the target over owned tiles, then parent-ward moves from the leaves inward; collects the whole stack at the target in `tree_depth` moves. Returns `(next_move, arriving_army_estimate, depth)`. **Defend, Kill-shot, and Attack all call this identically** — the single "assemble a stack at a point" primitive. (This is the piece that separates "ant crawl" from "actually threatens"; do it properly rather than "march biggest stack one step.")
- **`route_to(target)`** — dispatches to a single-step push (Expand; no consolidation needed for 0-garrison empty land) or a `gather_tree` step (everything else).
- **`spent_push(plan)`** — detects a plan no longer making progress (not capturing toward target / stack no longer larger than what's ahead) → abort, re-evaluate. Combined with `pick_target` re-running each tick, this is the entire exit/abort system.
- **`LargestFrontierStack()`, `nearest_unowned(from)`** — cheap source/target lookups for Expand.

## 7. Resolved design decisions (v1)

### 7.1 Fat general / peacetime reserve — accepted, with a note

During peace, Expand spends the distributed land-tick surplus but never touches the general's stack, and `gather_tree` only pulls it when Defend or Attack fires — so the general's army accumulates and sits. This emergent reserve is simple and *great* for defense (the reserve is already parked on the most important tile). **Accepted for v1, conditional on Attack reliably triggering** (the reserve must actually get spent, or the bot turtles).

> **Note — "optimizing peacetime" (future knob):** the fat-general reserve is the classic turtle weakness — a large army doing zero economic work, on one tile, handed to anyone who breaks through via inheritance. One possible later refinement is a **reserve cap**: accumulate up to a threshold, above which the excess becomes spendable by Expand. The reserve size is also what the Defend interrupt recalls when Attack over-commits forward — so "how much the general holds back" is effectively the bot's whole risk posture in a single number.

### 7.2 City-less economy — accepted, with a note

No city capture in v1. City is pure economy with no survival stakes, and taking one pulls army off both frontier and defense for a long vulnerable gather window — real complexity (affordability, timing, safety) for a long-game payoff.

> **Note — eval implication:** a bot that never takes cities is economically out-scaled over a long game by anything that does (including the NN once it learns). So games against this bot are mostly **decided in the opening and early-mid**, before the city gap compounds. The eval signal therefore measures "can the NN survive contact and convert an early edge," not long-game economic play. Acceptable and arguably desirable for a beatable fixed opponent — chosen deliberately, not inherited. City becomes the first real feature gate (between Expand and Attack): opportunistic only — nearest known neutral city when a frontier stack ≥ garrison+2 is already available, no active threat, early enough to pay back.

### 7.3 Attack stickiness — reuse Defend hysteresis (milder)

See §5.4. In-flight gathers don't churn on close-scoring targets.

### 7.4 Opening — greedy nearest-empty is fine for v1

For FFA, a sloppy opening isn't very costly. Greedy nearest-empty (§5.3) is the v1 opening. The fixed fan-out from the perfect-start solver stays a knob if the bot proves too weak a sparring partner.

## 8. Known limitations (record, no action needed)

- **Single-threat blindness.** `ThreatWindow` tracks one largest stack, so the bot is blind to being poked by two players simultaneously. Acceptable, legible limitation.
- **Discovered-general memory required.** Kill-shot relies on *persistent memory of discovered generals*. Structures never move, and a general only stops being a general when captured (globally announced in chat), so a general seen once stays known — which is exactly what lets Kill-shot fire on a now-fogged general. The implementation must honor this (don't forget a general just because it re-fogs).
- **No multi-front modeling, no alliance behavior, no meta-reasoning about being seen.** All deliberately out of scope (these belong in the NN).

## 9. Tuning levers (most → least consequential)

The top levers in every branch share the same shape: a *when-to-commit* threshold and a *how-committed* margin. The machinery is shared; the character is in these dials.

1. **`N` (window length), `M` (closing-steps required within the window), and `eat_min` (Defend trigger sensitivity).** Jointly set twitchy-vs-lax. This decides whether the bot is even a *valid* benchmark (too lax → eats rushes; too twitchy → abandons economy chasing phantoms). Tune first and most carefully.
2. **Kill-shot margin + its travel-time coefficient.** The bot's main teeth. Too eager → throws stacks at generals it can't take and strips itself; too timid → never commits, reads as passive.
3. **`LocalLandValue` flip threshold (Expand → directional).** The passivity dial — the thing standing between the bot and the turtle-blob. Too high → never seeks contact; too low → abandons free economy to pick fights early.
4. **Severity margin in `CanReachGeneral`.** How often the bot drops into the passive rally-to-general posture vs. the stronger intercept. The main exploitability dial on defense.
5. **Commit margins** (Defend collision margin; Attack gather margin). Over-commit strips the frontier; under-commit stalls or loses the collision.
6. **`lookahead` (intercept projection).** Too short → collide too late; too long → gather to empty space on a feint/reroute. Forgiving in the middle.
7. **Detection radius `R`, distance metric, threat-identity tracking.** Detector-noise knobs; sensible defaults (a few tiles, BFS, single-largest-stack) work.
8. **Hysteresis / min-commit duration.** Zero by default (Defend and Attack); introduce only if thrash is observed.

Difficulty tiers (Easy / Medium / Hard), when wanted, are all Layer-2 reorderings/threshold changes — e.g. an aggressive variant is just Attack reordered above some of Expand, plus enabling the City gate. No routing/machinery changes.

## 10. Eval-harness considerations (for the benchmark, not the bot)

The bot is the easy part; these determine whether the eval *number* is signal or noise.

- **Use denser metrics than win rate.** Binary win/loss in 8-player FFA is brutally sparse and noisy. Track **average final placement (1–8)**, **survival time**, and **peak land/army share** — these give a smooth improvement curve long before the NN starts actually winning.
- **Hold the eval bot out of training.** If it's also a curriculum opponent, win-rate-vs-it stops being an unbiased generalization signal. Keep at least one frozen, eval-only version.
- **Fight overfitting with an ensemble, not a point.** A single deterministic bot is one set of quirks to exploit. Report against a small ensemble (3–4 bots differing in expansion aggression, gather cadence, city play) plus per-bot **seeded** stochasticity (a bot that always gathers on turn 25 is a clock the NN can read).
- **Reduce variance by fixing seeds.** Map gen, spawns, and tie-breaks seeded so checkpoints face identical scenarios (paired comparison kills most FFA variance). Run hundreds of games per checkpoint; report confidence intervals. `1 NN vs 7 bots` is the cleanest layout; give each bot its own seed to avoid degenerate symmetric dynamics.
- **A difficulty ladder beats a single opponent** as a progress signal — watch the NN climb Easy → Medium → Hard. Each rung is a Layer-2 config (above), trivial to define.
- **Keep it cheap.** Pure heuristics + BFS on a small grid. Do **not** reuse the beam-search/SA solver as the eval bot — too slow, and too strong/weird in the midgame to be a coherent beatable baseline.

> v1 minimum to unblock training: a single Medium-tier bot (Defend + Kill-shot + Expand + Attack fallback, no cities). Grow the suite later.
