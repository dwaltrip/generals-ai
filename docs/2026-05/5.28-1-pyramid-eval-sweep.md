# Pyramid vs direct: eval sweep findings + post-mortem on 5/27 training runs

**Date:** 2026-05-28
**Branch:** `main`
**Scope:** Reviewing where the project stands post train-spike-1; loss-level comparison of three 5/27 training runs; 200-game eval sweep (pyramid vs direct, temp sweep, self-vs-self noise floor) using newly-built diagnostics + sweep tooling; implications for the next training run and the upcoming big run.

## Where we are

Status check across the project, several weeks past train-spike-1:

- **Cloud infra is solid on Modal.** `run_bc_modal.py` is the entry point. T4 is the default and the smallest available GPU class; the existing recipe (bs=512 direct, fp16, T4) runs cleanly. L4 is the smallest class that fits pyramid+bs=512 — T4's 16 GB ceiling OOMs on that config.
- **Obs tensor:** 96 channels; `opp_N_has_seen` + signed ownership transitions + production-subtracted delta landed; two fog leaks closed. Still pending: `opp_N_city_inference` zero-stub channels (~7% of obs is dead weight), normalization-divisor calibration, and the 3-invariant testing strategy from `5.21-8`.
- **Model architecture:** padding mask through Pass/Value heads landed; `pyramid` ValueHead variant available as a runtime knob alongside `direct`. Width bump (128/128/160 → 256/256/320) still deferred — it's the next architectural change that would make H100 worth its premium.
- **Eval tooling (new this week):** `packages/eval-bot/` (hand-coded baseline, M3 done), `packages/game-runner/` (generic N-player game loop), `packages/eval-tools/scripts/run_adhoc.py` (ad-hoc eval session runner). Self-play demo working — 2-hour BC checkpoints play recognizable generals.io.
- **What's missing for confidence:** no big training run yet. Current checkpoints are all 2-3 hr T4 runs (≤ 5 epochs, ≤ 24k games). Eval-bot wins a meaningful fraction of head-to-heads against these checkpoints.

## Takeaways from 5/27's three training runs

Three runs, same `cloud_24k.json` slice, same 1500-max-batches recipe, same seed=0:

| Run | bs | head | samples/ep | train_pol | val_pol | train_value | val_value | val_top1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T21-39 | 256 | direct  | 384k | 1.066 | 1.099 | 0.281 | **2.353** | 0.678 |
| T21-50 | 256 | pyramid | 384k | 1.054 | 1.084 | 0.258 | **2.316** | 0.682 |
| T21-42 | 512 | direct  | 768k | 1.032 | 1.049 | 0.562 | **1.755** | 0.692 |

(All values are at epoch 5, the final epoch of each run.)

**Pipeline reproducibility confirmed.** The 5/27 bs=512 direct run essentially reproduces 5/23's 1st-poc run (val_policy 1.049 vs 1.045, top1 0.692 vs 0.693). The padding mask change between those two runs produced no measurable change in loss curves at this recipe.

**Head variant signal at bs=256 is small.** Pyramid wins by 1.5% val_policy, ~0.04 val_value, 0.4pp top1. The 5.24-1 expectation ("no big swing at small data scale") holds. +19% wall (matches FLOPs).

**The headline finding: batch size, not head variant, was the big mover for val_value.** Train value loss is *higher* at bs=512 (0.56) than at bs=256 (0.28), but val value is dramatically *better* at bs=512 (1.76 vs 2.32-2.35). Textbook less-overfitting signature. Mechanism: value target is constant per (game, perspective) — at bs=256, a batch sees ~30-40 distinct value targets and the head finds features that distinguish *training games* (memorizes); at bs=512 it sees ~70-80 distinct targets per batch and is forced to learn features that distinguish *placement classes* (generalizes). Same direct head; completely different generalization behavior, purely from batch class-diversity.

**val_policy descends in every run we've trained, at every batch size — "still descending" is a real signal for the primary loss.** The bs-conditional behavior is on val_value: at bs=256, train_value bottoms out around 0.26 while val_value plateaus around 2.3 (the head has memorized training labels and gets nothing new from more epochs); at bs=512, train_value sits near 0.5 and val_value descends with it. Implication: at bs=256, additional epochs train the policy further but the value head is stuck. bs=512 unstuck both. "Train longer" is the right call for val_policy at any batch size; "fix the value head" requires bs=512.

## The eval sweep

Building blocks added this session:

- **Per-tick model diagnostics in `ModelAgent`.** `agent.py` now records per-tick: value head's placement distribution stats (`exp_placement`, `top_prob`, `entropy`), pass-head sigmoid prob, masked policy distribution stats (`top1_prob`, `top3_prob`, `entropy`). Exposed via `get_diagnostics()` which `MetricsCollector` picks up via duck-typed protocol. Persisted under `policy_diagnostics` in each game's `metrics.json` and embedded in `results.jsonl`.
- **Sweep orchestrator:** `packages/eval-tools/scripts/eval_sweep.sh`. Runs 5 matchups (200 games) — primary 80-game pyramid-vs-direct at temp=0.8, ±40 games at temp=0.5 and 1.0, plus 20-game self-vs-self noise floor per head.
- **Sweep analyzer:** `packages/eval-tools/scripts/analyze_sweep.py`. Parses a sweep dir and reports win-rate table (Wilson CI + one-sided binomial p), self-vs-self balance, pyramid win rate vs temperature, per-policy diagnostic means split by win/loss, plus a value-head trajectory section.

**Sweep results — bs=256 direct vs bs=256 pyramid, n=200:**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## What we learned

**Pyramid > direct holds across temperatures, but the gap shrinks with temp.** 67.6% → 61.8% → 56.2% as temp rises 0.5 → 0.8 → 1.0. Mechanism: high temp adds sampling noise that buries architectural differences. Practical implication: **use temp=0.5 (or lower) when evaluating architectural choices** — it's the cleanest signal-to-noise ratio. Higher temps obscure real differences.

**Draws scale with temp.** 8% at temp=0.5, 20% at temp=1.0. Stalemate-by-stochasticity: noisy policies waste moves and stuck endgames result. Raising `max_turns` would let more draws resolve, but the underlying cause is the inference temperature; lower temp probably gets you more value than longer games.

**Noise floor is clean at the slot level.** Self-vs-self matchups show no slot bias (9-10 and 11-8 by literal seat, well within binomial range for n=20). The sim is slot-symmetric under swap-slots. *Policy-instance variance* (which of two identical-policy instances happens to win) is higher (one self-match landed 5-14-1 by original-policy-index) — useful to keep in mind when interpreting single-matchup results around n=20.

**The behavioral signature of pyramid's win is the pass rate, not the policy or value head.**

| Mean pass rate | temp=0.5 | temp=0.8 | temp=1.0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct  | 33% | 26% | 21% |
| Pyramid | **15%** | **13%** | **12%** |

Pyramid passes roughly half as often as direct, consistently across all temps. Direct's pass rate is also strongly temp-dependent (lower temp → more passing) because the pass head's sigmoid threshold dominates the bernoulli sample. Pyramid's pass head is structurally identical to direct's (we didn't touch that head) but reads trunk features that produce systematically lower pass probabilities. **Most likely mechanism: trunk regularization shaped by pyramid's value-head gradient flow** — pyramid's deeper value head sends different gradients into the shared trunk during BC training, the trunk learns different features, the pass head reads those features and outputs lower pass probabilities. The lift in win rate is *downstream* of a value-head change, even though pyramid's value head itself isn't doing anything special for this matchup.

**The value head is currently uninterpretable in 2-player eval.** Trained on 8-player FFA where placement ranges 1-8; we're running 2-player matches where placement is 1 (win) or "loser." Both direct and pyramid value heads produce roughly the same outputs (`exp_placement` ~2.88 across 80 games for both), with the same trajectory pattern (drift from 3.7 at game start to 2.5 at game end, regardless of outcome). `analyze_sweep.py`'s "Pyramid value-head trajectory" section labels this drift as a "memorization signature" — that label is misleading; it's flagging OOD behavior, not memorization. **TODO: relabel or skip that section for non-8p evals.**

**Single-game value-head reads are not representative of population behavior.** A single game can land far from the population mean — one observed at `exp_placement=1.13`, another at `3.25`, against a population mean of ~2.88 across 80 games. Future-self: use ≥30 games before drawing conclusions about value-head behavior. Eyeballing one game is misleading.

**Loss is a poor proxy for skill.** The 1.5% val_policy improvement of pyramid over direct corresponds to ~13pp win rate in eval — nearly an order of magnitude amplification. Architectural decisions made on loss curves alone, without eval signal, are very likely to be wrong about magnitudes even when they're directionally correct.

## Mechanism story (proposed, not proven)

The pass head and policy head architectures are *identical* between direct and pyramid runs. Same seed, same data, same trunk init. The only architectural difference is the value head:

- **direct value head:** Conv(C→1) → ReLU → flatten → Linear. ~9k params.
- **pyramid value head:** PyramidModule(N=0/M=0/M=0) → Conv(C→1) → ReLU → flatten → Linear. +800k params (~+20% total model).

So any difference in pass head output between the two runs has to come from differences in trunk features. Trunk features are shaped by the gradient signal backpropagated from all three heads during BC training; value head's gradient signal in particular is weighted by λ (the value loss coefficient). Pyramid's deeper head fits training labels harder and presumably routes a differently-shaped gradient back into the trunk. The trunk learns features that — incidentally — make the pass head less likely to fire.

Per the 5.21-5 framing, the value head's primary purpose in BC was always "trunk regularization via auxiliary task" — it's not a load-bearing prediction in BC, just a signal that shapes the shared backbone. This experiment is consistent with that framing: changing the auxiliary head's shape changes what the trunk encodes, even though the auxiliary task's outputs themselves don't matter for in-game decisions.

**This is not proven.** Three alternative explanations not ruled out:
1. **Capacity-anywhere helps.** Adding params anywhere would have similar effect; pyramid is one instance.
2. **Stochastic variance.** Same seed=0 but MPS/CUDA aren't bit-deterministic across architectures. The pyramid lift could partly be lucky init.
3. **Architectural correction.** Pyramid corrects a specifically undersized value head (5.24-1 called the original "tiny value head" anomalous). Diminishing returns may set in for further additions.

Distinguishing tests would each cost a small training run; deferred unless the question becomes load-bearing.

## Worthwhile next steps

In rough priority order:

1. **Pyramid + bs=512 training run (in progress on L4).** Completes the 2×2 factorial of {direct, pyramid} × {bs=256, bs=512}. Highest-information experiment we can run right now — existing runs cover three of the four cells, only pyramid×bs=512 is missing. Expected output: a checkpoint that should be the best yet — combines pyramid's policy/pass advantage with bs=512's value-head generalization fix.
2. **Eval pyramid+bs=512 vs current best at temp=0.5.** As soon as it lands. Two natural matchups:
   - pyramid+bs=512 vs pyramid+bs=256 — isolates the batch-size effect, head constant
   - pyramid+bs=512 vs direct+bs=512 — isolates the head effect, batch constant
   Each ~40 games at temp=0.5 is enough given how clean the temp=0.5 signal reads. Total wall ~20-30 min on MPS.
3. **`force_move=true` ablation.** If we run the pyramid-vs-direct sweep again with `force_move=true`, does pyramid's advantage shrink or disappear? If yes → win advantage is purely from pass rate. If no → policy head is also a contributor. ~50 min sweep on MPS. Settles the "is it just the pass head" question definitively.
4. **Width bump as a real experiment, not just a deferred entry.** 256/256/320 trunk + pyramid + bs=512 + L4 or A100. ~half-day cloud cost. Tests the "capacity in the trunk" hypothesis directly. Output: either "yes, more capacity helps too" → commit to bigger model for big run, or "no, the existing trunk is fine" → focus next phase on data scale alone.
5. **The big run.** 50k+ games × 10+ epochs, on the architecture that wins the above. Decision deferred until we have data on (2) and (4).

## Things to dig into / discuss further

- **The pass head deserves its own session.** It's the behavioral signature of the win, and we still don't understand it well. Worth tracing the pass head's learned bias toward "pass" — is it dominated by frames where passing was the BC target (early-game stillness, end-game waiting), or is something more structural going on? `pass_frac` in val was ~17% across all the recent runs, but per-tick pass probability is bouncing around 13-33% depending on model/temp. Gap is worth understanding.
- **OOD value head + future PPO.** The value head's 8p-trained / 2p-evaluated mismatch isn't blocking BC, but PPO will use the value head as a critic for advantage estimation. If the value head's predictions are uncalibrated in any setting we actually deploy, advantage estimates will be wrong. Probably need to: (a) train BC on multi-player-count slices so value head sees varied placement distributions, or (b) at minimum re-eval BC value-head calibration in 8p matches before any PPO work.
- **The bs=512-is-a-floor finding is load-bearing.** It changes how we read every loss curve going forward, and constrains the big-run config. Worth writing up properly somewhere more durable than this note — probably an addition to the network architecture or training docs. The "more samples per batch increases value-target class diversity" framing is a permanent insight, not session-of-the-moment context.
- **Eval methodology learning.** This session validated `--swap-slots` as a real slot-bias check, validated temp sweeps as a temperature-confound check, validated self-vs-self as a noise-floor check, and validated the diagnostics-per-tick instrumentation as the way to read *what models are actually doing*, not just *who's winning*. Worth codifying this as a recommended eval protocol — when running any future arch A/B, the minimum-viable eval is "n=80 at temp=0.5, with swap-slots + self-vs-self baselines + per-tick diagnostics." Less than that produces results that mislead.
- **`analyze_sweep.py` improvements.** The "memorization signature" tag in the value-head trajectory section is misleading for non-8p evals; it should detect player-count from the meta and either skip or relabel. Small fix.
- **Cost framing for the big run.** At the current 4.2-5M parameter scale, T4→H100 throughput speedup is probably 3-5×, not the 7-8× the cost ratio would suggest. GroupNorm/softmax fp32 ops are the ceiling on T4 *and* would still be the ceiling on H100; tensor cores are ~95% unused either way. H100 only earns its premium after the width bump makes tensor cores load-bearing.

## Tooling additions (file-level summary)

- `packages/self-play/self_play/agent.py` — per-tick diagnostic capture in `ModelAgent`. `get_diagnostics()` returns aggregates + per-tick series under keys `value`, `pass_prob`, `policy`, `decisions`, `config`. Picked up automatically by `MetricsCollector` via duck typing.
