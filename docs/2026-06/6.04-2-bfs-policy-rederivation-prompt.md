## 3. The rule under test: BFS-passability policy

This is a project-specific design decision for our model, **not** a generals.io mechanic. We are validating that the test suite properly tests this rule.

**Here it is:**

A cell is **passable** unless it falls into one of these impassable categories:

1. **Known mountain.** The perspective has had vision of the cell and saw a mountain.
2. **Unresolved structure ("structure-in-fog").** The perspective knows a structure exists at the cell (existence is always visible) but has **never** had vision of it, so it cannot tell mountain from city. Treated as impassable.
3. **Discovered enemy general.** An enemy general the perspective has gained vision of (i.e. known to be a general). The **self** general is always **passable**. An *undiscovered* enemy general does not count here — until seen it looks like an ordinary tile, and is passable.
4. **Unaffordable non-self city.** A cell the perspective has resolved to be a **city** and does **not** own, when it cannot afford to take it. Affordability: a non-self city is **passable iff**

   ```
   total_army  >  city_army × 4
   ```

   - `total_army` = the perspective's current total army (scoreboard).
   - `city_army` = the city's army **as the perspective currently sees it** if the cell is visible, otherwise **the army it last saw** there while it had vision.
   - The comparison is **strict** (`>`). At exactly `city_army × 4`, the city is **impassable**.
   - The factor is **4**.

   A city the perspective **owns** is always **passable** — the affordability formula does not apply to self cities.

Everything not in categories 1–4 is **passable**: standard tiles (owned, blank, or never-seen), self-general, the self-city, and any affordable non-self city.

## 7. Flagged conventions (reason about these explicitly; don't assume)

- **Never-seen land.** A non-structure tile the perspective has never had vision of (e.g. (2,0) in scenario A) — passable or not? Decide it from the policy in §3, and say which category (if any) it falls into.
- **Strictness of the affordability boundary.** §3 specifies `>` (strict). Make sure your A3/A5 (at-threshold) and A4/A6 (one-above) verdicts reflect that exactly — this boundary is one of the things the comparison is meant to pin down.
- **Self vs non-self city.** The self-city rule (always passable) and the non-self affordability formula are different branches. In A2, the self city is judged at a total army far below its garrison×4 — make sure you apply the right branch.
- **Source of `city_army`.** Whether a city is currently visible or fogged determines which army value applies (§3). For each city verdict, state whether the cell is visible or fogged at the final tick, and which army value you used.
