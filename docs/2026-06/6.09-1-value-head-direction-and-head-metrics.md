# Value-head direction and improved instrumentation

Date: 2026-06-09

Status: design sketch — reviewed; instrumentation plan ready to implement (first-increment scope and other open questions at end still to settle)

## Where we are

- Phase 1 is behavior cloning from a strong-player replay corpus, on a reduced-width DeepNash-derived network: a U-Net/pyramid trunk (GroupNorm), with three heads — policy, value (categorical placement, 8-way), and pass.
- The data pipeline is now tuned for the H100 (fp16 obs, n=20 starvation fixed); the largest run to date is ~24k perspectives. Throughput is no longer the bottleneck for the runs we want. We are hitting ~2.5k samples per second with the current (softly) preferred model size (0.75x the width of the DeepNash size, totaling around 10m-11m params).
- The basic model architecture is looking quite strong, and the model is learning well so far. More data, more epochs, and more parameters (e.g. larger "width") are all fairly consistently giving better results on the primary outcomes: making good moves and winning games. Turn these knobs up and we see lower val policy and pass loss, higher win-rates in evals vs. less-trained checkpoints, and "better vibes" for hand-analyzed replays (I look at games and assess if the play "looks better" using my knowledge and experience as a top FFA player). This all feels quite promising.
- The initial dense-history sweep (n=5/10/20) is done.
    - The results were a little disappointing: val policy seemed to be slightly worse with higher `n`. Theoretically, this feels quite wrong. Having more memory of recent tactical activity across visible tiles should be a very strong benefit for the model, especially in FFA, which is more strategically complex compared to 1v1 play.
    - The value-head also overfit sharply at higher `n` — `train_value` fell steeply while `val_value` rose above its marginal floor. Leading hypothesis (call it the **trunk-corruption hypothesis**): the dense-history channels double as trajectory fingerprints — exact army-deltas / ownership-transitions over `n` ticks nearly identify the specific game — and the value-head memorizes them, since its target is fixed per game while the policy's per-frame target isn't. The cost to the policy would be indirect: the shared trunk spends finite capacity on these spurious features instead of game-understanding ones. This fits what we see — the policy degraded on both train and val by similar amounts, which looks like a representation-quality drop rather than policy overfitting. If the hypothesis holds, fixing the value-head may let dense history *help* the policy, inverting this sweep's result. (The discriminating experiment is the first item under *Value-head architecture directions*.)
    - Mitigating this value-head degradation is an important goal of upcoming architecture work. More data may help on its own, but I want to make sure we are utilizing the available architecture knobs and not relying solely on scale to fix issues.
- In terms of high-level project direction, we are beginning to more seriously think about "Phase 2": self-play PPO, with the BC network as initialization (policy = actor, value = critic). I want to make sure the BC work leaves us well-poised for that transition.

## What's motivating this work

The value-head is the one murky part of an otherwise-promising model. Overfitting on it has recurred across many of our runs, and we can't tell whether that's relatively harmless (the head near its floor, trunk still healthy) or actively capping the policy (the trunk-corruption case above). The dense-history sweep gave an unusually clean instance, but the overfitting is a general, recurring issue, not a dense-history artifact. Closing that diagnostic gap is the point of this work.

A scattering of supporting notes:

- Example of the murkiness: an "at-the-floor" value-head whose loss seems stuck is easy to mistake for a broken one; in reality, being near the marginal-entropy floor is OK for the BC phase if the trunk is healthy and the head isn't strongly overfitting (train loss << val loss).
- I'm about to try improving the value-head architecture (and explore aux heads — see below), and without better instrumentation I won't be able to tell whether any given change actually helped. So the new metrics come first.
- The instrumentation is general — compare each head to its trivial baseline, watch the train/val gap, check for collapse, and so on — so it applies to any head we might add, not just the value-head. The same metrics will pay off for the auxiliary heads and future architecture work.
- A secondary goal is my own understanding. There's a lot of nuance in how to think about the value-head (the *Background* section below covers the BC-vs-PPO framing), and I want to internalize the key concepts from recent sessions — the marginal-entropy floor, game-phase stratification of `val_value`, the train/val gap — as I refine the BC work, do the "real" large BC training run(s), and move into PPO self-play (or something similar).
- See `docs/teaching/interpreting-bc-runs.md` for some early work on the metric framework (floors, train/val patterns, capacity-vs-effective-sample) and `docs/2026-06/6.07-4-dense-history-sweep-retro-prompt.md` for the sweep numbers this grew out of.

## Background: the value-head's role in BC vs. PPO

The value head plays a different role in each phase, and conflating them causes confusion.

**BC phase — scaffolding, not the product.** The policy head is what BC is training; the value head is auxiliary, there to help shape the shared trunk. Its target (final placement) is fixed per game, so it's a sparse, low-effective-sample signal — and for much of the game, especially early, placement carries little predictive signal. So an average `val_value` sitting near the marginal-entropy floor can be healthy; the genuine gains concentrate in later phases, which is what the players-remaining stratification is for. That same sparsity makes the head prone to memorization, which is what can corrupt the trunk. So in BC the bar is *do no harm*, not *be accurate* — the levers under consideration (smaller head, head-specific regularization, modest λ) all serve that goal, and the train/val gap is the thing to watch. Absolute accuracy barely matters.

**PPO phase — the actual critic.** Now it's operationally required for advantage estimation (GAE), and it trains on per-step bootstrapped TD targets — a far denser signal than BC's one label per trajectory. BC-phase overfitting in the head itself should largely wash out: the head is effectively retrained under a better regime, on a balanced outcome distribution (every self-play game produces the full placement range, unlike the win-skewed BC corpus). What matters shifts too: less CE calibration, more whether `V(s)` orders states by eventual outcome — and it must reduce to a sane scalar `V(s) = E[return]`, since GAE bootstraps against real rewards.

**The bridge between the phases is the trunk.** The head's own weights are cheap to relearn in PPO (or reinitialize, if we go that way); the trunk representation it helped shape carries over and is hard to undo. That's the real reason to keep the BC value head honest — not for its own sake, but to hand PPO a clean trunk.

## The two threads this supports

- **Value-head architecture work** (next): reduce overfitting / trunk corruption, and make the head a PPO-ready critic. Driven by the sweep's value-head memorization signature — the current, at-scale evidence (large train−val value gap with the `pyramid` head at 24k perspectives).
- **Auxiliary-head exploration** (horizon): denser, per-frame, self-supervised prediction targets that shape the trunk better than the sparse placement label — several of which are also strategically central to fog-of-war FFA.

Both depend on the same evaluation discipline, so instrumentation comes first.

## Principle: every head vs its trivial baseline

- **Value-head** → marginal-entropy floor (predict placement frequencies, ignore the board). Below = using input; at = honest but no signal; above = confidently wrong.
- **Pass-head** → base rate of `is_pass` (majority-class floor).
- **Policy-head** → no clean trivial baseline, and that's fine. Any input-blind reference (random-over-legal, or a naive expansion heuristic) is both (1) almost certainly far below the model and (2) badly underspecified — the action space (every owned tile × direction) is too large and complex to pin a canonical baseline to. So the operative reads are top-1/top-3 directly and run-over-run, plus **policy entropy** as a separate PPO-readiness signal (the exploration budget PPO inherits — not a quality measure).
- Each future aux-head → its own analogous baseline (defined per head).

The recurring failure mode to guard against: a head that hits its floor by *collapsing to the prior* (predicting the marginal every time) rather than by honestly varying with the input. Floor-matching alone can't tell these apart; the collapse checks below can.

## Standing panel (every run, all cheap)

| Metric | Reads | Anchor / watch-for |
|---|---|---|
| `val_value` vs **floor** | value-head generalization | below floor = signal; at = honest; above = confidently wrong |
| **train−val value gap** | trunk corruption / memorization | small = clean; `train_value→0` while `val` flat = memorization signature |
| **best-epoch** `val_value` | when value turns over | track the *minimum*, not final epoch (value's best epoch lands earlier than policy's) |
| `val_policy` + top-1/3 | policy quality + calibration | improving run-over-run; CE↔top-1 gap = calibration |
| **policy entropy** | exploration budget for PPO | watch it *not* collapse toward 0 |
| `pass_bce` + pass acc | pass-head | beats its base rate, not just "looks low" |

## One-off calibration reads (do once; promote to standing only if useful)

- **`val_value` by players-remaining bucket, each vs its own conditional floor** — the "is the head *sick* or *at its irreducible limit*" test. `(players_alive, starting_player_count)` looks like the strongest basis for a phase axis, at least theoretically: players-remaining moves in lockstep with the eliminations that determine placement, while the natural alternatives — hard-coded turn-number boundaries, percent-of-game-elapsed, and the like — are brittle across games and carry much less information about the high-level situation. The exact bucketing derived from those two inputs is an open question; the endgame bucket (2 remaining, floor ≈ ln 2) is the sharpest single diagnostic. Note: the bad-outcome buckets are thin by construction because the quality filter drops fast-death perspectives.
- **Value prediction entropy / argmax mobility** — the collapse check. Confirm the predicted placement distribution actually moves across states; a frozen critic carries no advantage signal for PPO.

## Implementation punch-list (current state → what's needed)

Audited against `packages/training/training/bc/{loss,eval,dataset}.py`, `scripts/compare_runs.py`, and the report generator.

**Already done — nothing to do**

- `train_value`/`val_value`, top-1/3, `val_policy`: computed, stored, shown.
- Pass accuracy + base rate (`pass_acc`, `pass_frac`): computed and stored; shown in `compare_runs` as *optional* columns, absent from `report.md`.

**Display-layer only (read existing `epochs.jsonl`, no training change)**

- Best-epoch `val_value` (argmin over epochs).
- train−val value gap (derived column).
- Surface `pass_acc`/`pass_frac` in `report.md`.

**Compute-and-thread (new metrics computed in the run; per-epoch ones flow to `epochs.jsonl` automatically)**

- **Marginal floor**: a *split* property — compute once at run start from the manifest (logic exists in `scripts/marginal_entropy.py`), store in run metadata, show as the reference next to `val_value`. Not per-epoch.
- **Policy entropy**: formula already exists in `inference.py`; compute over masked logits in the val loop, average over non-pass frames.
- **Value prediction entropy + argmax histogram** (collapse check): mirror the existing `action_dist` histogram pattern, on `value_logits`.

**Real plumbing (the only non-trivial item)**

- Per-frame **`players_alive`** is not in the batch dict (`encode_frame` returns obs/mask/targets only). It's cheap to derive: `total − count(elim_timestep ≤ t)`. Add it to the per-frame dict.
- Then stratify. First cut: a **standalone offline script** (load checkpoint → run val once → dump per-frame `(value_ce, players_alive, placement)`), bucket + per-bucket floor offline. This keeps the heaviest item out of the training loop and lets us choose bucket boundaries before baking them in.

**Suggested order**: display-layer freebies → the three compute-and-threads (completes the standing panel) → offline stratification dump.

## Value-head architecture directions (forward-looking, lower confidence)

Placement stays the critic target: it's the true outcome, it aligns with the eventual PPO placement reward, and denser proxies (land/army rank) only loosely track winning. The directions here aim to make that placement critic honest and PPO-ready. Denser per-frame signals that genuinely correlate with the outcome live in the aux-head menu below, as auxiliary targets rather than critic replacements — with one tracked exception, the survival-horizon swap discussed there.

For reference, the two implemented value-head variants (`training/bc/model.py::ValueHead`):

- **`direct`** — Conv2d(C→1, k=3) → ReLU → mask → flatten → Linear; ~9k params (the v1 default).
- **`pyramid`** — the same, with a U-Net-style pyramid module in front; +0.82M params (the DeepNash-spec shape). This is what the sweep ran.

A third shape, **GAP+linear** (~1k params — global-average-pool, then a single linear layer), exists today only in the probe harness (`scripts/value_head_probe.py`).

Hypotheses to test once instrumented — not decisions:

- **First, validate the trunk-corruption hypothesis** (see *Where we are*): a `λ_value=0` run at n=20 (or one that detaches the value gradient from the trunk). If the policy recovers toward the n=5 level, trunk corruption is the likely cause, the value-head fixes below are high-value, and dense history may yet pay off for the policy. If the policy doesn't recover, the cost comes from the input channels themselves (e.g. more channels making optimization harder) and the value work is decoupled from the dense-history question. This gates how much the rest of this section matters.
- **Shrink the head, don't grow it.** The sweep ran `pyramid` and showed the memorization signature even at 24k perspectives; a smaller head (`direct` or GAP+linear) has less capacity to store per-game signatures. (An old probe pointed the same way — GAP+linear was its only non-memorizing variant — but at 81 perspectives it was far too underpowered to be load-bearing; noted for completeness.) Caveat: `direct` itself may have shown some overfitting in earlier runs — worth double-checking old run data before treating it as the safe landing spot. This does sit in tension with PPO, which wants a capable critic — but the critic's capacity can live in the shared trunk: a thin head reading a rich trunk makes a fine critic, while having less ability to corrupt the trunk.
- **Head-specific regularization** (weight decay / dropout on the value path only) so we damp value memorization without touching the policy/trunk path.
- **Modest λ** so a memorizing value-head can't drag the trunk toward fingerprint features — the trunk is what PPO inherits and can't easily undo.
- **PPO-readiness checks** (defer until reward mapping is fixed): scalar reduction `V(s) = E[reward]` under the placement distribution, and a **rank/monotonicity** metric (does `V(s)` order states by eventual outcome?) — closer to what GAE needs than CE.

## Auxiliary-head candidates (exploration menu)

Unifying rationale: denser, per-frame, self-supervised targets should give the trunk far more effective signal than one placement label per trajectory — and several are strategically central to the game, so they may shape the trunk toward features the *policy* wants too.

- **Enemy general location (spatial belief head)** — per-opponent probability heatmap over the board for where their (possibly fogged) general is. This is the strongest candidate: it's literally belief-state inference over hidden state, which is the core skill in fog-of-war play; it's self-supervised (replays have true positions); and it's interpretable / potentially usable at inference, not just trunk-shaping.
- **Threat / intention heads (relational)** — "who attacks me next" / "who do I attack next": per-frame predictions over the player set. Denser than placement; forces the trunk to model inter-player dynamics.
- **Elimination / survival heads (temporal)** — a cluster of related per-frame targets: which player is eliminated next (identity), how many turns until the next elimination (timing), and whether *I* survive the next K turns (survival horizon). All are denser, more-learnable relatives of placement and directly value-relevant (eliminations drive placement).
    - The survival-horizon variant is the closest relative of the value signal, and the most viable value-head alternative we've considered. Swapping it in for placement would be a speculative experiment, though: a critic that estimates expected *outcome* is the standard actor-critic formulation (DeepNash, and return-based value heads across AlphaStar / OpenAI Five / the AlphaZero family), and PPO's advantage estimation assumes `V ≈ expected return` — which survival-horizon isn't. The plan is to carry it as an aux head, where it has no such obligation, and revisit the swap only given a concrete reason.
- **(Maybe) per-frame standing** — rank by land/army at time t: a cheap, dense board-control signal.

Considerations to weigh before committing to any:

- **Target extraction** from replays is real pipeline work; some targets need a precise definition ("attack").
- **Multi-task balancing** — each head adds a loss weight to tune; heads can compete for trunk capacity.
- **Same trunk-corruption risk**, though denser targets should be less prone to the fingerprint-memorization seen with placement.
- **Train-only vs inference-time** — decide per head whether it's pure trunk-shaping or also consumed by the agent (general-location plausibly the latter).
- **Same instrumentation discipline** applies to each — which is the argument for building the panel first.
