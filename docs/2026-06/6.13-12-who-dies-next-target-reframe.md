# Elim head — reframing the target toward "who dies next"

**Date:** 2026-06-13

**Description:** Discussion and takeaways from the session that reframed the next-elimination auxiliary head. Forks off the readout-sweep conclusion ([`6.13-11`](6.13-11-elim-readout-sweep-results.md)) that the per-player time-bin head's imminent signal narrows to data-scale (#1) vs under-carved-trunk (#3). This session moved the question from *"which lever carves the current target"* to *"is the target itself mis-shaped"* — landing on a **who-dies-next** target as the leading redesign. The PoC + experiment plan is a separate, forthcoming write-up.

## The shift in one line

The per-player **time-bin** target asks the head to resolve a partly-irreducible quantity (exact ticks-to-death) and is **agnostic to player identity**; a **who-dies-next** target points only at the part of the elimination signal the current state actually determines, and is identity-relational by construction. The leaning move is to pivot the aux head's target accordingly.

## How the discussion moved

Three moves changed the question from "which experiment settles #1 vs #3" to "the target is the lever":

1. **Reality-checked the pessimism.** Pulled the one 24k run with a tamed value head (`runs-cloud/2026-06-10T09-20-33Z`). Its within-bucket value signal was a single ~2σ sliver — 3-alive at Δ −0.087 below the conditional floor at the value optimum (ep2) — and it *erodes* by the best-policy epoch (ep6: −0.026, and 2-alive crosses back above floor). *(measured: `stratified_val_loss_analysis.py report ... --epochs 2,6`.)* So the prior that "a bigger run will again be underwhelming" is grounded, not idle.
2. **Ruled out the input.** The obs already carries every cue an expert uses to predict eliminations — per-opponent army/land totals, land-rate (snowball), the capture log (`opp_N_captured_by`), contact, BFS-distance-to-known-generals, and fog-memory — and the scoreboard-derived per-opp scalars are populated **even for fully-fogged opponents**. *(verified: `packages/training/training/bc/constants.py:60`.)* So the flat imminent signal is not an input gap; it is downstream (representation / data / target shape / irreducibility).
3. **Surfaced the game-wisdom by asking for it — the turning point of the session.** The reframe started from a direct question to Daniel: *at 10–20 ticks before a player is eliminated in FFA, how much is actually legible on the board?* — aimed at whether the flat bin-1 `[10,20)` signal is a model limitation or an irreducible property of the task. Daniel's answer supplied the predictability taxonomy (next section), and from there the two of us riffed into the most productive lines of the session: that the obs already carries the leaderboard cues the taxonomy relies on (so the input is not the bottleneck); that aggregate precision *under-reads* a head facing a heterogeneously-predictable target; and that the target should therefore point at doom and order, not exact timing — which is what led to who-dies-next. The insight came from domain expertise that a single pointed question pulled into the open — a pattern worth repeating deliberately.

## Elimination predictability — the domain read that reframed the target

*Daniel's read as an experienced FFA player, given in answer to the question above — judgment, not measurement, but the load-bearing input to the pivot.*

Eliminations split by how predictable they are from the current state:

- **Predictable minority:** vision on decisive action — an attacking army near or exceeding the defender's *total* army; a general visibly losing a fight with little army on it; two players fighting where one is clearly ahead (army/land totals, a growing leaderboard gap).
- **Unpredictable majority:** little information; the kill is roughly a coin-flip from here. Likely the largest share.
- **Wide inferable gray zone:** even with no vision, the leaderboard (army + land) and the timestamped capture log support a lot of inference — a snowballing player tends to keep snowballing.

A second axis cuts across this: even when *doom* is legible, *timing* often is not. Time-to-elimination depends on the defender's territory size and the attacker's search for the crown, which is near-random. **So doom and elimination order are frequently predictable; precise tick-latency frequently is not.**

Two consequences drove the pivot:

- The per-player absolute-time-bin target forces the head to fit the noisy latency dimension — capacity spent on what the state does not determine.
- Aggregate per-bin precision averages predictable and unpredictable eliminations together, so it *could* under-read the head — if the head does well on the predictable subset, that performance is diluted to a mediocre pooled number. Untested directly; the predictability-conditioned read (back-pocket) is what would show it.

## What we now believe (takeaways)

- **The predictive cues are present in the input; the gap is downstream.** What's verified is that the channels carry the leaderboard / vision / capture cues an expert uses — not that the trunk uses them or that the data suffices. *(verified: channel list.)*
- **Decomposed, the current head shows three limitations:**
  - bin-0 `[0,10)` carries real signal (~21.7% precision, 8.3× base) at a conservative low-recall operating point, but bin-1 `[10,20)` is **flat** (≈chance precision at ≈0 prediction-frequency); the bin-0/bin-1 split does not close under weighting or readout tweaks. *(measured: [`6.13-8`](6.13-8-elim-weighting-sweep-results.md), [`6.13-11`](6.13-11-elim-readout-sweep-results.md).)*
  - **Class imbalance is not the bin-1 bottleneck.** The imminent bins are ~25× rarer than `never`, but class weighting — the natural imbalance remedy — only slides the operating point along a fixed PR curve (a prior knob) and adds no discrimination. bin-1's flatness is absence of separable signal at this readout/scale, not a thresholding artifact. *(measured: [`6.13-8`](6.13-8-elim-weighting-sweep-results.md).)*
  - The head is **identity-agnostic**: it predicts each player's time-bin independently, so nothing in the loss rewards getting the *relative* order right — yet placement is fundamentally an identity ordering. *(code + reasoning.)*
- **Predictability is heterogeneous, and timing is partly irreducible** — so the time-bin target asks the head to fit noise, and aggregate precision under-reads it. *(domain judgment + reasoning.)*
- **`elim_target_tau = 1.0` on every elim run, never A/B'd** — the softest setting in the value head's working range. But the bin-1 confusion smears to *distant* bins, not adjacent ones, which is the opposite of what over-softening predicts — so τ is probably not the core problem, and a τ sweep would likely not rescue the time-bin head. *(verified: sweep configs; reasoning.)*
- **The meta-pattern still stands:** both the value head and the elim head learn survivor-*counting* but not survivor-*ranking* at sub_5k ([`6.12-4`](6.12-4-wave-a-results-and-synthesis.md)). The pivot is a bet that a better-shaped target reaches the ranking signal where knob-tuning the time-bin form did not — not a claim that scale is irrelevant.

## The candidate: who-dies-next

For each frame, predict **which player is eliminated next** — a single categorical (softmax) across the currently-alive players, including self, with uncertainty carried by the distribution's entropy. It addresses the diagnosed pathologies at the root:

- **Removes the rare-class loss structure.** In the time-bin head, abundant easy bins (`never`/long) dominate the loss, so a lazy head minimizes it by predicting the common bins and eating the small penalty on the rare imminent ones. Who-dies-next makes imminence the *entire* question — exactly one player is next each frame — so there is no easy mass to hide in and every frame's gradient lands on the discriminative comparison. (This reshapes how the gradient is allocated; whether separable signal then exists is the data-scale / irreducibility question below, which the reshaping does not settle.)
- **Forces the cross-player comparison** the within-bucket signal is made of. The softmax normalizes across players, so the gradient rewards "opp-3 is more doomed than opp-5" — the relational/ranking signal the identity-agnostic time-bin head never pressured.
- **Predicts only the predictable part.** It drops the noisy absolute latency entirely and concentrates gradient on the head of the ordering distribution — the part the state determines — while declining to fit the unforecastable mid/tail.
- **Correlates with placement more directly.** Placement is an identity ordering; an identity-relational target is closer to it than per-player independent time-bins.
- **Is legible.** It has an obvious heuristic baseline — *the lowest-army (or lowest army+land) player dies next* — so "does the head beat the obvious rule" is immediately readable, unlike the time-bin head's "beats its soft-marginal floor by 0.01–0.03 nats."
- **Is probe-friendly.** Architecturally close to the existing value probe head (`[B, players]` categorical), so a frozen-trunk first taste is cheap to build.

**Assessment (calibrated):** stated as a strong hypothesis, not a measured result — `who-dies-next` is rated above the current softened-time-bin head because it attacks the *diagnosed* pathologies structurally and is far more legible, which serves the immediate goals (understanding the dynamics, getting a legible green light, de-risking before a 24k run). To be validated by the PoC; re-evaluated if it reads poorly or a design blocker appears.

## The main risk: memorization

The who-dies-next label is **piecewise-constant within an inter-elimination phase** (it does not change until someone actually dies) — the sparse-fixed-target shape that made the value head memorize dense-history fingerprints. Mitigations:

- **Harmless at low λ.** Wave A's magnitude law ([`6.12-4`](6.12-4-wave-a-results-and-synthesis.md)) showed a hard-memorizing head does no policy harm at λ≈0.1; at λ_elim=0.15 memorization would be "doesn't help" rather than "corrupts."
- **Detectable.** The optimal predictor is uncertain early in a phase and sharpens as the kill becomes legible, so prediction confidence *ramping up* as the next death approaches indicates genuine signal, while flat-confident-from-frame-1 indicates fingerprinting.
- **Milder than the value head** (~7 label updates per game vs 1), with uniform label-smoothing available if needed.
- **A density-preserving fallback exists** (the per-player doom-binary, below) that sidesteps the constant-label problem if it bites.

## Why this is not redundant with the value head

The value head predicts the perspective player's *own* final placement — a single 8-way categorical, the integral of the whole future cascade. *(verified: `value_target = placement[k] - 1`, head output `[B, 8]`, `model/heads/value.py`.)* Who-dies-next differs on two independent axes:

- **Egocentric vs allocentric:** "where do I place" vs "what happens to the field next." Even perfectly learned, these are different functions of state; a value head reading a trunk that knows who is about to die is strictly better off.
- **Global integral vs local next step:** final placement requires forecasting a cascade mostly not yet set in motion; the next elimination is temporally near, so its cause is often visible now. Who-dies-next is the *tractable decomposition* of placement — the easy local step the value head's sparse final-outcome target is denied. Similarity in content is the point of an aux head; the difference is density, shape, and horizon.

## Lingering questions

- **Does who-dies-next enrich the trunk for the value head?** Still the 24k transfer question — target shape does not change that this is a separate test. A legible head is a *better thing to scale*, not a substitute for the scale test.
- **Will memorization actually bite at λ=0.15**, or stay harmless? The confidence-ramp diagnostic answers it directly.
- **Horizon handling:** let early-game predictions go near-uniform/honest (lean), or add an explicit "no imminent death" escape class?
- **Ordering depth:** who-dies-next pressures only the top of the order. Whether the deeper ordering (2nd-next, …) is ever needed is open; probably not first.
- **What does dropping full ordering + timing cost** that we cannot yet see? Argued to be the noisy/less-needed parts — a watch-item, not settled.
- **Data scale for the new target:** sub_5k may have limited the time-bin head; the new target deserves a fair first test (see back-pocket: larger manifest).

## Back-pocket ideas

- **Doom-binary** (per-player "dies within K ticks"): keeps per-player density, flips per-frame near death (memorization-safe), drops latency. The fallback if who-dies-next's memorization is real.
- **Ordinal-CDF** (per-player "dies within T" per threshold): keeps density *and* the full ordering, robust to exact-bin noise; the "ordinal done right" reframe of the time-bin head.
- **Predictability-conditioned evaluation** (stratify accuracy by vision/contact, lopsidedness, or time-to-next-death): a methodological tool that applies to any target; could show the *current* head is better than its aggregates say.
- **Frozen-trunk who-dies-next probe** on the current trunk — a cheap first taste of whether the relational features already exist (with the caveat that the current trunk was shaped by a different target, so a negative is ambiguous).
- **Larger experimentation manifest (~10k vs sub_5k):** the meta-pattern blamed sub_5k for the within-bucket wall, so the new target deserves a fairer first test; it also hedges — learning at 10k but not 5k is itself a clean data-scale read.
- **Heavy-pressure-on-sub_5k** (config-only #1-vs-#3 splitter) and the **24k transfer run** (+ a shuffled-target control for attribution): the data-vs-trunk ladder from [`6.13-11`](6.13-11-elim-readout-sweep-results.md) is deferred, not dead — valid for whichever target we run.

## Transferable principle

Point the target at what the state *determines* (doom, order, the next event) and refuse to make it fit what the state does not (precise latency, the deep unforecastable ordering). Predict only the predictable part. The corollary on the metric side: precision over the predictable subset, not aggregate accuracy, is the honest read — and recall is mechanically inflatable by over-prediction ([`6.13-8`](6.13-8-elim-weighting-sweep-results.md)).
