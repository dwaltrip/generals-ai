# Elim-head weighting sweep — weighting is a prior knob, not a discrimination lever

**Date:** 2026-06-13

**Description:** results + synthesis for the elim-head class-weighting sweep set up in [`6.13-7`](6.13-7-elim-head-stage1-read-and-weighting-sweep-handoff.md). Forks off the value-head Wave A conclusion ([`6.12-4`](6.12-4-wave-a-results-and-synthesis.md)); head design in [`6.13-5`](6.13-5-next-elimination-head-design.md), bin edges + weight vectors in [`6.13-6`](6.13-6-elim-head-stage0-corpus-measurements.md).

All cells are single-seed, sub_5k, n20, 5 epochs, seed 0; cross-cell policy comparisons are at matched epoch 3. The only variable across the three weighting cells is `elim_bin_weights = (1/freq)^β` (mean-normalized), with β ∈ {0.5, 0.75, 1.0}. The unweighted **baseline** is the Stage-1 `elim-lam0.15` cell ([`data/training/sweeps/2026-06-13-elim-head-stage1`](../../data/training/sweeps/2026-06-13-elim-head-stage1)); the three weighted cells are in [`data/training/sweeps/2026-06-13-elim-head-weighting`](../../data/training/sweeps/2026-06-13-elim-head-weighting).

## The short version

The primary question was: does upweighting the rare imminent-death bins make the head *learn* them? Read on recall alone, the answer looked like yes — bin-1 recall climbed from ~0.1% (unweighted) to 62% across the sweep. The confusion matrix reverses that: the bin-1 lift is almost entirely **over-prediction** (the head guesses bin 1 on 42% of all players at ~chance precision), not identification. Anchored against the unweighted baseline, a single mechanism ties the per-bin results together: **class weighting slides each bin's operating point along a fixed precision–recall curve set by how much separable signal the trunk holds; it does not bend the curve outward.** Weighting buys a shift in the head's *prior* (predict the rare bin more), not a gain in *discrimination*. The standout imminent signal — `[0,10)` — already discriminates at 8.3× base-rate precision in the unweighted head, and weighting *degrades* it. Do-no-harm holds at every β. Whether the head enriches the shared trunk is unmeasured here and remains the 24k question.

## Settled findings

**1. Class weighting is a prior knob, not a discrimination lever.** It slides each bin's operating point along a fixed precision–recall curve, rather than improving how well the head separates the classes. (Why discrimination is low to begin with — signal absent from the trunk vs. present but unread — is a separate question; see *What flat imminent precision does and doesn't tell us*.)
- As β rises, prediction-frequency on a bin moves dramatically while precision moves little or *downward*: bin 1 pred-freq 0% → 42.5% at flat ~5% precision; bin 0 pred-freq 0.9% → 8.4% while precision *falls* 21.7% → 8.2%. If weighting added discrimination, attending to a bin more would raise its precision — instead precision is flat-or-falling, the signature of moving along a curve rather than bending it.

**2. Overall recall falls as β rises** (29.0% → 23.5 → 19.9 → 16.6) — β rotates the prediction budget from common bins onto rare ones; it does not add accuracy.
- The unweighted baseline has the highest overall top-1; it declines monotonically with β. The rare-bin gains are exactly mirrored by mid-bin collapse — bins 3/4/5 (≈50% of all frames) fall from baseline recall 15/29/36% to 3/6/8% at w1.0.

**3. The bin-1 recall lift is over-prediction, not learning.** `[10,20)` has a flat PR curve — no separable signal there to find.
- At w1.0 the head predicts bin 1 on 42.5% of players against a 2.9% base rate (≈15× over-prediction) at 4.2% precision — barely above the 2.9% chance rate. In the confusion rows, bin-1 predictions are nearly independent of the truth: true-`never` players are called bin-1 27% of the time, true-bin-5 players 42%.
- The unweighted head predicts bin 1 ~never (0.0% pred-freq) — correctly declining to bet on a bin it cannot resolve.

**4. bin 0 `[0,10)` carries by far the strongest imminent signal, it is intrinsic, and weighting degrades it.**
- The unweighted head discriminates bin 0 at 21.7% precision — 8.3× its 2.6% base rate — while predicting it only 0.9% of the time (a high-precision, low-recall operating point).
- Precision is monotone-decreasing across the sweep (21.7 → 12.4 → 12.3 → 8.2): the signal exists with no weighting, and weighting trades it down its curve for recall.

**5. The Stage-1 "imminent starvation" headline conflated two different things.** bin-0's low recall was a recall artifact — a sensible conservative operating point on real signal, not an inability to learn; bin-1's low recall was genuine non-learning (chance-level precision). So weighting was treating a misread symptom for bin 0 and an unfixable one for bin 1.
- The unweighted head's low bin-0 recall (7.5%) co-exists with high precision (21.7%, 8.3× base) — the textbook profile of a conservative threshold on a real-but-rare signal. Contrast bin 1, the genuine can't-learn case: low recall *and* chance-level precision.

**6. `never` (survivor counting) is genuinely learned — but it is the easy signal the value head already gets for free.**
- `never` precision is 48–73%, far above any other bin, on a steeply bowed PR curve (down-weighting it raises precision 48% → 73% as its calls become rarer and purer).
- This is the same counting/ranking split the value head shows: the value head beats its conditional floor only at players_alive ≤ 3 (near-terminal counting) and fails at high alive-counts (ranking). "never" detection is the counting half — which the conditional floor already credits, so it is not the valuable signal.

**7. Do-no-harm holds at every β** — adding and weighting the elim head leaves the BC policy and the value head intact.
- Matched-ep3 val_policy is 0.9649 / 0.9714 / 0.9690 / 0.9628 (baseline / w0.5 / w0.75 / w1.0): w0.5 sits marginally above the anchor band's upper edge, but there is no systematic worsening with β (a real weighting cost would show heavier β → worse policy, which is not what happens), and by ep5 all four converge to ~0.936 / 72.3% top-1. The 0.009 spread is noise.
- The value head also stays healthy across cells (best CE 1.28–1.34, all below the 1.406 floor).

**8. Only the sparse-target value head overfits late; the dense-target policy and elim heads do not.**
- Value CE turns up after ep3 (baseline 1.314 → 1.542; w1.0 1.278 → 1.515), while policy top-1 rises monotonically (68 → 72%) and elim val CE is flat within each cell across epochs (w0.75: 1.26/1.26/1.26/1.24/1.25).
- This is consistent with target density being the discriminator: value = one label per game, policy = one per frame, elim = one per player per frame — the sparsest target memorizes. It is the small-scale advantage the dense aux-head direction is betting on, observed in-vivo on three heads sharing one trunk and one dataset — though with one head per density level, density is confounded with which-head / which-loss, so the ordering is suggestive, not isolated.

## The mechanism: prior vs. likelihood

Minimizing a class-weighted cross-entropy is equivalent to minimizing ordinary cross-entropy on a resampled distribution where each class appears with frequency ∝ (weight × base rate). The Bayes-optimal predictor under that is the posterior with a **reweighted prior** — so to first order weighting changes *where the decision threshold sits* (upweight a class → lower its threshold → predict it more) rather than adding likelihood information. In a finite-capacity network it could in principle reshape features by reallocating gradient toward the upweighted class; the precision evidence below shows it did not do so here — the threshold-shift idealization held.

Each bin therefore has a precision–recall curve whose shape is fixed by how much separable signal the trunk holds for it, and weighting only slides the operating point along that curve. The shape is readable from how precision responds as weighting pushes prediction-frequency up:

- **bin 1 — flat curve → ~no signal.** Precision ≈ base rate and unmoved (even falling) as the head is pushed to predict it 0% → 42%. Pushing buys recall with no precision to trade.
- **bin 0 — gently bowed curve → real but limited signal.** Precision always well above base rate (3–8×), but it declines as the head predicts the bin more. The unweighted head sits at the high-precision corner; weighting drags it toward recall.
- **`never` — steeply bowed curve → strong signal.** Down-weighting moves it toward the purer-but-rarer corner (48% → 73% precision). Plenty of signal; weighting just chooses an operating point on it.

The general takeaway, transferable beyond this head: **weighting reveals and repositions existing signal; only more or better signal — data, features, richer targets, or capacity — bends the curve outward.** If precision does not improve as you sweep the weight, there is nothing there to find and you are only choosing an operating point.

## Methodology lessons

- **Recall after upweighting measures the intervention, not the model.** Upweighting a rare class mechanically inflates its recall via over-prediction; recall cannot distinguish "learned the class" from "guesses it more." Evaluate with a metric the intervention cannot directly move — precision, or the full confusion structure. This is what reversed the bin-1 read.
- **A weighted loss is a different objective; its value is not comparable across weightings.** The `elim` / `elim_soft` CEs drop monotonically with β (1.83 → 1.06) purely because each cell is scored under its own weights — an artifact of the changed objective, not improved fit. Likewise the CE-vs-floor margin (the floor is unweighted) is apples-to-oranges for weighted cells. Cross-cell, only unweighted prediction-side metrics compare: `elim_top1`, per-bin recall *and* precision, prediction entropy, confusion. Within a single cell, across epochs, the weighted CE is a valid convergence/overfit signal (same weights throughout).
- **Decompose "the model is bad at the rare class" into imbalance vs. capacity vs. signal.** They need different fixes (weighting/resampling; bigger model; more data / better features / richer targets). A per-class precision sweep over the weight is a cheap discriminator: imbalance-limited classes respond, signal-limited ones don't. Here bin 1 is imbalance-shaped in *prediction* but signal-empty (flat curve), and bin 0 is signal-limited (highest class weight, least recall gain, precision that only falls when pushed) — so cranking weights did not improve discrimination for either, despite what the recall view suggested.
- **After an intervention "works," re-check it with an orthogonal metric off data you already have.** The confusion matrices came free from the dumps already on disk, and the precision they exposed — the bin-1 over-prediction and the bin-0 intrinsic signal — is what the recall view had hidden.

## Value head

Adding the elim head — at any weighting — did not move the value head's within-bucket behavior at sub_5k: every cell still beats the conditional floor only at low alive-counts (counting) and sits above it when many players remain (ranking). Best value CE across cells spans 1.28–1.34 (all below floor), a 0.06-nat range with no β trend and a single seed — i.e. no detectable elim → value transfer here, which is expected: within-bucket value signal is not visible at sub_5k for either head.

One faint, heavily-caveated thread points the other way: at ep3, w1.0 (heaviest elim pressure) had the smallest gap-above-floor at the hard high-alive buckets (alive=6/7 Δ ≈ +0.04/+0.02, vs a noisy, non-monotone-in-β +0.14 to +0.25 across the lighter cells). That is directionally what the aux bet predicts — elim trunk-pressure helping survivor-ranking — but the confound is strong: w1.0 also has the best *global* value CE at ep3 (all-bucket Δ −0.128 vs baseline −0.092), so the high-alive edge plausibly just tracks that overall checkpoint advantage rather than a high-alive-specific transfer. Single seed, ~9k frames per bucket, and the lighter-cell spread is itself non-monotonic in β. File as a hypothesis to watch at 24k, not a result.

## What flat imminent precision does and doesn't tell us

That weighting can't lift imminent precision is consistent with four mechanisms this sweep cannot separate:

1. **Signal absent at this data scale** — too few learnable imminent examples in 5k games. Lever: more data (24k).
2. **Signal in the trunk, washed out by the readout** — the head global-average-pools, which dilutes spatially-localized evidence (danger at ~3 of ~200 tiles → a ~1.5% perturbation of the mean). Lever: the readout (the pre-registered footprint-pooling A/B). The bin-0-survives / bin-1-washes pattern is what this predicts — globally-apparent doom survives averaging, a local vulnerability does not.
3. **Signal learnable but not yet carved** — weak elim gradient (λ_elim 0.15), 5 epochs, the policy and value gradients dominating trunk-shaping. Lever: more elim pressure / more epochs.
4. **Head too weak to read it** — a single linear conv can't extract a feature that is nonlinear in the trunk channels. Lever: a richer head.

So finding #1's "signal the trunk holds" is better read as "signal *this readout surfaced at this budget*" — one of four live explanations, not the settled one.

The leaning next move is a **readout / head-structure tweak**, the lever for #2 and #4, with the footprint-pooling A/B already pre-registered. The specific design is left open and should be revisited against the aux-head lit ([`6.13-2`](6.13-2-aux-heads-fundamentals.md) / [`6.13-3`](6.13-3-aux-heads-applied-per-entity-hidden-info.md) / [`6.13-4`](6.13-4-aux-head-literature-synthesis.md)) before committing. A frozen-trunk-vs-readout probe is a more surgical alternative — it isolates whether the signal already sits in the current trunk (a clean positive would be decisive; a negative is partly ambiguous, since training with mean-pool may itself have suppressed localizing the signal) — noted for completeness, though the end-to-end tweak more directly tests the design we'd actually ship.

## Open questions and Stage-2 framing

- **Does the elim head enrich the trunk?** This sweep measured *head quality* (prediction precision); it cannot see *trunk transfer*. The two can genuinely conflict: the best head for its own predictions (unweighted) need not be the best head for shaping the trunk. The only thread toward transfer is the faint w1.0 hint above.
- **The Stage-2 β decision is therefore not settled by head quality.** Head-quality says light or unweighted (weighting demonstrably degrades bin-0 discrimination and floods bin 1); trunk-shaping is unmeasured. The honest plan is to **test unweighted vs. a light weight at 24k and let trunk-transfer adjudicate** — not to lock β from sub_5k evidence. The higher-recall-lower-precision operating point that weighting produces means the head emits gradients on more imminent frames, which conceivably shapes the trunk differently even though it hurts the head's own predictions; that is exactly what is unmeasured.
- **The meta-pattern stands:** both the value head and the elim head learn survivor-*counting* at sub_5k but not survivor-*ranking*. This is consistent with a data-scale phenomenon but does not establish one — both heads read the trunk through pooling, so a shared readout bottleneck would produce the same shared wall (see *What flat imminent precision does and doesn't tell us*). Whether a denser, ranking-shaped target changes the calculus — the entire aux bet — remains genuinely unresolved, and is what Stage 2 exists to test. The Stage-2 baseline candidate is the 6.10 24k run (`data/training/runs-cloud/2026-06-10T09-20-33Z`), reusable as the without-elim arm (single-seed, cross-code caveats in [`6.13-7`](6.13-7-elim-head-stage1-read-and-weighting-sweep-handoff.md) §5).
- **Attribution confound to carry forward:** turning the elim head on adds a second trunk-gradient source, so a positive Stage-2 transfer is confounded between "the elimination *signal* enriched the trunk" and "the extra gradient *pressure* perturbed it." A shuffled-target control arm (same weight, no real signal) is the clean attribution, to run only if Stage 2 is positive.
