# Elim-head board-removal + soft-target A/B: synthesis and direction

*Date: 2026-06-21. The interpretation companion to the [6.21-2](6.21-2-elim-board-removal-soft-ab-diagnostics-numbers.md) inventory, which holds the numbers and the per-analysis first-pass reads. This doc draws the cross-analysis conclusions the inventory deliberately deferred. Prior arc: [6.20-2](6.20-2-elim-head-diagnostics-and-direction.md) (the soft-target direction), [6.19-2](6.19-2-elim-head-gate-result.md) (the gate, Tier-0/Tier-1 framing), [6.18-9](6.18-9-elim-head-synthesis-and-direction.md) (floor-vs-signal framing), [6.15-2](6.15-2-army-regression-probe-results.md) (army fidelity). Analysis references like "#5" point at the inventory's numbered analyses.*

## TL;DR

- The soft target is a mild **calibration** improvement — lower elim CE and a smaller policy tax at every epoch — but it brings no top-1 / ranking gain, no stable value movement, and it carries a fixable endgame target bug. Keep it; it is not the lever.
- The elim head's residual is **representation/extraction-limited**, and it shows up across regimes (worst on crowded boards), not as a single-regime quirk. The trunk loses signal it demonstrably has (army fidelity), and the deeper headroom is spatial/threat signal.
- We **believe** much of that spatial signal is already in the obs (especially dense-history) but have not confirmed it — so the working read is *extraction*, not *absence*, held as a hypothesis the next probes are built to test.
- The **value question is parked**: the A/B is within noise (#4), and per the Tier-0 → Tier-1 logic a not-yet-sound head isn't expected to move value anyway — so "value-neutral" is the expected result, not a measurement gap. It reopens only after the representation lever moves; don't reseed it now.
- **Reframe:** the near-term payoff of this arc is the elim task as a **diagnostic instrument for the trunk's representations**, not a deployed aux head. The new path is a family of "mid-level abstraction" probes — what the trunk has learned between raw army and the final outcome.

## 1. The soft-target verdict

The sweep's pre-registered question was narrow: does the soft target fix the elim head? The answer is yes-on-calibration, no-on-everything-else.

**What it did.** Soft sits lower on the elim CE curve and carries a smaller train/val gap at every epoch (#1), and its policy tax is smaller than hard's at every epoch (#4) — the one stable signal in the A/B. But the CE improvement is a **level-shift, not a slope change**: the late-epoch overfit onset and its rate are unchanged (#1). And it is **calibration, not ranking**: top-1 never improves (#3), confidence drops uniformly, and the CE win is concentrated in mid-crowding where the head is moderately-confident-but-often-wrong (#9). Mechanically this is expected — the hard label and the soft target share a mode, so soft reshapes off-peak mass, not the argmax.

**What it didn't do.** No top-1 gain (#3). No change to what the trunk encodes about army — the soft trunk reaches the same army-R² as hard, just earlier (#6). No stable value movement — the hard/soft value ordering flips across epochs, and on the trained value objective the winner alternates every epoch (#4).

**The endgame bug.** The soft target has a soft-only construction error at game end: the eventual winner (never removed, stamped at game-end by the sentinel) is included as a candidate and draws ~45% of the soft mass on the ~12% of frames at present=2 — about 6.5% of all soft-target mass points at a player who never dies (6.21-2 #11 and methodological note). It is objectively wrong, not a close-fight feature: the target is built with hindsight, so who-dies-next has a definite answer (the loser); the winner's mass is the sentinel fiction, and the genuine "this 1v1 is a coin-flip" uncertainty belongs in the *model's* prediction, learned from many one-hot frames, not in the target. The fix is `winner removal_dt → +inf`, which collapses present=2 back to one-hot (soft == hard there) and realigns the implementation with the design's "sparse late → near one-hot" intent. Because it is soft-only (the hard target is correct everywhere), it mildly *handicaps* soft in this A/B — soft's true numbers are a touch better than measured.

**What we cleared up.** The trunk did not regress relative to the prior 11k checkpoint — the apparent army-R² drop was measurement-set + epoch choice, not the model (#6, #7). And the elim head's memorization is not anomalous: the value head carries the same ~0.7 train/val gap (#2), so the large gap is normal for these outcome-prediction heads at the 5k scale, not an elim-head pathology.

**Disposition.** Keep the soft target — it is mildly net-positive, more so once the endgame bug is fixed — but it is not the lever that moves the head. The lever is representation (§2).

## 2. The central finding: the head is representation/extraction-limited

The elim head's core shortfall is that it cannot identify the next victim, and the evidence points at the head's ability to extract discriminating signal rather than at the target or at overfitting.

**The argument, weighted honestly.** Three alternative causes can be set against signal-limitation, and they survive the stress-test unevenly:

- *Not target-limited.* The soft objective's achievable CE floor sits far below the head's actual CE in every regime (#12) — a perfect predictor of this target would score well; the head is nowhere near it. This is the cleanest of the three.
- *Overfitting is real but reads downstream, not causal.* The train/val gap is large (~0.6–0.7), so memorization is happening. But soft reduced it without lifting val ranking (#1, #3) — if memorization set the val ceiling, reducing it should have helped. So the memorization looks like a *symptom* of low generalizable signal, not the cause of the cap. Held mild: a stronger regularizer could still claw back a little.
- *Crowded-board collapse is partly irreducible.* The head's confidence decays toward uniform as candidates multiply (#9) — the fingerprint of missing discriminating signal — but the early game is also genuinely high-entropy (many players at even strength), so some of that uniformity is what an oracle would also do. Confounded.

So the load-bearing case for "there is extractable signal the head doesn't use" is not the A/B/C elimination above (which is supporting, and partly confounded) but three sturdier legs: the **army-extraction loss** (#5 — the trunk resolves the weakest player ~44% vs an 83% exact-army ceiling, a concrete loss immune to the chaos confound), the **perfect-army gap** (the lowest-army rule — exact army level — tops out ~47% on who-dies-next, so much of the target depends on non-army signal), and **FFA domain knowledge** that the spatial clues exist and predict. The conclusion holds even where A/B/C don't.

**Extraction, not absence — a working hypothesis.** We believe much of the predictive spatial/tactical signal is already in the obs, especially the dense-history channels, but we have not analyzed this in depth or confirmed it — it is non-trivial to do. On that working assumption the bottleneck is *extraction*: the conv trunk with a mean-pool elim readout has weak inductive bias for per-player spatial structure (mean-pooling washes it out). But "the obs lacks some of it" is not ruled out — and finding those gaps is part of what the next probes are for (§4).

**The texture of the missing signal (Daniel's FFA read).** The predictive spatial clues span a range. "Large" ones — a sizeable army moving toward a player — a human reads instantly. Subtler, contextual reads are where the strongest players separate themselves, and where even many human players struggle. Crucially, even the *large* clues are non-trivial for this network: "moving toward player X" is a relational abstraction over a target whose tiles are scattered and non-compact, especially as fighting progresses. And the signal is most useful **combined** — spatial together with leaderboard (army/land level and their rate/history) and events (who just killed whom) — rarely in isolation.

**An upstream assumption this exposes.** We have verified the dense-history channels *encode* what we designed (technical tests), but never checked whether the trunk *extracts* the intended meaning from them. That gap is exactly what §4's probe targets.

## 3. Reframe: a lens on the trunk, and why value is parked

**The value question is parked, and reseeding it now is low-value — for two reasons.** Empirically, the value A/B is within noise (the hard/soft ordering flips across epochs, #4), so more seeds would only tighten a null. Mechanistically, the Tier-0 → Tier-1 ordering ([6.19-2](6.19-2-elim-head-gate-result.md), held there as a hypothesis) predicts that a head which isn't *sound* — beats the heuristics, healthy curve — can at best no-op for value: an unsound head feeds the shared trunk idiosyncratic gradients, not structure value can use. The current head fails Tier-0 (below the rule, overfits), so "value-neutral" is the *expected* reading. The question reopens once the representation lever moves Tier-0 — open, but parked.

**The arc as a diagnostic instrument.** Set against several sessions of work, the elim head is not earning its place as a *deployed* aux head — it carries a policy tax, sits below the lowest-army rule, and is value-neutral. But the elim *task* has become a productive lens on the trunk: the army-fidelity probe, and now the mid-level-abstraction probes, measure what the trunk encodes, which bears directly on the value head through the shared trunk. The near-term deliverable of this work is **representational understanding — and the obs/architecture changes it motivates — that help the whole model**, not a shippable elim head. That is the honest framing of where the arc stands and what it is for.

## 4. Direction

**Centerpiece — the mid-level-abstraction probe family.** Define a movement/threat target ("is a sizeable army approaching player X") and regress it off the frozen trunk, with a raw-obs control as the army probe had. The control is what makes it a *gap-finder*, not just a readout test:

- High trunk R² → the signal is in the trunk; the elim head's failure is a **readout** problem (lever: a stronger per-player readout — e.g. attention over players — instead of mean-pool).
- Low trunk R², high raw-obs R² → the signal is in the obs but the trunk doesn't surface it: an **extraction** problem (lever: architecture / capacity / auxiliary spatial losses).
- Low on both → the signal isn't readily in the obs: an **obs gap** to fill (lever: explicit derived channels).

Defining the target is non-trivial, and the variations that surface while building it (approaching-whom, with-what-margin, across-which-border) are themselves informative. This opens a new line — probing what the trunk has learned at the *middle* level, between raw army (#5) and the final value outcome — that is more general than the elim head and applies to the value head directly.

**Concrete fixes (cheap, well-grounded).**

- `winner removal_dt → +inf` in the soft kernel — a correctness fix that removes the present=2 penalty and de-handicaps soft's A/B (§1).
- A shared `is_valid_elim_frame` helper that training and analyses both call, so what we measure provably matches what we train on (6.21-2 methodological TODO).

**A cheaper intermediate.** Land, and army/land rate and history, are unswept leaderboard signals ([6.20-2](6.20-2-elim-head-diagnostics-and-direction.md)). 6.20-1's rate-construction nulls were standalone, hard-target only; the "useful combined" point gives them new life as a complement, and they sit between army-level (#5) and the spatial probes — a cheaper test of how much of the headroom is still leaderboard vs genuinely spatial.

**Explicitly not next.**

- A τ sweep. The model-free diagnostics say τ is a calibration knob: raising it lifts the CE floor everywhere (#12), helps mid-crowding marginally, worsens the endgame, and cannot touch the signal-limited ranking. The only reason to test it is the value angle, which is parked.
- A value reseed (parked, per §3).

## 5. Open items and caveats

- **Single-seed throughout.** The parked-value logic covers the most important case, but the army-R² trajectory difference (#6) and the soft-improvement magnitude are also single-seed — treat them as directional.
- **death→removal is unevaluated.** We adopted it this session for value-relevance, it introduced the endgame bug, and it shrank the valid-frame set (present=2 is now ~40% valid — the un-removed endgames are masked). It was never A/B'd against the death target — an open validation, not a settled win.
- **Unexplained:** the soft trunk's army-R² is flat across epochs while the hard trunk ramps to meet it (#6). Single-seed, mechanism unknown.
- **Pointers.** Numbers and reproduction: [6.21-2](6.21-2-elim-board-removal-soft-ab-diagnostics-numbers.md). The dense-history-N quality sweep remains pending and bears on §2's "is the movement signal conveyed well" question.
