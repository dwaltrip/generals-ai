# Agent B — Applied lit review: per-player outcome prediction + per-entity heads in hidden-information games

Charter: inform the design of a "next-elimination" auxiliary head for our generals.io BC/PPO net. The head reads the shared U-Net spatial trunk `[B, C, H, W]` and predicts, per frame, which currently-alive player gets eliminated next — a per-player categorical (8 players, masked to alive). Leading head-shape candidate: `Conv2d(C -> 8)` then masked spatial pool.

Verification convention: each finding tagged "verified against source: <url>" (content actually fetched/read) or "model knowledge, UNVERIFIED". Where a primary arXiv `/pdf/` would not render (binary), I used the arXiv HTML mirror (`ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org`) or a reputable secondary source and tagged accordingly.

## Thread 1 — Outcome / event prediction over other players (incl. opponent modeling, ToM)

### 1a. ToMnet — Machine Theory of Mind (Rabinowitz et al., 2018)
Verified against source: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1802.07740 (full HTML); abstract https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07740

What it did: A meta-learning observer network that models *other* agents. Three modules:
- **Character net** parses past trajectories of an agent into a character embedding `e_char` (per-past-episode embeddings are summed, `e_char,i = Σ_j e_char,ij`, so it aggregates over a variable number of observed episodes).
- **Mental-state net** parses the current episode so far into `e_mental`.
- **Prediction net** takes `(e_char, e_mental, current_state)` through a shared torso into multiple output heads:
  1. next-step action probabilities (softmax over the 5 gridworld actions),
  2. per-object "will this agent consume object X before episode end" (binary per object) — a *future-outcome* prediction,
  3. successor representation (expected discounted state occupancy) at several γ,
  4. (optional) a per-object belief-state distribution.

Hidden-info handling: trained with no explicit belief supervision, ToMnet still learned that an agent's policy is sensitive to changes inside its field of view and invariant to changes outside it — i.e., it predicted behavior consistent with the agent holding a *false belief* (Sally-Anne).

Relevance to our next-elimination head: ToMnet's "will object X be consumed before episode end" head is the closest structural analog to ours — a per-entity binary/categorical prediction of a *future discrete event*, read off a shared torso, trained purely by observing trajectories. Our "which alive player dies next" is the same shape (swap objects→players, consumption→elimination). It is precedent that such future-event heads (a) train fine from pure observation/BC data and (b) usefully shape the shared representation. The summed per-episode character embedding is less relevant to us (we predict within one game), but the multi-head-off-shared-torso pattern is exactly our setup.

### 1b. Suphx — Global Reward Prediction (Li et al., 2020, mahjong)
Verified against source: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.13590 (full HTML)

What it did: Mahjong is a 4-player hidden-info game scored by final placement. Round scores are noisy and the final game reward is shared across rounds, so neither is a good per-round RL signal. Suphx trains a **global reward predictor** Φ — a two-layer GRU + two FC layers — that takes per-round features (this round's score, accumulated score, dealer position, repeat-dealer/Riichi-bet counters) and predicts the **final game reward**. During RL, the credit for round k is `Φ(x^k) − Φ(x^{k-1})`. The predictor is pre-trained on human pro logs by MSE.
- Separately, **oracle guiding**: an oracle agent sees all hidden tiles/wall; perfect features are dropped out gradually so a normal (imperfect-info) agent learns from the oracle's guidance.
- Policy/value nets are deep CNNs over 34×1 tile channels with 100+ look-ahead features; discard head = 34 outputs, others binary.

Relevance: This is the strongest precedent for **placement-outcome as the learning target in a hidden-info, many-player game**. Our rationale ("elimination order ≈ final placement order, and it's denser than the sparse placement label") is essentially Suphx's: turn a sparse end-of-game placement signal into a dense per-step prediction. Note their predictor is a *separate, pre-trained recurrent* net consuming scalar game-state features, not a head off a spatial trunk — so it argues *for the value of predicting placement*, not for our specific head shape. Oracle guiding is a separately interesting idea for Phase 2 if we ever want a fog-piercing teacher.

### 1c. OpenAI Five — auxiliary prediction heads incl. net-worth-rank (Dota 2, 2019)
PRIMARY-VERIFIED against source: "Dota 2 with Large Scale Deep Reinforcement Learning," cdn.openai.com/dota-2.pdf (arXiv 1912.06680). Verbatim text below was confirmed against the primary PDF (cross-checked in the adversarial audit; the PDF renders as binary through the fetch tool, so the verbatim quote is reproduced from the audited primary text rather than re-extracted here).

What it did: Small networks of fully-connected layers transform the shared LSTM output into auxiliary heads trained in a value-function-like fashion (when a segment contains the ground-truth label it is used for all timesteps; otherwise the model's own end-of-segment prediction is bootstrapped as the label). The **net-worth-rank** head predicts, verbatim, **"which rank among the team (1-5) in terms of total resources collected will this hero be at the end of the game?"** — and this prediction is **used by scripted item-buying logic to decide which agents buy items shared by the team such as wards**. On gradients: **win probability "passes gradients to the main LSTM and rest of the agent with a very small weight," while other auxiliary predictions use stop_gradient**. The model can also be trained to predict last-hits, tower counts, and similar quantities.

Relevance: **Net-worth-rank is a per-entity *ordering/rank* prediction trained as a value-function-style head — the single closest precedent to "predict elimination order."** It shows (a) per-entity end-of-game *rank* prediction is a real, deployed auxiliary head in a top game AI, and (b) the standard recipe is a small head off the shared embedding trained with value-style bootstrap targets. Note also the win-prob-vs-stop_gradient split: it's a concrete precedent for deciding whether our elimination head should pass gradient into the shared trunk (to shape it, like UNREAL/Agent-Modeling aux) or be stop-gradient'd off it (a pure read-out that doesn't perturb the policy features) — OpenAI Five lets win-prob shape the trunk with a small weight but stop-gradients the rest. Differences from ours: theirs sits on an LSTM vector, ours on a spatial map; theirs ranks teammates, ours ranks all alive players by death order.

### 1d. Cicero / no-press Diplomacy policy+value nets (Meta / FAIR, 2019–2022)
Verified against source: per-power value/SoS + GNN encoder described in NeurIPS "No-Press Diplomacy from Scratch" surfaced text https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/95f2b84de5660ddf45c8a34933a2e66f-Paper.pdf and Paquette et al. DipNet; Cicero overview https://ai.meta.com/research/cicero/ . GNN/per-power detail: verified-via-secondary-excerpt (search synthesis of the from-scratch paper); exact layer counts UNVERIFIED at primary.

What it did: 7-player hidden-intent game. DipNet (Paquette 2019) and successors imitate ~150k human games with a **graph neural network over the board** (provinces/locations as nodes), an ~8-layer GNN trunk with a current-state and previous-state encoder. The **value head outputs one value per power** (per-player) — an MLP→softmax producing predicted **sum-of-squares final scores for each of the 7 players**. The policy produces **per-power action distributions**. Cicero (2022) adds a dialogue model on top, and at each turn explicitly **predicts the likely actions of every other player** (the anchor/imitation policy) and feeds those beliefs into a planning algorithm.

Relevance: Two precedents at once. (i) A **per-player value head** (one output per player, here via softmax over powers) is standard in a many-player game — direct support for "one prediction per player" outputs. (ii) **Explicitly predicting other players' actions/outcomes as a first-class network output** is core to a state-of-the-art multi-player agent. The board-graph GNN is an alternative entity encoder (Thread 2) if our grid ever wants relational structure, but for an 8×~20 generals board a conv trunk is simpler.

### 1e. Agent Modeling as Auxiliary Task (Hernandez-Leal et al., 2019)
Verified against source: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.09597 (full HTML)

What it did: Extends A3C with an auxiliary head that predicts **other agents' actions**, trained by cross-entropy against the observed one-hot opponent/teammate action. Two variants:
- **AMS-A3C** (parameter sharing): the opponent-action prediction head shares the conv + early FC layers with the actor-critic; the shared representation helps both.
- **AMF-A3C** (policy features): learns latent opponent features `h_opp` that condition actor/critic via element-wise multiplication.
Evaluated on a cooperative grid-world and a 2-player 8×8 Pommerman; both beat vanilla A3C in stability and reward.

Relevance: Direct precedent that **predicting other agents' behavior as an auxiliary head off a *shared* conv representation improves the main agent** — the exact mechanism we hypothesize for our next-elimination head enriching the shared trunk. AMS (parameter sharing) is the closer match to our plan; AMF shows an alternative if we ever want the elimination belief to *condition* the policy rather than just shape features.

### 1f. Terminal Prediction as Auxiliary Task (Kartal et al., 2019)
Verified against source: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.10827 (full HTML)

What it did: Self-supervised auxiliary head predicting **temporal closeness to a terminal state** — a scalar in [0,1], with linearly interpolated targets (0 at episode start → 1 at end). The head fully shares A3C parameters except its own final layer; loss is MSE, combined as `L = L_A3C + 0.5·L_TP`. On Pommerman, A3C-TP converged faster and found better policies than vanilla A3C.

Relevance: Precedent that a **dense per-frame "how close to a terminal event" signal is a cheap, effective auxiliary that needs no extra labels** (targets derived from episode timing). Two takeaways for us: (1) it validates dense per-frame outcome shaping; (2) a *time-to-elimination* regression (per alive player, fraction of game until they die) is a natural sibling/alternative to our categorical "who dies next" — same data, and Terminal Prediction is the template for the regression form. **HYPOTHESIS (ours, not from the paper):** the regression form may give a denser/smoother gradient than the categorical form. Plausible but unproven — the paper compares Terminal-Prediction vs. no-aux, not regression-vs-categorical; worth an A/B, not assertable as a finding.

Related landmarks (cross-references — covered in depth by the companion fundamentals report, not deep-dived here):
- **UNREAL** (Jaderberg et al., ICLR 2017; verified-via-secondary, openreview.net/pdf?id=SJ6yPD5xg + summaries): the canonical "auxiliary tasks on a CNN-LSTM A3C agent" result — pixel control, **reward prediction**, and value-function replay sharing the base representation. The foundational precedent for our whole premise (aux heads off a shared trunk help).
- **FTW / Capture-the-Flag** (Jaderberg et al., Science 2019; verified-via-secondary, science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aau6249): human-level Quake III CTF from pixels+game-points via population-based training — a multi-agent, partial-observation landmark.
- Lample & Chaplot 2017 (model knowledge, UNVERIFIED at primary): predicting presence of enemies as an aux task in an FPS. Reinforces "predict salient others/events as aux."

## Thread 2 — Per-entity head architectures (spatial encoder → one output per player/entity)

The design question: from a board embedding `[C, H, W]`, produce one number (or distribution) per player. The literature offers a spectrum; mapped to our candidate.

### 2a. Per-channel spatial pooling (our leading candidate: `Conv2d(C→8)` + masked pool)
Closest literature anchor — **relational deep RL output aggregation** (Zambaldi et al., 2018).
Verified against source: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1806.01830 (full HTML)

What it did: A CNN produces k feature maps n×n; x,y coords are concatenated to each pixel vector; the n² pixels are treated as a set of entities (an n²×k matrix). Multi-head dot-product self-attention (MHDPA) reasons over entities; **the relational module's output is aggregated by feature-wise max-pooling across space (n×n×k → k-vector)**.

Relevance (literature-backed): The "reduce a spatial tensor to a per-feature vector by pooling over space" operation is exactly our `Conv2d(C→8)` → spatial-pool plan, where each of the 8 output channels is a per-player score map and we pool (sum/mean/max over the 8×~20 board, masked to that player's footprint or to the whole board) to one logit per player. That spatial→per-feature pooling reduction is the part Zambaldi directly supports. Note the **max-pool vs sum/mean** semantic difference is a generic property of pooling, also independent of any specific finding here: max ≈ "is there any strong evidence somewhere that player p dies next," sum/mean ≈ "aggregate evidence weighted by footprint."

Two design notes are **HYPOTHESES (ours, not transferred from Zambaldi)** — flagged because the reviewer caught them stated too firmly:
- **Coordinate channels.** Zambaldi appends x,y coords so that *permutation-invariant self-attention* over the entity set can recover position; a plain `Conv2d` already encodes position implicitly via its receptive field, so the x,y-coords trick does *not* transfer to our conv-pool head as a literature-backed move. *Hypothesis:* explicit coordinate channels may still help if absolute board geometry matters for elimination (e.g. corner/edge generals) — testable, not established.
- **Pooling choice for localized evidence.** *Hypothesis (our own reasoning, not a finding):* elimination evidence often localizes near a player's general, so masked-mean over the player's tiles or a learned soft-attention pool may beat plain global pooling. This is the motivation for trying those pools; it is a guess to A/B, not a result from the literature.

### 2b. Entity/transformer encoder + pointer network — AlphaStar (Vinyals et al., 2019)
Verified against source (architecture details from JAIR mini-AlphaStar paper https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/article/download/12839/26707/27852 and reproduction notes, both restating the original spec): verified-via-secondary; original Nature paper not fetched, tagged accordingly.

What it did: Units are encoded as a *set* by an **entity encoder = a Transformer (3 self-attention layers, 2 heads, dim 128; per-layer projection to 256 + 2-layer MLP)** over up to 512 entities, producing per-entity embeddings plus a 1D reduced embedding. A **pointer network** then assigns a probability to each candidate unit for selection — handling a *variable* number of units.

Relevance: This is the canonical "one output per entity, variable entity count" machine. If we ever represent players (or their generals/armies) as explicit *entities* rather than reading them off board channels, an attention/pointer readout gives a per-player score that naturally handles the alive-mask (eliminated players simply aren't in the entity set, or are masked in the pointer softmax). For 8 fixed player slots, the simpler fixed `Conv→8`+pool is lighter; the pointer pattern earns its complexity only if entity counts vary or per-unit selection is needed. Our masked softmax over alive players is the small-scale analog of the pointer's masked entity softmax.

### 2c. Relational / self-attention over board cells — Zambaldi (as 2a) and Diplomacy GNN (1d)
Verified: Zambaldi as above; Diplomacy GNN per 1d.

Relevance: If elimination depends on *relations* between players (who is adjacent to/threatening whom), a relational module (self-attention over board-cell entities, or a GNN over a player-adjacency graph) could feed the per-player head with relation-aware features. Likely overkill for v1, but the natural upgrade path if a plain conv head underperforms on contested multi-player situations.

### 2d. Spatial-belief / heatmap heads
Model knowledge, UNVERIFIED (general pattern; concrete instance below is verified): producing a per-cell probability map (a heatmap over the board) is standard for spatial outputs — our policy head itself is one (per-cell move logits). DeepNash's policy heads (1g/3a) are spatial distributions over the board. A "where will player p be eliminated" heatmap, then pooled to a scalar per player, is a hybrid of 2a and this. Useful if we want an interpretable spatial elimination-risk map as a by-product.

### 2e. Multi-head value (per-subtask), not per-entity — JueWu/Honor of Kings (Ye et al., 2020)
Verified against source: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.12692 (full HTML, "Towards Playing Full MOBA Games with Deep RL")

What it did: **Multi-head value estimation** decomposes the value into **five heads by reward category** — Farming (gold/xp/mana), KDA (kills/deaths/assists), Damage, Pushing (turrets), Win/Lose — and recombines as `V̂ = Σ_k w_k V̂_k`. Overall net: spatial/image encoder (convolutions) + vector encoder (MLP) → concatenated → **LSTM (1024 units)** for partial observability → action heads, with **target attention** (LSTM output as query, stack of game-unit encodings as keys) to select the target unit.

Relevance: Two distinct lessons. (i) The **"target attention"** mechanism (query from the trunk, keys = per-unit encodings) is another per-entity readout pattern — a lighter cousin of AlphaStar's pointer net — directly usable if we treat the 8 players' general-tiles as keys and read a per-player score. (ii) Multi-head value is *not* per-entity (it decomposes by reward type), but it's relevant context: it argues for **decomposing value into semantically meaningful sub-predictions**, which is the same philosophy as adding our elimination aux head. Don't conflate "multi-head value" (their decomposition) with "per-player head" (our goal) — easy to misremember.

Caution on attribution: the "JueWu" work spans multiple papers — "Towards Playing Full MOBA Games…" (arXiv 2011.12692, the RL system with multi-head value) and "Supervised Learning Achieves Human-Level Performance…" (the SL system JueWu-SL). Multi-head value belongs to the **RL** paper (2011.12692), verified above.

## Thread 3 — Hidden / imperfect information & fog-of-war architectures

### 3a. DeepNash / Stratego (Perolat et al., 2022) — the closest architectural twin
Verified against source: arXiv abstract https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15378 ; architecture details corroborated across MarkTechPost https://www.marktechpost.com/2022/07/09/... and deeplearning.ai https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/deepnash-the-rl-system-that-plays-stratego-like-a-master/ (Science primary https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.add4679 returned 403, not fetched). Tag: architecture verified-via-secondary (multiple consistent sources); exact channel/block counts UNVERIFIED.

What it did: Stratego is a 2-player imperfect-information game (opponent piece identities hidden). Net = **a U-Net torso with residual blocks and skip connections, feeding four heads** (each head a smaller replica of the torso + final layers): one **value head (scalar)** and **three policy heads** (deployment, piece-selection, action), each a spatial distribution over the board. The **observation is a spatial tensor of stacked frames (~82)**: own pieces, *public* information (the still-possible identities of each piece given history), game phase, and the **last 40 moves**. Trained by R-NaD (model-free, converges toward ε-Nash).

Relevance — high. **This is essentially our architecture** (shared U-Net trunk → value head + spatial policy heads) applied to a hidden-information game, which is strong validation of our overall design. Most directly transferable idea: **represent hidden state in the input as "public belief" channels** — instead of trying to *see* hidden pieces, DeepNash encodes *the distribution of what each piece could still be* given observed history. For generals' fog of war, the analog is encoding, per fogged tile, the set/distribution of what could be there (last-seen ownership, time-since-seen, inferred army bounds) as input channels — and stacking recent-move/last-seen frames. Our elimination head would then read evidence partly from these belief channels. Also: DeepNash adding *more heads to the same U-Net torso* (4 heads) is exactly the move we're making (add an aux head); precedent that the U-Net torso comfortably feeds several heads.

### 3b. Generative fog-of-war defogging — predicting the hidden state *as output*
This is the complement to DeepNash's belief-as-input-channels (3a): rather than feeding learned belief channels *in*, these systems make the hidden state itself a *prediction target* — a generative/regression head that reconstructs the fogged enemy units (counts and locations) the model cannot directly see.

- **DefogGAN** (Jeong, Choi, Kim, Gwon, AAAI 2020; arXiv 2003.01927).
  Verified against source: abstract https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.01927 (read); AAAI proceedings https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/5853 ; code https://github.com/TeamSAIDA/DefogGAN .
  What it did: takes a *partially observed* StarCraft: Brood War state and **generates a "defogged" full game state as predictive information** — i.e. infers the enemy buildings/combat units hidden by fog. Architecture is a **conditional GAN variant with a pyramidal reconstruction loss** (optimizes at multiple feature-resolution scales), combining GAN adversarial loss with a reconstruction loss that emphasizes regressing unit positions and quantities. Validated on a large corpus of professional replays; reported to predict enemy buildings/units about as accurately as professional players.

- **StarCraft Defogger / forward modeling** (Synnaeve et al., NeurIPS 2018).
  Verified against source (PDF read): https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2018/file/287e041302f34b11ddfb57afc8048cd8-Paper.pdf
  What it did: frames defogging as **state estimation + future-state prediction from partial observations**, predicting hidden opponent unit counts and positions with **convolutional encoder-decoder networks** (with recurrent components to exploit temporal correlations), trained on human Brood War games. The predictions were plugged into a rule-based bot's enemy-unit prediction and improved win rates against strong community bots — so it functioned as a deployed predictive module, not merely representation-shaping.
  (Related, surfaced not deep-read, verified-via-secondary: "Self-supervised Learning for Predicting Invisible Enemy Information in StarCraft II," Springer 2022, https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-16072-1_13 — same family, SC2.)

Relevance: This is a **distinct, complementary auxiliary-head family** from our next-elimination head. Next-elimination predicts an *outcome over players*; defogging predicts the *hidden spatial state*. For generals' fog of war, a defogging head off the shared U-Net trunk would predict, per fogged cell, the likely enemy ownership / army / general-location — a spatial heatmap output (ties into 2d). It could (a) enrich the trunk with the structure needed to reason about opponents we can't see, and (b) feed the elimination head useful "where are the threatened generals" features. HYPOTHESIS (ours): a defogging aux head would help the elimination head specifically — plausible given both need opponent-state understanding, but unproven; the cited works show defogging *is learnable and useful for a bot*, not that it boosts an elimination/placement predictor. Worth listing as a candidate second aux head, A/B-tested independently. Note the GAN machinery (DefogGAN) is likely overkill for v1; a plain conv encoder-decoder regression head (Synnaeve-style) is the lighter starting point.

### 3c. Suphx oracle guiding (mahjong) — see 1b
Verified: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2003.13590
Hidden-info-specific takeaway: train an oracle that *sees* hidden state, then anneal its privileged features to zero so the deployable imperfect-info net inherits the oracle's shaping. A Phase-2 option if our fog net struggles; our BC setting already has full replay info available at train time, so an oracle-style privileged teacher head is feasible.

### 3d. ToMnet false-belief modeling — see 1a
Verified: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1802.07740
Hidden-info takeaway: a network can learn to predict others' behavior *consistent with their limited observations* without explicit belief labels — encouraging for predicting opponent eliminations under fog, where our net also only sees public/observed info about opponents.

### 3e. Cicero / Diplomacy — hidden intent — see 1d
Verified-via-secondary as in 1d.
Hidden-info takeaway: explicit per-player action/outcome prediction is the backbone of a SOTA hidden-information multi-player agent; supports making other-player prediction a first-class head, not an afterthought.

### 3f. Poker (Pluribus) & Hanabi — surfaced, not deeply fetched
Model knowledge, UNVERIFIED (not fetched this session): Pluribus (6-player no-limit Hold'em, Brown & Sandholm 2019) uses search + abstraction rather than a spatial per-entity head, so it's weak precedent for our head shape; its relevance is as a multi-player hidden-info landmark. Hanabi (the "Hanabi Challenge," Bard et al. 2019) centers on *belief/ToM over hidden hands* and is the canonical cooperative hidden-info benchmark; relevant to belief representation but not to a grid/per-entity head. Flagged for follow-up if belief-representation detail is needed; I did not verify their architectures against primary sources here.

## Synthesis → our next-elimination head

- **Precedent that the head is worth building**: ToMnet (per-entity future-event head), OpenAI Five net-worth-rank (per-entity end-of-game *rank* head trained value-style), Suphx (placement-outcome as dense signal in hidden-info many-player game), Agent-Modeling-as-Aux (predicting others improves the shared trunk), Terminal-Prediction (dense per-frame outcome shaping with free targets). All primary-verified (OpenAI Five net-worth-rank upgraded to primary-verified, verbatim quote in 1c).
- **Precedent for "one output per player"**: Diplomacy per-power value (softmax over players) and per-power policy; OpenAI Five per-hero rank. Supports a fixed 8-slot per-player output with a masked softmax over alive players.
- **Head-shape decision**: `Conv2d(C→8)` + masked spatial pool is well-grounded — the spatial→per-feature pooling reduction is the relational-RL (Zambaldi) pattern with one channel per player, and that reduction is the literature-backed part. Two refinements I floated are **hypotheses, not transfers** (see 2a): coordinate channels (Zambaldi adds them for *attention* permutation-invariance, not plain convs) and masked-mean/attention pooling (motivated by our own guess that elimination evidence localizes near generals). The heavier alternatives — AlphaStar pointer net, JueWu target-attention with general-tiles as keys — are the upgrade path if the plain conv-pool head underperforms on contested multi-player spots.
- **Hidden-info / fog**: DeepNash is the architectural twin — a shared U-Net trunk feeding value + spatial heads on an imperfect-info game, with hidden state encoded as *public-belief channels* + stacked recent-move frames. Strongest single template to borrow, both for adding our aux head to the U-Net and for how to feed it fog-aware belief inputs.
- **Complementary second aux head (distinct family)**: generative defogging (DefogGAN, Synnaeve defogger — both verified) predicts the *hidden state itself* (per-cell enemy ownership/army/general) as a spatial output, vs. our outcome-over-players head. A conv encoder-decoder defogging head is a candidate companion aux; HYPOTHESIS that it specifically aids the elimination head (unproven — A/B independently).
- **Sibling formulation worth A/B-ing**: time-to-elimination regression per alive player (Terminal-Prediction-style) vs. categorical who-dies-next. Same labels; HYPOTHESIS that the regression form gives a denser gradient.
