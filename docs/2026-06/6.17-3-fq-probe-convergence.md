# fq ↔ probe convergence: a materialize → train substrate

*Date: 2026-06-17. Status: design / direction. The full unification described here is a **loosely-held ambition — a hypothesis to disconfirm**, not a plan. The near-term commitment is small: one modest step pulled into existence by the argmin toy experiments (§7). Everything larger is trigger-gated on a real second consumer, per fq's own "let the second consumer pull the abstraction" principle — and existing reuse (30-line `ProbeTask` subclasses already share machinery without any of this) is the standing reminder that the heavier waist must earn its place.*

## 1. Thesis

The `fq` analysis toolkit and the frozen-representation probe framework were built separately, for different jobs — fq to kill val-split-walk boilerplate for numpy analysis, the probe framework to ask "is signal X decodable from representation Y, at what capacity." Looked at by structural affordance — what the code is close to becoming — they are **two halves of one `materialize → train` pipeline**, converging on a shared substrate from opposite sides. fq approaches it from the data side; the probe framework from the training side.

What the two tools literally share is modest — a dict of N-major columns, the generic shape of any dataframe — so their convergence is suggestive, not proof of a deep abstraction. The honest reading splits in two:

- A **minimal waist** that is real and small: a column→tensor function plus a neutral home for the few definitions that must match prod (§5). This earns its place as soon as a second consumer needs it.
- A **fuller unification** — the three-layer engine (§3), `FeatureSource` retiring into fq (§2) — that is the *loosely-held ambition*: a hypothesis to disconfirm as real consumers arrive, not a committed design.

The convergence is **additive**: we factor out a shared waist and keep each tool's first-class uses distinct and well-furnished. The goal is not to blend two tools into one general-purpose tool that does neither job well — that would degrade both. §4 (use cases) and its rule discussion exist to hold that line.

## 2. The duality at the center

The probe framework's in-memory training container and fq's analysis container are the same object viewed two ways.

```
ProbeCache:  feats[N,C,H,W]   target[N,…]   aux{name→[N,…]}              (torch; no provenance)
FrameTable:  cols{name→[N,…]}                            persp_val_index, frame_t, game_id   (numpy; + provenance)
```

`ProbeCache` is a `FrameTable` in which one column is privileged as `feats`, one as `target`, and the rest as `aux` — minus provenance, in torch instead of numpy. The privileging is a **training-time role assignment**, not a fundamental difference in the data.

Two consequences follow:

- `FeatureSource` is a `Deriver` *by type* — a frozen-trunk feature is another per-frame quantity, a `trunk_feats` column. But *operationally* they are opposites: a `Deriver` is a cheap per-frame numpy read in a Python loop; a `FeatureSource` is a batched trunk forward, chunked and parked on CPU in fp16 because it will not otherwise fit. That operational gap is much of *why* the probe framework is separate machinery, so the cleanest answer may be that `FeatureSource` never enters fq at all (§6). Treat the subset claim as type-true and operationally open.
- `ProbeTask` factors into `{target deriver}` + `{models, loss, metrics}`. The "which signal" is a derived column; the rest is the training spec.

Run that to its conclusion and the reduction is *partial*. The probe framework's **data-walk and target extraction** reduce to fq derivers; its **feature-production machinery** (the trunk forward and its chunked CPU caching) most likely stays separate furniture rather than becoming an fq deriver. So the mature picture is `fq (materialize data + targets)` + `a training engine (sweep small models)` + `the probe's feature/decodability furniture on top`. fq owns the better *data model* (named columns, provenance, canonical single-source derivers, representativeness guards); the probe framework keeps its training engine and its furniture.

## 3. The waist (the shared substrate)

Three layers, with the containers *layered* rather than merged:

- **`FrameTable` (fq)** — the source. Numpy columns + provenance. Training-agnostic; knows nothing about models or loss.
- **role-assignment view** — a thin "these columns are inputs, this is the target, these are aux" wrapper that the *engine* owns (not fq). Keeps fq pure.
- **device tensor bundle** — the engine builds this from a table view for one training run. This is what `ProbeCache` becomes: demoted from "the source" to "the training-time materialization of a row set." `FrameTable` and `ProbeCache` are not unified into one object; they are *source* and *training-time materialization* of the same rows.

The **training engine** is then: *train a sweep of small models over `(table view, models, loss, metrics)`* — cache to device, run the AdamW train/eval loop, record per-epoch curves and metrics. Today this is `cache_probe_features` + `train_probe_head` + `run_probe`. Maturing it is more than a flag-flip. `cache_probe_features` is a structurally different walk from `build_frame_table` (torch tensors and no provenance, vs numpy columns with provenance, `per_game` caching, and a forced `shuffle_buffer_size=0`), so it would be *replaced* by "fq table → device bundle," not patched. And the loop's single spatial `feats[N,C,H,W]` — consumed by conv heads as `head(feats, aux)` — has to generalize to a role-assigned set of columns that may be plain `[N,8]` tabular inputs. Each piece is bounded, but it is real reshaping, not a one-line generalization. §7 takes only the first slice of it.

**Dependency direction — a rule to establish at integration time, not a current invariant.** When the engine reads fq tables, the dependency must run `engine → fq` and `fq` must import nothing from the model/training stack. Today there is no dependency between `fq/` and `probes/` in *either* direction — the probe engine walks `IterableDataset` directly, not an fq table — so this is a rule to hold *when* the integration happens. The one real dependency now is `analysis/families/ → fq` (domain derivers built on the base), which is already the correct direction. Shared definitions belong in a neutral home both sides import; none exists yet (see §6).

### The rules, judged against the use cases

Most rules are instrumental — they serve a use case and are relaxable when a use case says so. Two are near-intrinsic *guards*, and even those are justified by concrete cost, not dogma.

- **fq-standalone (serves F1) — near-intrinsic.** The moment fq needs a checkpoint or the model/training stack to answer a question, F1 dies. State the rule as an *import-graph* constraint, because that is what actually protects F1: **`fq` imports nothing from the model/training stack.** Discipline-shaped rules (e.g. "model-derivers are opt-in") do not protect it — opt-in-at-spec-level still puts the model stack in fq's dependency graph. So heavy, model-dependent features stay *out* of `fq` entirely.
- **Don't re-implement pandas badly — near-intrinsic scope guard.** Data-processing libraries are a famously deep well of edge cases; a half-reimplemented one is worse than none. So `FrameTable` stays a dumb columnar container and analyses drop to numpy / `pd.DataFrame(t.cols)`. The carve-out: a *super-tight, well-contained thin helper is fine if it demonstrably cannot spill into pandas-reimplementation*. `subset(table, mask)` is safe; a `groupby`/`agg`/`merge`/`pivot` surface is where reimplementing pandas begins and stays out. This rule is held on engineering risk, not aesthetics.
- **"Derivers are lightweight, dependency-light" — the tempting relaxation probably loses.** The trunk-feature-as-a-column idea (P1/P4) tempts fq toward a heavy model-forward deriver. But that collides head-on with the import-graph rule above: a model-deriver *anywhere in fq's import graph* breaks F1, and "opt-in" does not change the graph. So the likely resolution is not an opt-in heavy tier inside fq but **model-features produced engine-side, never becoming fq columns at all** (§6, Option 3). fq's lightweight-deriver character is then preserved, not relaxed.
- **"Dumb container, no groupby/agg" — held because nothing is *blocked*, not because of purity.** Game-clustered CIs and margin-binning (P3) *want* group-and-aggregate, but numpy at the consumer serves them — more verbose, not blocked. If a real use case is ever blocked, drop the rule without ceremony (subject to the pandas guard above).
- **"Probe decodability stays first-class" — not a structural rule at all, it is P1/P2.** A refactor that generalizes the engine but keeps "point at a checkpoint, ask is-it-decodable" crisp is fine; one that makes P1 clunkier in the name of merging is not. The test is the use case, not the structure.

## 5. Relationship to the prod training loop

There are effectively three loops: prod (`bc/train.py` — `TrainConfig`, `BCModel`, `bc_loss`, the streaming `DataLoader`, MFU instrumentation, checkpointing, Modal), the probe/analysis engine (small models on cached features), and — emerging — the unified training engine of §3. The question is how much the analysis engine and prod should share.

The answer turns on splitting "similar" into two kinds:

- **Semantic similarity** — same *loss*, same *metric*, same *model module*, same *data quantity*. You want this; divergence here is a correctness bug. The clearest example: the probe's `masked_cross_player_ce` is a hand-kept copy of the prod elim loss in `bc/loss.py` — two definitions meant to be identical, kept in sync by discipline. That is a latent version of the army-from-sim/obs bug F2 was built to kill.
- **Mechanical similarity** — same optimizer-step code, batching, epoch driver, checkpointing. You do not need this and should not force it. Prod is streaming-throughput-bound (workers, autocast, MFU, a moving target being learned); analysis is cached-measurement-bound (in-RAM, single-process, frozen substrate, weight inspection, sample-complexity subsetting). The mechanics do not change *what is measured*, only how fast.

**Working heuristic, not a clean law: maximize semantic sharing, minimize mechanical coupling.** The analysis engine is semantically faithful to prod (imports the same definitions) but mechanically independent (owns its own loop). Prod sits *outside* the waist; it connects only through the shared definition layer and the shared data format (use case X is the existence proof — online analysis touches prod's state, shares definitions and columns, never the loop).

The detail that matters: a definition gets single-sourced **exactly when a use case requires the analysis result to be faithful to prod** — not "all losses/metrics live in a shared home."

- Cross-player CE: **share** — decodability probes (P1) and the real-aux-head experiments (P4) measure something that must match prod's loss. (The argmin toy, P3, only needs *a* CE, not prod's exact one — so this single-sourcing is a P4-bridge item, not part of the toy's near-term step; see §7.)
- A tie-aware accuracy or a `W ≈ −I` weight check: **do not share** — a new measurement with no prod counterpart; a shared home would be busywork.
- The army quantity: **already shared** (F2), because cross-analysis agreement demands it.

The boundary is drawn per-definition by faithfulness-requirement, not by type — and it is genuinely fuzzy at the edges. It can run *through the middle of one function* (share the cross-player CE formula, but not prod's reduction or class-weighting), and a single experiment can be faithful in its *loss* while deliberately unfaithful in its *metric* (the toy trains with prod's CE but reports a tie-aware accuracy prod has no counterpart for). So "single-source where faithfulness is required" stays a heuristic that needs a judgment call each time, not a rule that resolves itself.

## 6. Open questions

- **Where model-running derivers live (the sharpest one).** `FeatureSource ⊂ Deriver` is type-true but operationally false (§2): a trunk-feature deriver is a heavy torch model-forward that would drag the model stack into fq's import graph, breaking F1. The leading answer is therefore **Option 3: `FeatureSource` stays the probe framework's furniture and never enters fq** — model-features are engine-side, not fq columns. The alternatives (a heavy "model-deriver" tier inside fq; riding the deferred lazy-obs `FrameView`) are on the table only if a consumer makes the F1 cost worth paying. Resolve when the P4 consumer exists, not before.
- **Synthetic / analytic row source (F5).** Should `build_frame_table` accept row sources other than a dataset walk (a generator of controlled distributions)? The toy's analytic labels are the pull. Extrapolation can be had from *filtered real frames* for now, so this is anticipated, not demanded.
- **The neutral home for shared definitions.** Where do single-sourced losses/metrics that must match prod live, such that prod, the analysis engine, and fq all import them without circular dependencies? No such home exists yet: `FrameNeeds` currently lives inside the engine (`probes/core.py`), imported only by `probe_runs/`, and the cross-player CE is a copy. These would be the first definitions to move to such a home — unstarted work.
- **Exact semantic-share boundary cases.** §5 names the principle; the per-definition calls beyond the three examples need to be made as they come up.
- **What triggers the probe migration.** When does the probe framework's bespoke walk actually move onto the fq table + role-assigned engine? Gated on the engine proving out on a real consumer (the toy is the first; P4 is the one that exercises the trunk-feature path).

## 7. Worked example: the argmin toy

The argmin-toy experiments (design in the 6.17-1 / 6.17-2 docs) are the first consumer that instantiates the *minimal* waist, and a good test of whether the picture holds. The task: from one frame, predict the index of the alive player with the smallest army — a deliberately isolated cross-player-comparison step, studied across input encodings and architectures.

How it maps to the waist:

- **fq materializes** a per-frame table: `army[8]`, `land[8]`, `alive[8]`, `label` (lowest-index argmin over alive), `margin` (bottom-two gap), `n_alive`, `game_id`. The label and margin already exist as derived columns; only a land deriver is new. This makes the toy **fq's first training consumer (F4)**.
- **The engine sweeps** the model ladder (the linear / equivariant / shared-φ / pooled-MLP / flatten families) over that table, with tie-aware metrics and weight inspection. This is **P3** — learning dynamics on a known-decodable signal.

The modest first step it pulls into existence (the only near-term commitment):

- A land deriver and a lean toy `FrameTableSpec` — small, unambiguously on-fq's-gradient, reusing the existing label/margin derived columns.
- A training loop written to read a table + an explicit role-assignment, rather than hardcoding `army`/`alive` — the *seed of the table-reading engine*, sidestepping the `ProbeCache` costume (the toy's player-axis models take real inputs, not a vestigial `feats`), and promotable later if the P4 (real-aux-head) consumer pulls it.

Explicitly deferred to their trigger consumers: single-sourcing the cross-player CE into a neutral home (a P4-bridge faithfulness concern — the toy only needs *a* CE, not prod's exact one), `FeatureSource → deriver` (model-running derivers), migrating the probe framework's walk onto the engine, the synthetic row source, the lazy-obs `FrameView`, and table caching. None are needed for the toy; each waits for a real consumer to demand it.

The concrete build steps for the toy are a separate follow-up doc, written with this direction in hand so the small step we take is the *first inch of the right path* rather than a throwaway.
