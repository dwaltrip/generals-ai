# Elim-head gate: Gap-1 survives the corrected obs

*Date: 2026-06-19. Status: result + direction. Runs the gate from [6.18-9](6.18-9-elim-head-synthesis-and-direction.md) §3.B on the corrected obs from [6.19-1](6.19-1-obs-player-status-channels-landed.md). Answers 6.19-1's "what's next" gate item. Branch: `elim-head-fix-obs-bugs`. Sweep: `2026-06-19-elim-head-coherent-ab`.*

## TL;DR

- The gate was a clean **elim-off vs next_death A/B** on the corrected obs (sub_5k, seed 0, λ_elim 0.15, n20), holding everything but the elim head fixed.
- **Gap-1 survives.** Even at its best the head trails the lowest-army rule (peak top-1 44.8% vs 47.2%), loses the frames where it disagrees with the rule, and is worst in the imminent regime. The obs-correctness fix was necessary but not sufficient — so the §3.C representation work rises in priority. (Today's part is ruling out the bug; the case that the bottleneck is signal was already standing — 6.15-2, the lost disagreements, the missing army-rate channel.)
- The obs fix **helped modestly** (better peak, less memorization), but **epochs and (very likely) data are not the lever** — more epochs overfit, more data didn't lift the ceiling last time. Consistent with the signal/representation-bound hypothesis (6.18-9 §2).
- **Do-no-harm, recharacterized:** the next_death head carries a *small but real* policy tax (~+0.015–0.020 val CE, ~0.5pp top-1), present on both broken and corrected obs and **larger than the time-bin head's within-noise tax**. Prior "do-no-harm holds" for next_death was transferred from the time-bin head and missed it.

## The gate result (measured): Gap-1 survives

next_death on the corrected obs, 5 epochs (ep1–3 in the A/B, ep4–5 via a clean resume):

| ep | HEAD top-1 | dt<10 | val next_elim CE | disagree (head/rule right) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 41.2% | 32.9% | 1.6460 | 22.1 / 32.1 |
| 2 | 41.7% | 37.6% | 1.5162 | 23.0 / 33.1 |
| **3** | **44.8%** | **41.2%** | **1.5008** (min) | 26.6 / 31.0 |
| 4 | 42.3% | 39.9% | 1.5682 | 24.3 / 33.3 |
| 5 | 41.2% | 39.9% | 1.6157 | 23.6 / 33.9 |

- Lowest-army rule: **47.2%**. The head peaks below it (ep3), then degrades.
- **Loses the disagreements:** on the ~54% of frames where head ≠ lowest-army, the *rule* is right more often (31.0% vs 26.6% even at the ep3 peak). So the head is a worse discriminator than the trivial rule on contested frames, not a source of signal beyond it.
- Worst in the **imminent dt<10** regime (41.2% vs the rule's 48.1%) — the regime the head was supposed to add value in. It only beats the rule at far horizon (dt>200), the easy position-driven part.

Same shape as the broken-obs run. So the §3.B read is "still trails": the obs bug isn't the cause, which adds to the prior case for signal/representation — the §3.C work rises in priority.

## The obs fix helped — small and consistent (measured; magnitude single-seed)

Best-vs-best, each run at its ep3 peak:

| | broken-5k | corrected-5k |
|---|---|---|
| top-1 | 44.0% | 44.8% |
| dt<10 | 39.0% | 41.2% |
| val CE | 1.541 | 1.501 |

Plus less train-side memorization at every epoch. But the advantage lives **at the peak** — by ep5 the corrected and broken val CE coincide (~1.616), washed out by overfit.

Calibration on this improvement: the **attribution** is clean — the two runs share seed 0 and differ only in the obs, so the consistent gap (val lower through ep4, train higher throughout) is caused by the obs, not a seed draw. The **effect size** is not established: *every* next_death run is seed 0 (the PoC, the 11k pair, this gate), so no `next_elim` seed band exists anywhere, and whether the ~0.04 val CE gap would survive reseeding is untested. This is the weaker of the session's two single-seed claims — the do-no-harm tax at least replicated across two obs settings, whereas this improvement *is* the broken-vs-corrected comparison, so it has no second axis to lean on.

## Epochs ruled out (measured); data very likely ruled out (hypothesis)

- **Epochs:** the corrected run overfits at **ep3→ep4** — val CE *and* top-1 both peak at ep3 then degrade — same onset as broken. More epochs make the head worse. The disambiguated status lowered the overfit *gap* but did not postpone the *turn*.
- **Data:** the broken-obs 11k run (`2026-06-14-yolo-11k-elim-head-v2`) did **not** lift the ceiling over 5k (best-val ~1.585 vs 1.541; peak top-1 ~43% vs 44%, both far below the rule); its worse late overfit is partly a step-count artifact (5 epochs of 2× data ≈ 2× steps). Combined with corrected-5k matching the broken-5k pattern, this **predicts** corrected-11k won't help — but **corrected-11k is untested** (we judged the confirmatory run not worth it). Labeled hypothesis.
- Net: epochs aren't the lever and scale very likely isn't — pointing to Gap-1 being signal/representation-bound (6.18-9 §2, 6.14-1), itself still a hypothesis until a signal lever is shown to fix it.

## Do-no-harm: a more accurate measurement of an existing phenomenon

The clean A/B is the first **controlled** elim-off-vs-next_death comparison (the 6.13 next_death PoC had the head on in every cell; 6.13-15's "do-no-harm holds" was *transferred* from the time-bin head's validation, with no elim-off arm of its own). It shows the next_death head carries a small but real, consistent policy cost — **not** literally zero:

- **Corrected-obs A/B (ep1–3, matched control):** next_death val_policy ~+0.014 CE / ~0.5pp top-1 worse than elim-off, every epoch and in train too.
- **Broken-obs, assembled from existing runs:** pairing the `2026-06-13-elim-head-stage1` elim-off anchor (`value-lam0.1`) with the `2026-06-13` next_death PoC (`elim0.15-v0.1`) — identical config, same seed, both broken obs — gives **~+0.020 val CE / ~0.54pp top-1**, consistent across all 5 epochs. (Cross-sweep but same-seed/same-config — the comparison basis 6.13-11 itself used.)

So the tax is **pre-existing** (visible on broken obs, just never assembled into a comparison) and **the head's**, not the obs change — it's ~the same on both obs (corrected slightly smaller). And it is **larger than the time-bin head's**: the time-bin do-no-harm validations (6.13-7, 6.13-11) found ≤0.005 val_policy, within the single-seed band; next_death's ~+0.015–0.020 is ~3–4× that. Mechanistically plausible — next_death is a single cross-player softmax pushing one shared-trunk gradient vs. the time-bin head's per-player bin CE — but that explanation is a hypothesis.

**How to read it:** "do-no-harm" should mean *acceptably small harm*, not *no harm*. The tax is a cost against a benefit the head does not currently provide (it doesn't beat the rule), so the next_death head is a **research diagnostic, not shippable in the policy model as-is**; it's tunable via λ_elim if a signal lever makes it useful.

**Calibration:** all of this is **seed 0**. The tax is consistent across two independent obs settings (broken + corrected), which argues it's real, not seed jitter — but next_death's *own* seed variance is uncharacterized. The value head is unaffected in the corrected A/B (value CE converges by ep3); value wasn't separately pulled for the broken pair.

## Direction

The short-term target is a 5k run with minimal overfit and val beating the heuristic. Long-term, the elim-head should help improve the value-head — this was the original motivating impulse.

### Re-grounding: what success might look like for the elim-head

- **Success metric (layered).** Improving the value head is the goal (above), but it can't be evaluated while the head looks broken on its own terms — so the metric is two gated tiers.
    - **Tier 0 — the head is sound (the current gate).** Both required: (a) *beats the heuristics* — val top-1 surpasses the best model-free baseline (today lowest-army 47.2%; once lowest-land / army-delta land, the best of them), and wins or holds the frames where it *disagrees* with the rule (matching top-1 by agreeing everywhere doesn't count); and (b) *healthy curve* — elim val loss at least **plateaus** across epochs, ideally keeps falling (the current peak-at-ep3-then-degrade fails this). Plateau + beats-heuristics is the acceptable floor; monotonic-falling is the stretch.
    - **Tier 1 — the head earns its place (the actual goal).** With the elim-head ON, the **value head** is measurably better than with it OFF — a clean A/B on the value head's own metric, read through two channels: **lower value val CE** (representation enrichment — the trunk encodes death/threat structure value draws on) and **less value-head overfit / a healthier value curve** (regularization — the aux gradient makes the shared trunk more robust; elim-head is one regularizer among several like dropout/weight decay, but the only one that also enriches features). Today the aux head does the *opposite* (a small policy tax, value ~neutral — the do-no-harm read above), so Tier 1 is a **flip**, not a tightening.
    - **The tiers are causally ordered.** An aux head that *memorizes* (unhealthy Tier-0 curve) pushes idiosyncratic gradients into the shared trunk — an *anti-regularizer*, injecting noise rather than constraining — so it can only no-op or hurt value. An aux head that learns *generalizable* structure both enriches and regularizes. So a healthy Tier-0 curve is the **precondition** for the head to help value at all, not just a milestone before it. (Mechanistic argument — held as a hypothesis.) The shrinking train/val gap stays a *leading indicator*, with "val keeps falling / plateaus" now named as a Tier-0 condition in its own right.

### What to try next

If Gap-1 is signal-bound (one of our leading hypotheses), the head overfits for lack of generalizable signal in its input — it memorizes train idiosyncrasies because there's nothing better to learn. On that reading, a §3.C signal lever should shrink the train/val gap *and* lift the val ceiling.

Items we've been considering:

- **5k is a cheap screen.** A lever that does nothing at 5k is very unlikely to be the fix. Caveat: a data-hungry lever (e.g. learning to read army *trends*) could under-show at 5k, so don't fully trust a 5k *null* for a lever with strong priors; direction-positive at 5k → scale with confidence.
- **Cheap experiment to try:** the army-delta ("attacked hardest") trend baseline already exists in `who_dies_next_baselines` — pull its numbers; then add the still-missing **lowest-land / land-delta** baselines (§3.B wanted them). They're model-free and tell us *which* signal is predictive (does land or a trend beat lowest-army?) — so the obs change targets a known-predictive signal instead of a guess.
- Then we work **obs enhancements** / **improved representations**: Add the signal to the obs (leaderboard trend / army-rate is a confirmed obs gap; or land), and look for the un-turning-over 5k val curve.
    - Look more broadly at other ways of improving the representation.
- The imminence-weighted soft target is also parked in 6.18-9 §3.C.
