# Data-starvation sweep results and pin_memory research

**Date:** 2026-06-06

## What we did

Ran the config sweep planned in [`6.06-5`](6.06-5-handoff.md): 8 cells crossing N ∈ {5, 20} × prefetch_factor ∈ {2, 4} × pin_memory ∈ {true, false}, each 800 batches on H100 with profiling and the new per-span distribution stats from [`6.06-5` step (a)](6.06-5-handoff.md). Sweep data is in `training/data/sweeps/2026-06-07-data-starve-handoff-probe/`.

Then researched the PyTorch DataLoader pin_memory bottleneck in depth — mechanisms, known issues, alternative libraries, workarounds — and wrote it up as a reference doc.

## Sweep findings

Comparing the gen5+pin cells (pf2-pin-n5 at 2484 sps vs pf2-pin-n20 at 2084 sps — a ~16% throughput gap):

- **pin_memory=false is catastrophic.** h2d goes from ~4 ms to ~200 ms per batch, halving throughput. Not a viable configuration.
- **Workers are ~50% idle** at both n=5 and n=20. The 19% obs-build compute increase at n=20 is absorbed by idle headroom — workers are not the bottleneck. This rules out the memory-bandwidth hypothesis from [`6.05-3`](6.05-3-obs-pipeline-starvation-n20.md).
- **prefetch_factor 2 vs 4 makes no difference** at n=20 (2084 vs 2009 sps). Rules out a depth/latency issue.
- **12 workers** (tested separately, prior session) didn't help either.

The last three findings point the same direction: a throughput ceiling in the single-threaded handoff path between workers and GPU. The multi-second tail stalls in the `handoff` span (max 2.6–3.1 s, p50 ~1.8 µs) appear across all pinned configs regardless of N or prefetch.

One confound: pf4-pin-n5 drew a gen4 PCIe machine (us-east5), so that cell's numbers aren't comparable to the gen5 cells.

## Pin_memory research

The pin_memory thread bottleneck is a known PyTorch issue with two documented mechanisms: serial memcpy throughput through a single thread, and GIL contention from tensor destruction (`munmap` syscalls holding the GIL, measured at 50+ ms GPU bubbles on 8×A100). Pre-pinning in workers is not viable (shared memory and pinned memory are fundamentally incompatible — confirmed by PyTorch maintainer albanD).

Alternative libraries (DALI, FFCV, SPDL, TensorDict) redesign the pipeline rather than patching the pin_memory thread. TensorDict's multi-threaded pinning is the closest to a drop-in fix.

Full details, links, and analysis in [`pytorch-dataloader-pin-memory-bottleneck.md`](../pytorch-dataloader-pin-memory-bottleneck.md).

## Where we are

The bottleneck is bracketed to the handoff path (section B in [`6.06-4`](6.06-4-train-data-flow-profile-summary.md)) — confirmed by elimination from three directions. We now understand the architectural constraints of the pin_memory thread and what options exist. We have not yet directly instrumented the handoff path to confirm which specific mechanism is binding.

## Potential next moves

1. **Instrument the pin_memory thread** — monkey-patch `_pin_memory_loop` to time pin_memory() vs queue ops. Highest signal for the effort.
2. **fp16 obs** — halves bytes through the entire handoff path. Useful regardless of which mechanism is binding (already identified as leading lever in [`6.05-3`](6.05-3-obs-pipeline-starvation-n20.md)).
3. **TensorDict multi-threaded pinning** — evaluate integration with our custom pipeline.
4. **Accept ~16% and move on** — the gap is real but not catastrophic; other work may have higher ROI.
