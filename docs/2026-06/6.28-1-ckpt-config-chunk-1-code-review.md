## Overall

The chunk lands what it set out to land, and the code reads well on its own terms. The home split is the strongest part — four concerns (config *shape* / model *construction* / `.pt` *format* / *runtime state*) each have a clear module, and the boundaries are the ones a reader would draw independently of the doc. The read path is a single discriminator with two arms, no premature tier machinery. The round-trip test is genuinely load-bearing (full config equality, Paths included, through the `weights_only` constraint). The `is_arch_bearing` fix — the one mandatory correctness change — is additive and pinned by a test that asserts both the v0 invariant and the downstream `model_key` consequence.

The follow-up inventory in working-doc §7 is unusually honest; most of what I'd flag is already there. Below I separate (a) things I'd reinforce or sharpen in that list from (b) a few observations not currently captured.

## Structure & organization — solid

- **The home map holds up.** `bc/config` (torch-free by data), `bc/model_builder` (the construction seam), `bc/storage/checkpoint` (format adapter), `bc/state` (runtime). Each module's docstring states its one concern and the modules don't bleed into each other. The split between `TrainingState.to_dict` (runtime serialization) and `serialize_checkpoint` (format wrapping) mirrors the read side (`from_checkpoint` reads runtime keys, `load_checkpoint` reads format keys) — that symmetry is real and worth keeping.
- **`code_sha` placement is right.** Modeling it as an immutable `TrainingState` init fact alongside `config`, captured at the launch site rather than inside the serializer, matches its nature (code provenance, distinct from config) and correctly handles the remote-has-no-`.git` case. The `git_sha → data_manifest_sha` log rename plus the new startup `code_sha` line disambiguate the two SHAs at the points an operator reads them.
- **Path serde introspects `TrainConfig`'s type hints** rather than hardcoding the three names — the kind of small decision that stops a class of future rot. Shared as a `stringify_paths`/`rewrap_paths` pair while `resolve_config` and `from_file` keep their own assembly: the right call, since they genuinely differ (flat-loss shim, CLI overrides, version handling).

## Coupling

Mostly well-judged. The deliberate edges:

- **`storage/checkpoint.py → bc/checkpoint.py` (new → old)** for the legacy path (`arch_for_load`, `is_combined_checkpoint`). This is the documented strangler arrangement and the right direction — the new module is the entry point delegating back to the not-yet-migrated reconstruction. Fine.
- **`model_builder` → `train_config`** exists only for the `ConfiguredModel.config: TrainConfig | None` annotation. Harmless, but worth noticing that the "construction seam" module now imports the full recipe type purely for a wrapper field. If `ConfiguredModel` ever felt like it wanted its own home, this is the seam that would split.

One coupling point I'd add to the follow-up list:

- **The v1 discriminator ("config" key) is encoded in two modules.** `storage.is_versioned_checkpoint` returns `"config" in obj`; `checkpoint.is_arch_bearing` independently returns `... and ("config" in obj or "arch" in obj)`. Both now carry knowledge of where v1 records its presence. They can't be unified yet without inverting the strangler edge (old `checkpoint.py` would have to import the new `storage`), so leaving them split is defensible *for now* — but it's a second site that has to change when the layout does, and it isn't in §7's convergence list (which names the two `in_ch` homes but not this). Worth a one-line note next to the "two homes for `in_ch`" item: these converge together when v0 support shrinks and the discriminators move into `storage`.

## Initial-steps vs. groundwork balance — appropriate, with two thin spots

The chunk is deliberately conservative and that judgment reads as correct. Two places lean toward groundwork-ahead-of-use; both are defensible, noting them so the bet is explicit:

- **`build_model` is a one-line `BCModel(arch)` today.** It does nothing a direct call wouldn't. It earns its place on two grounds that are actually true here, not just asserted: it's the single seam *shared by both* `load_checkpoint` and `TrainingState.fresh` (so it's consolidation, not speculation), and it's the named home for the future configurable/historical builder. I'd keep it. If it were *only* the future-builder argument I'd push back; the shared-seam fact is what makes it carry weight now.
- **Every v1 `.pt` now carries the full `TrainConfig`, but nothing reads `cm.config`.** Inference and eval still read `cm.cfg` (the arch). This is real groundwork for chunk-2/resume-from-`.pt`, and the `cfg`-as-property indirection is what will make the chunk-2 storage flip contained. Reasonable. The one thing inside it I'd treat as the highest-value open question (and §7 already flags it): the block stores invocation-local fields (`run_dir`, `device`, `num_workers`, …) that describe one invocation, not the model — and `config.run_dir` would actively mislead a resume-from-`.pt`. Nothing reads them yet, so it's latent, but it's the open design decision most likely to bite, and it's the one I'd want settled before `cm.config` gets its first real consumer.

## Specific findings

Ordered by how much they'd matter. None are blockers; the chunk is behavior-preserving for real checkpoints (the writer-flip is gated behind the read path, exactly as the handoff specified).

1. **`_PATH_FIELDS` matches `hint is Path` exactly — an `Optional[Path]` field would silently slip through.** `tuple(name for name, hint in get_type_hints(TrainConfig).items() if hint is Path)` catches only bare `Path`. A future `Path | None` field would not be stringified, and `weights_only=True` would then reject the load at runtime with a non-obvious error. §7's "weights_only safety for a future non-`Path` field" covers the spirit, but the `Optional[Path]` case is the more likely concrete trigger (config fields drift toward optional), and the introspection currently gives a false sense that "any Path field is handled." Worth either widening the predicate to unwrap `Optional`/`Union` or asserting in the serializer that no leftover `Path` survives stringify.

2. **`migrate` assumes `config_version` inside a block that `is_versioned_checkpoint` only checked for `"config"` presence.** A malformed/truncated `config` block (present but missing `config_version`) raises a bare `KeyError` from `config["config_version"]` rather than a clear "not a valid versioned block." §7 names this exact gap — agreed it's real; flagging it here as low-cost to close (a guard in `migrate` or `resolve_config`) and worth doing before external/older artifacts flow through the v1 path.

3. **The `from_checkpoint` arch-gate comment now describes a v0-only path as if general.** Lines ~124–130: the `if "arch" in obj:` assert and its comment ("An arch-bearing checkpoint's recorded arch must match the resume config's arch") only ever fire for v0 now — a v1 checkpoint nests arch in `config`, so the gate is dormant. The working doc records this ("now-dormant `from_checkpoint` arch-gate — never fires for v1"), but the in-code comment reads as if the assert is the live general gate. Since this is explicitly deferred to resume rewiring, I'd leave the code but add half a sentence to the comment marking it v0-only, so a reader at the call site isn't misled before that rewiring lands.

4. **Tests re-implement `to_dict()` as a local `_runtime()` helper (twice).** `test_checkpoint._runtime` and the inline runtime dict in tests duplicate `TrainingState.to_dict`'s key set. It's defensible as testing the wire contract independently of the producer, but calling `state.to_dict()` would couple the round-trip test to the real serialization surface and catch drift between the two. Minor; mention only because the duplicated key list is exactly the thing the proposed `TypedDict` (§7) would unify.

5. **`serialize_checkpoint(runtime: dict[str, Any], ...)` is an untyped contract** — already the lead item in §7 ("type the v1 format contract"). Agreed it's the highest-value typing cleanup: the runtime keys are stringly-shared across `to_dict`, `serialize_checkpoint`, `load_checkpoint`, and `from_checkpoint` with no single source. Calling it out here only to second the priority.

- `value_head_variant` flows through `load_checkpoint`'s signature but is correctly consumed only on the legacy arm; the versioned arm ignores it. Containment is as designed.
- `in_ch` is written from `config.arch.in_ch` and validated against `model.cfg.in_ch` (same source, since the model is built from `config.arch`) — consistent, and the check runs before `load_state_dict` so the error stays clear.

- The `1026420` FLOPs fix (`out.policy_logits` attribute access vs. the old subscript) is correct and its trimmed comment reads clean.

## Addendum — read against the synthesis (6.26-5)

Re-reading the two-axes synthesis after the diff sharpened a few things and changed the priority of one. It does not change the overall verdict — if anything it raises my confidence that the scope cut was the right one.

**The code respects the shape≠behavior discipline, and doesn't overclaim.** The synthesis's central caution is "don't let a shape-fix stand in for a behavior-fix": a self-describing checkpoint is *necessary but not sufficient* for faithful reconstruction. Chunk-1 is correctly scoped to the shape axis only, and the code honors that — nothing in the docstrings or naming implies that carrying the full `TrainConfig` reproduces an old checkpoint's behavior. `code_sha` is described as provenance (which is all the synthesis claims for it — the loosest rung of the detection ladder, "over-triggers, not precise"), not as a drift guard. Good. The one place worth a sentence of care for future readers: `ConfiguredModel.config` being "the full recipe" can read as more than it is — it's the stored shape, not a fidelity guarantee. Not a change to request, just the conflation the synthesis says is the natural one to make.

**Two of the synthesis's "→ chunk-1" predictions were deliberately reversed by the later design, and the code correctly follows the newer doc.** This is worth stating explicitly so a reader cross-referencing the synthesis doesn't think something's missing:

- The synthesis projected a **`LoadPlan` tagged union** (Native real; Compat/Unsupported reserved-empty) as "the structural firewall" for the later tiers. 6.27-1 dropped it as premature for a two-way branch, and the code ships a single `is_versioned_checkpoint` discriminator. Crucially, the *underlying property* the synthesis wanted from LoadPlan — "tiers fillable later without re-plumbing callers" — is still satisfied by the as-built code, and arguably better: `load_checkpoint` returns `ConfiguredModel` uniformly across both arms, so a future Compat arm is an added internal branch returning the same type, and callers (`BCModelHandle`, the `.model` takers) never see the discrimination. The firewall is the uniform return type, not the union. So dropping LoadPlan cost nothing structural.
- The synthesis projected **reserving a nullable `arch_version` slot** in the v1 format. 6.27-1 §D reversed this (the can't-retrofit argument is weak when the value is null and the scheme is deferred), leaning toward a later `ModelConfig` field that rides inside the versioned block. The code has no `arch_version` — matching 6.27-1, not the synthesis. Correct per the superseding doc.

Both are sound calls; I flag them only because the synthesis is the durable conceptual reference and these two "→ chunk-1" lines in it are now stale.

**The one priority shift: the `v1.json` key-set freeze is the deferral with a clock on it.** My follow-up section endorsed the deferrals evenly. The synthesis (the frozen-vs-live discipline, and the `vN.json` snapshots as "the frozen reference that makes the chain checkable") plus 6.27-1 §C single one out as time-sensitive in a way the others aren't. The argument: the migrate *mechanism* shipped (empty `MIGRATIONS` + dispatch + `v > CURRENT` guard), but the *anchor* it rests on — a frozen snapshot of the current v1 key-set — was not pinned. Config shape changes come from *other* work at ~18/month, are imminent, and aren't fully controllable; if one lands before v1 is frozen, the "v1 baseline" gets reconstructed *after* a drift instead of captured at the known-correct moment. I verified no such snapshot exists in the tree. It is genuinely git-recoverable (you can reconstruct v1's key-set from history at the pre-change commit), so this is "mildly awkward if deferred," not a hazard — which is presumably why it was deferred. But it's cheap now and lossy-if-late, and that asymmetry is exactly the kind a review should surface: of everything in §7, this is the one I'd lean toward pulling forward rather than batching with the rest of the version-bump guardrail. Judgment call, and the git-recoverability makes either choice defensible.

**Axis-lens enrichments to existing findings** (re-framings, not new defects):

- **`in_ch` is the lone behavior-axis signal that actually landed in chunk-1.** The synthesis frames it as "the arch stamp's narrow cousin" — a narrow behavioral-drift checksum, not just a shape/integrity check. So alongside `load_state_dict(strict=True)`, `validate_in_ch` is one of the two behavioral guards present today (catching obs-channel-formula drift); the broader forward-golden / `arch_version` machinery is the deferred rest. The `validate_in_ch` docstring already frames it behaviorally ("the obs-channel formula drifted from the trained weights"), which is accurate under this lens.
- **The stored config block mixes the two axes.** The synthesis separates `arch`+obs (behavioral-reproduction) from `loss`+metrics (measurement-comparability), with operational fields belonging to neither. The v1 block lumps all three. For chunk-1's shape-storage purpose that's correct and I wouldn't split it. But it adds a reason the already-flagged "decide what the config block contains" follow-up matters: once a reader treats the block as authoritative, the behavioral fields and the measurement fields want to be reached for different purposes, and the operational fields (`run_dir` et al.) belong to neither and actively mislead a resume-from-`.pt`.
- **The frozen-migration discipline is correctly established while the live-drift legacy path is correctly quarantined.** The synthesis names *why* `LEGACY_ARCH` is the cautionary example: it's a frozen *instance* of the *live* `ModelConfig` class, so `arch_for_load`'s `asdict(LEGACY_ARCH)` fill "collapses to current shape" rather than pinning a fixed historical version. Chunk-1 leaves that path untouched (the deferred v0→v1 normalizer is what replaces it) while standing up the frozen, append-only `MIGRATIONS` mechanism for v1+. So the two disciplines coexist exactly as the strangler intends — the clean frozen path for new artifacts, the live-drift path quarantined behind the legacy delegate with a note that it's slated for replacement. Reinforces the structure assessment; no action.

## Bottom line

Chunk-1 is in good shape: the structure is clean and the boundaries are principled, the one mandatory correctness fix is additive and tested, and the conservative scope cut (read path before writer-flip) is honored. The groundwork-ahead-of-use is limited to two well-justified spots. The follow-up list is thorough and accurate — my code-level additions are small: the `Optional[Path]` sharpening (#1), the duplicated "config"-key discriminator as a named convergence point, and the two doc/test polish items (#3, #4). Reading against the synthesis confirms the scope cut and the two structural simplifications (no `LoadPlan`, no `arch_version` slot) were the right calls and preserve the properties the synthesis cared about; the one priority I'd raise from it is pulling the `v1.json` key-set freeze forward, since it's cheap now and lossy if a config change lands first. Nothing here argues against proceeding to the next chunk.
