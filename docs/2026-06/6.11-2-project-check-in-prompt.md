## Brief highlights and thoughts, looking back

To help get the ball-rolling and also provide a bit of grounding on my view of things, here are a few key highlights and stand-out items, from my perspective:

- We've made strong progress on value-head over-fitting. It's somewhat tamed / fixed. Not perfectly, but it feels like it's getting into the "acceptable" range. At least, based on my understanding of what's needed for a value-head once you leave the first phase of the plan for an ML project like ours. **Phase 1:** supervised learning training a BC model and **Phase 2:** reinforcement learning via self-play (PPO). I have some things I want to try before kicking off phase 2 in earnest. But, in theory, the value-head now looks like it isn't a major blocker.
    - This work also seems to have largely fixed the "dense-history" underperformance issue, where giving the model more knowledge about recent activity resulted in a *worse* val_policy number.
        - There is more that could be done here, as my baseline rough intuition is that going from n=5 to n=10 to n=20, where `n` is the number of timesteps of history available, should have a bigger impact on performance. A possible counterpoint may be that the model is simply not skilled enough to consistently make good use of the extra information (seems quite plausible to me).
- Instrumentation that is a standard part of training runs is significantly improved. As well as the tooling for interacting with the metrics and understanding them. `quality.md`, `compare_runs`, and so on.
    - This instrumentation and tooling was very helpful during the value-head work from the previous bullet. And those benefits should carry over into upcoming architecture work.
- Refactorings:
    - `bc/evals.py` became `bc/eval/*` with a much cleaner multi-file structure beginning to emerge. This became more important as we added more metrics-related code.
    - Similarly, `bc/model.py` is now `bc/model/*` with sub-modules `bc_model`, `trunk`, `heads.policy`, `heads.pass_head`, and `heads.value`. We will be doing more architecture experimentation, and a clean folder structure like this will make that much easier.
- Some nice improvements to `eval-tools` landed as well, including better metrics, code cleanups, and better support for multiplayer matchups. `--slot-rotations` is available which is basically the multiplayer version of the 1v1 only `--swap-slots` flag.

The first bullet above, about the value-head, is one that I'd like to get your most direct and focused feedback on, as I'm relatively new to ML, at least in terms of actual hands-on work [1]. Let me know if you think my description is solid or needs any clarification, based on the evidence you see.

[1] I have a math background, am an experienced software engineer, and have followed AI pretty closely for many years as a "sophisticated layperson" with strongly-adjacent technical skills. I have also done one previous non-trivial AI project where I created 2 different fine-tunes of yolov8 and then chained them together for a computer vision task.
