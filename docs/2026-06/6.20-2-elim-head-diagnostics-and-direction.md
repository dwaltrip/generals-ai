# Elim-head: diagnostic findings and revised direction

*Date: 2026-06-20. Companion to the numbers doc [6.20-1](6.20-1-elim-head-diagnostics-numbers.md), which holds all the data and reproduction steps; this doc does not restate the tables. Prior context: [6.19-2 (gate result)](6.19-2-elim-head-gate-result.md), [6.18-9 (synthesis & direction)](6.18-9-elim-head-synthesis-and-direction.md), [6.15-2 (army probe)](6.15-2-army-regression-probe-results.md).*

## Scope and focus

This session ran cheap model-free and frozen-representation diagnostics — no training runs. We were looking at why the head peaks below the lowest-army rule (44.8% vs 47.2% top-1), and what that implies for which candidate fixes to try next.

## Headline results

- Switching the elim head to a soft target — a distribution over the next victims rather than a single one-hot label — is the most compelling next experiment. The hard target forces a confident pick on the frequent near-ties at the bottom of the leaderboard, the overfitting is localized to the elim head, and a soft target is also plausibly more useful to the value head, which is the reason the head exists at all. This is a strong hypothesis, not yet run.
- Simple total-army rate features (deltas, worst-tick, percentiles; absolute or normalized) did not add predictive value beyond the lowest-army ranking, so adding leaderboard-rate channels to the obs is low priority.
- The head's shortfall traces to how it reads the army ranking it already has — it loses resolution at the bottom of the ranking — and to the target's order ambiguity, not to a missing leaderboard signal. The untested axis is local / spatial signal, the likely home of real headroom above the heuristics.

## Code changes

No changes to training code, and no fix applied. We did build a new, reusable analysis script for these baselines — `family_a_victim_baselines.py` (under `training/analysis/scripts/`):

- Should be easy to extend (e.g. with a land-based heuristic, see "Open questions" below)
- Added a `windowed_per_game` deriver to the `fq` toolkit to support the analysis
- Replaces the older, more brittle `who_dies_next_baselines.py`

## Caveats and standing context

Two caveats apply throughout:

- The frozen-representation probe numbers (6.20-1 analyses 3–6) are on the broken-obs 11k trunk checkpoint, single config. The gate showed the corrected obs barely moved the head, so the fidelity picture likely carries over, but that is inferred, not measured on the corrected-obs trunk.
- The model-free baselines (analyses 1, 8–11) are on the corrected-obs 5k split. The oracle figures there are upper bounds (they assume a perfect per-frame choice between two rules), not achievable accuracies.

Standing goal, unchanged: the elim head exists to improve the value head. Today it does the opposite by a small margin — it carries a policy tax of roughly +0.015 to +0.020 val CE and is value-neutral ([6.19-2](6.19-2-elim-head-gate-result.md)). So "help value" is a flip from slightly-negative, gated behind the head being sound (a healthy training curve, and matching or beating the model-free heuristics).

## Findings

Ordered by how much they shift the picture.

1. **Simple total-army rate features did not add predictive value beyond the army-level ranking.** As a complement to the lowest-army rule on the hard next-victim target, no rate construction (net change, worst single-tick drop, top-N worst ticks, percentile of per-tick losses; absolute or normalized) exceeded the accuracy of adding a uniform-random second guess — the exact `(lowest-army OR random)` bar of 59.0%. Only "2nd-lowest army" exceeded it (71.0%). Standalone, every rate construction also trails the lowest-army rule, under both hard and soft scoring. *Scope: total-army leaderboard series, hard-target complementarity. Not covered: complementarity under a soft target (untested), land, and per-opponent / border-local signal.* (6.20-1 analyses 8, 9, 11.)

2. **The predictable signal concentrates in the bottom of the army ranking.** The next victim is the lowest-army player 47% of frames, within the bottom two 71%, within the bottom three 82%. So the accuracy available beyond the 47% rule is mostly about resolving which of the already-weak players dies, not about supplying a missing leaderboard signal. (Analysis 9.)

3. **The head reaches the lowest-army victim through the convolutional trunk, and that path loses resolution at the bottom of the ranking.** Reading army off the frozen trunk and taking an exact argmin identifies the true weakest player ~52% of the time; off raw obs (near-exact army) ~71%. A no-model Monte-Carlo reproduces both numbers, and the cause is that the bottom-of-board army gaps are small (median 82, p10 = 1 army) relative to the regression residual (≈ ±54 army at R² 0.994). *Candidate fix, not yet tested: give the elim readout the exact per-player army directly, since exact army produces a correct argmin by construction.* (Analyses 3–5.)

4. **The next-victim target carries substantial order ambiguity.** The lowest-army player is among the next three to die 75% of frames but is the immediate next only 47%; the bottom cluster is frequently near-tied (p10 gap = 1 army). *Inferred:* a hard one-hot target penalizes the head for order coin-flips it cannot win, a plausible contributor to the overfitting. This motivates a soft target. (Analyses 5, 10.)

5. **The overfitting at epoch 3 appears localized to the elim head rather than the shared trunk.** The elim CE turns over at ep3 while the value CE on the same trunk stays flat. This is an indication — value's stability shows the overfit sits outside value's feature subspace — not proof the overfit is confined to the elim readout. It points toward elim-local regularizers over trunk-wide data scaling. (Analysis 2.)

6. **R² measures a different property than this task needs.** The probe's R² is a per-player longitudinal fit (against army's full range over a game); the head's task is a cross-sectional comparison at the bottom of a single frame. At matched noise the same fit reads ~0.99 longitudinally but ~0 (or negative) cross-sectionally at the bottom. *Consequence (inferred):* an army-reconstruction MSE loss optimizes the property R² measures, not the ranking the head needs, so its value for matching the rule is limited (its ceiling is the raw-obs conv result, ~0.71, below a direct exact-army input). (Analyses 4, 6, 7.)

7. **Two methodological corrections.** The earlier read of "(lowest-army OR best-delta) = 56.3%, ≈ +9pp of headroom" was an artifact of free coverage — any independent second guess adds ~9pp — and is superseded by the random-bar comparison in finding 1. Separately, the surprising argmin numbers were checked against an independent Monte-Carlo and a code audit, and are not a computation error.

## Implications for the candidate fixes

The fixes split by which part of "sound" they target. Under the working hypothesis that motivates this whole line ([6.18-9](6.18-9-elim-head-synthesis-and-direction.md), [6.19-2](6.19-2-elim-head-gate-result.md)), it is the *healthy-curve* condition, not the *beat-the-rule* condition, that is on the path to the value head: a head that memorizes pushes idiosyncratic gradients into the shared trunk, while a head that generalizes pushes structure the value head can use. That ordering, plus the evidence below, puts the soft target first.

**1. Soft target — the leading candidate (strong hypothesis, not yet a decision).** Replace the hard one-hot next-victim label with a distribution over the alive players. The evidence compelling us toward it:
- The target is genuinely mis-specified for a measurable fraction of frames (the order ambiguity in finding 4), so this is a target-correctness change with a regularizing side effect, not only a regularizer.
- The overfit is elim-local (finding 5), and this is the one head still on hard cross-entropy; soft targets are what previously addressed the value head's overfit.
- It removes no signal, and is a clean A/B at 5k.
- It is also the cheapest test of the leading overfit mechanism: if the curve flattens under a soft target, "hard CE on coin-flip frames drives the memorization" is confirmed; if not, that mechanism is ruled out cheaply.
- A calibrated death-risk distribution is plausibly a more value-relevant thing for the trunk to encode than an argmax (a second, independent reason tied to the actual goal), and unlike the direct-army-input idea, the gradient stays in the trunk. *(Hypothesis.)*

What it likely does **not** do: beat the rule on top-1. On near-ties the best calibrated output is roughly the prior over the bottom cluster, which is close to what the lowest-army rule already encodes. So judge a soft-target run on the training curve and the value A/B, not on whether it crosses 47%.

The soft-target *construction* involves several design choices (target shape, weighting, temperature). Those are open and left for a future session; this doc does not prescribe them.

**2. Direct exact-army input to the readout — a diagnostic, not a deployment fix.** This would let the head match the lowest-army rule by removing the conv-resolution loss (finding 3). But it bypasses the trunk, so it does little for the value head and is mildly in tension with the goal. Treat it as a test of "can the head match the rule when handed exact army," decoupled from the value objective — not as a change to ship.

**3. Elim-local dropout / weight decay — a control.** Cheap, and worth running alongside the soft target so the curve change can be attributed (target shape vs. generic regularization).

**4. Army-reconstruction MSE auxiliary loss — de-prioritized for matching the rule.** Finding 6: it optimizes the longitudinal property R² measures, not the cross-sectional ranking the head needs. Its only remaining rationale is trunk enrichment for the value head (Tier 1), not closing the gap to the rule.

**5. Leaderboard total-army rate channels — low priority.** Finding 1: no measured value beyond army level on the hard target.

## Open questions and scope limits

- **Local / spatial signal is untested, and is the likely home of real headroom above the heuristics.** None of the heuristics here use per-tile or border-local information. Daniel's read, from FFA domain knowledge: this signal definitely carries predictive power above any simple leaderboard heuristic; the open question is magnitude, not existence. It also plausibly fares better under a soft target. All of it is still on the table.
- **The rate result is hard-target complementarity only.** Whether army-rate complements the lowest-army rule under a *soft* target is untested — a cheap follow-up against the existing scripts. (It would not change the priority: rate trails level standalone under soft scoring too.)
- **Land is the one leaderboard axis not swept.** The new baseline tool (`family_a_victim_baselines.py`) is built to swap the signal series from army to land; land likely wants its own constructions rather than a 1:1 reuse of the army menu.
- **A confirmatory probe is cheap:** `regress_then_argmin` on the corrected-obs 5k trunk, to verify the fidelity picture (finding 3) holds there and isn't specific to the broken-obs 11k checkpoint.
- **An unrun structural diagnostic:** the value-off / value-on army-R² probe — does training the value head degrade the trunk's army fidelity? It bears on what produces the 0.52 number in finding 3, and on whether rebalancing value vs. elim is itself a lever.
- **When a soft target is run, re-measure the policy tax and the value A/B.** The elim gradient changes shape, so the do-no-harm read and the Tier-1 comparison have to be re-taken, not assumed.
