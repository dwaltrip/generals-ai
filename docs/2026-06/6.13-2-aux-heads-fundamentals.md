# Auxiliary Prediction Heads on a Shared Trunk — Fundamentals (Agent A)

Literature review for the generals.io BC→PPO project. Charter: when do auxiliary prediction heads on a shared representation actually help the primary policy/value objective, and when do they backfire?

**Project anchor for "relevance" notes:** we add a per-frame "next-elimination" head (per-player categorical: which currently-alive player is eliminated next) read off the shared U-Net spatial trunk, intended to enrich the trunk that the BC policy and future PPO critic both inherit. The placement value head (8-way categorical, one fixed label per game per perspective) is sparse; the aux head is meant to give dense per-frame signal.

**Verification convention:** each finding tagged "verified against source: <url>" (I fetched/read the source content) or "model knowledge, UNVERIFIED" (could not confirm against primary text in this pass). PDFs from arxiv/DeepMind/Nature mostly would not decompress through the fetch tool; where I relied on a secondary analysis page or search-result excerpt rather than the primary text, that is noted and the claim is tagged accordingly.

## Area 1 — Auxiliary tasks in deep RL / game agents, with evidence

### General Value Functions / Horde — Sutton et al. 2011, "Horde: A Scalable Real-time Architecture for Learning Knowledge from Unsupervised Sensorimotor Interaction" (AAMAS)

- **Verification:** verified against the primary PDF https://www.cs.swarthmore.edu/~meeden/DevelopmentalRobotics/horde1.pdf (Sutton, Modayil, Delp, Degris, Pilarski, White, Precup; AAMAS 2011, pp. 761–768).
- **What it did:** Proposes representing knowledge as **General Value Functions (GVFs)** — a value function whose policy, termination/discount function, and (pseudo-)reward function are all *parameters*, so a GVF answers a predictive question of the form "if I follow policy π, what is the discounted accumulation of signal c, terminating per γ?" Horde is an architecture of many independent **"demons"**, each learning one GVF in parallel, off-policy (via gradient-TD methods), in real time from a single stream of experience while some other behavior policy acts. GVFs can encode "will event X happen / how soon / how much" predictions about sensors and outcomes.
- **Evidence:** Horde is the *conceptual framework* for predictive-knowledge auxiliary tasks, not a benchmark-with-ablation result; UNREAL and successors are commonly framed as instances of learning GVFs as auxiliary tasks (auxiliary control = GVFs with pseudo-reward = pixel change; reward prediction = a GVF-style predictive question). Treat the framework as verified; "UNREAL builds on Horde" is a standard framing (the auxiliary-control tasks are explicitly cast as GVFs) — verified at the level of the framework correspondence, not a quantified claim.
- **Relevance to our next-elimination head:** This is arguably the cleanest conceptual framing for our head. "Which currently-alive player is eliminated next" is naturally a **GVF / predictive question**: the cumulant is the elimination event, the termination is the moment that player dies, and the prediction is read off the shared representation each frame. Framing the head as a GVF (a) makes the per-frame, off-policy nature explicit (we predict from human-replay experience, not the policy's own rollouts), and (b) clarifies that we are adding *one demon* to a Horde — the design question is whether that demon's gradients should shape the shared trunk (our goal) and at what weight. It also makes the natural extensions legible: "how soon is the next elimination" (a discounted/timing GVF) is a denser variant of the same head if the categorical proves too sparse.

### UNREAL — Jaderberg et al. 2016, "Reinforcement Learning with Unsupervised Auxiliary Tasks"

- **Verification:** verified against source (ar5iv rendering): https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/abs/1611.05397 ; abstract https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05397
- **What it did:** A3C base agent (CNN-LSTM) plus three auxiliary tasks sharing the same representation:
  - **Pixel Control** — auxiliary Q-policies trained to maximally change pixel intensity in cells of an n×n grid over the input (a dense, spatial control signal).
  - **Reward Prediction** — from three recent frames predict the sign of the next reward as a 3-way class (zero / positive / negative); training minibatches are *resampled* so zero and non-zero rewards are equally represented, to fight reward sparsity without biasing the policy.
  - **Value Function Replay** — extra value-function regression on resampled replay windows to speed value iteration.
- **Loss / weighting:** `L_UNREAL = L_A3C + λ_VR·L_VR + λ_PC·Σ_c L_Q^(c) + λ_RP·L_RP`. λ_RP and λ_VR were set to 1.0; λ_PC was tuned log-uniform over [0.01, 0.1] (Labyrinth) and [0.0001, 0.01] (Atari).
- **Evidence it helped (ablation):** On Labyrinth (13 levels) A3C baseline reached ~54% human-normalized; full UNREAL reached ~87% (≈61% absolute lift) with a ~10× mean learning speedup (up to ~18× on one nav level). On Atari (57 games) UNREAL reached ~880% mean / ~250% median human-normalized, beating A3C and Prioritized Dueling DQN, and was markedly more robust to hyperparameters. The paper states combining the auxiliary tasks gives further improvement over any single one; per-task isolated deltas are shown in their Figure 4 but not all quantified in text.
- **Relevance to our next-elimination head:** This is the canonical "dense auxiliary signal on a shared trunk improves the main objective, demonstrated by ablation" result, and it is RL not just supervised MTL. Reward-prediction's *class-balanced resampling* is a directly transferable idea: our next-elimination labels are imbalanced (most frames are far from any elimination; the identity distribution is skewed toward weak players), so balancing/ reweighting the aux minibatch matters. Pixel Control shows spatial, per-cell auxiliary heads on a conv trunk are a proven shape.

### AlphaStar — Vinyals et al. 2019, Nature "Grandmaster level in StarCraft II"

- **Verification:** Nature full text is paywalled (verified redirect to auth wall: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1724-z). Claims below are verified against the public DeepMind preprint PDF's secondary analyses: The Gradient deep-dive https://cyk1337.github.io/notes/2019/07/21/RL/DRL/Decipher-AlphaStar-on-StarCraft-II/ and the public PDF https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/research/alphastar/AlphaStar_unformatted.pdf (PDF text did not extract directly; relying on the analysis page + search excerpts, so the architecture-level claims are tagged accordingly).
- **What it did (relevant pieces):**
  - **Strategy statistic z** (verified via The Gradient analysis): z encodes the first ~20 build-order entries plus cumulative statistics from a sampled human replay; the policy is conditioned on z. During training, pseudo-rewards measure edit distance between sampled and executed build orders and Hamming distance between sampled and executed cumulative statistics. This is *conditioning + shaping*, not a separate prediction head, but it is the human-data auxiliary signal.
  - **Value/baseline uses opponent observations** (model knowledge / search-excerpt, UNVERIFIED against primary text): the value function is fed the *opponent's* observations to reduce variance of value estimates; used only in training, discarded at inference. RL used a mix of TD(λ), V-trace, and UPGO (self-imitation).
  - **"Supervised auxiliary outputs":** the charter's phrasing. The well-supported claim is that SL initialization from human data was reported as critical and that the policy is continually KL-regularized toward the SL policy. The existence of distinct *auxiliary prediction heads* in AlphaStar (beyond z-conditioning and the baseline) is **model knowledge, UNVERIFIED** — I could not confirm a clean ablation isolating a named auxiliary head from primary text.
- **Relevance:** Two transferable patterns. (1) The opponent-observation value baseline is a *privileged-information value head* trick: during BC/PPO training we have full game state (all players' future, who actually wins), so the value/aux heads can consume or predict privileged info that the policy never sees at inference. Our next-elimination label is exactly such privileged future info. (2) z-conditioning shows human-strategy structure injected as a training signal helps; our elimination-order labels are a similar "structure from the replay" signal.

### OpenAI Five — Berner et al. 2019, "Dota 2 with Large Scale Deep RL" (arXiv 1912.06680)

- **Verification:** gradient-design claim VERIFIED against the primary text via the ar5iv HTML rendering, Appendix D: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1912.06680?fallback=original (the cdn.openai.com PDF did not extract directly). Remaining items (Team Spirit annealing, "auxiliary heads not necessary for gameplay") tagged below where not confirmed against primary text.
- **What it did (relevant):** Single 4096-unit LSTM trunk with multiple semantically-meaningful action heads, plus auxiliary prediction heads including **win-probability**, **net-worth rank**, and team-objective predictions. The win-probability head is value-style: ground truth (win/loss) exists only at game termination, the same sparse-terminal-label problem as our placement target. Introduced **Team Spirit**, a 0→1 annealed hyperparameter trading own-reward vs team-average reward (annealing schedule UNVERIFIED against primary text — from secondary sources).
- **Gradient design (VERIFIED, Appendix D):** the paper states "win probability passes gradients to the main LSTM and rest of the agent with a **very small weight**; the other auxiliary predictions use TensorFlow's **stop_gradient** method to train on their own." So among the auxiliary heads, exactly one (win-prob) is allowed to shape the shared trunk, and only weakly; the rest are insulated from the trunk by stop-gradient and learn on their own features. The paper frames the small-weight choice as partly "for historical reasons," so it is a pragmatic setting, not a derived optimum. Whether win-prob uses bootstrapped value-style targets vs. only the terminal label is not fully spelled out in the appendix text I read — stated cautiously: it is trained value-style against a sparse terminal outcome, bootstrapping mechanism UNVERIFIED.
- **Evidence it helped:** no clean ablation isolating the win-probability head's contribution to play strength; it is an *included-not-isolated* case for *task usefulness*. But the *gradient-design choice itself* (small-weight-to-trunk for one head, stop-grad for the others) is documented and load-bearing, even if not separately ablated.
- **Relevance:** This is the closest published analog to our setup — a head predicting a sparse terminal outcome (win/loss ≈ our placement) read off a shared trunk. Two directly transferable design decisions: (1) **let the sparse-terminal-outcome head shape the trunk only with a small gradient weight**, so it enriches features without dominating the policy gradient — a concrete starting recipe for our next-elimination head's λ; (2) **default other/experimental auxiliary heads to stop_gradient** and only open the trunk to a head once you trust it. For us: start the next-elimination head at a small trunk-gradient weight (mirroring win-prob), and treat opening it to the trunk as the deliberate, tested decision rather than the default.

### FTW / Capture-the-Flag — Jaderberg et al. (arXiv 2018, "Human-level performance in first-person multiplayer games…" 1807.01281; published Science 2019)

- **Verification:** abstract verified https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01281 ; full text https://science.sciencemag.org/content/364/6443/859.full and Berkeley PDF mirror https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~russell/classes/cs294/f21/papers/Jaderberg-etal-2019-Science-Capture-The-Flag.pdf (not fetched in full this pass).
- **What it did (from abstract):** Population-based training; a **temporally hierarchical RNN** operating at two timescales; **each agent learns its own internal reward signal** to densify the sparse win/loss reward.
- **Evidence:** the abstract asserts the hierarchical representation and learned internal reward matter; I did not extract the ablation tables this pass, so per-component deltas are UNVERIFIED.
- **Relevance:** "Learned internal/dense reward to complement a sparse delayed win signal" is conceptually identical to our motivation (placement is sparse and terminal; we want dense per-frame signal). FFA elimination is a multi-agent, partial-information setting like CTF.

### DeepNash — Perolat et al. 2022, Stratego (Science / arXiv 2206.15378)

- **Verification:** architecture verified via search excerpts of the paper https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15378 / PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.15378 ; consistent with the project's own memory note (DeepNash architecture spec lines ~1578–1694 of extracted txt).
- **What it did (architecture):** A **U-Net torso with residual blocks + skip connections**, and **four heads**, each a smaller replica of the torso plus final shaping layers: one scalar **value** head and **three policy** heads (deploy + two action-component distributions). Trained with R-NaD (Regularized Nash Dynamics), model-free.
- **Evidence on auxiliary tasks:** DeepNash does **not** use a separate dense auxiliary prediction head in the UNREAL sense — its heads are the value+policy of the game. So it is *not* evidence for aux heads; it is evidence for the multi-head-on-shared-U-Net-trunk *shape* we already use.
- **Relevance:** Strong architectural precedent — our trunk+heads design mirrors DeepNash almost exactly (U-Net torso, heads as small replicas of the torso). It confirms the shape is sound, but it gives *no* direct evidence that adding an extra auxiliary head helps; that case must rest on UNREAL/SPR/MTL evidence.

### Navigation auxiliary tasks — Mirowski et al. 2017, "Learning to Navigate in Complex Environments" (ICLR)

- **Verification:** verified against the ICLR/OpenReview PDF https://openreview.net/pdf?id=SJMGPrcle and arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.03673 (Mirowski, Pascanu, Viola, et al.).
- **What it did:** A3C navigation agent from raw RGB in 3D mazes, augmented with **two dense auxiliary heads on the shared trunk**: (1) **depth prediction** — predict the depth map (the paper found framing depth as a *prediction loss* sharing the policy representation more valuable than feeding depth as input), and (2) **loop-closure classification** — a binary classifier predicting whether the agent has returned to a recently-visited location. Trained by a weighted sum of A3C gradients plus depth gradients (weights β_d1, β_d2) and loop-closure gradients (weight β_l).
- **Evidence it helped (ablation):** the paper's central claim is that the auxiliary tasks **dramatically improve data efficiency and final task performance**, approaching human-level navigation even with frequently-changing goals, and it ablates the auxiliary tasks to show the lift (the depth-prediction auxiliary in particular drives the gain). This is a clean "aux task on shared trunk, ablated, helped" result with a classification/regression head rather than a control/policy head.
- **Relevance to our next-elimination head:** This is a *closer structural analog than Predictron/VPN* — a small supervised **classification** head (loop-closure is binary; our next-elimination is a per-player categorical) tapping a shared trunk and improving the main RL objective, with the lift confirmed by ablation. It also models the multi-aux-head weighting we'd use (separate β per head). Loop-closure's "has a discrete event happened?" framing is conceptually adjacent to "which player's elimination event is imminent."

### Predictron & Value Prediction Network

- **Verification:** Predictron (Silver et al. 2017) abstract verified https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.08810 ; VPN (Oh, Singh, Lee 2017) verified https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.03497 .
- **What they did:** Predictron — an abstract Markov-reward-process model rolled forward "imagined" steps, trained end-to-end so accumulated internal rewards/values approximate the true value; more accurate value prediction than plain deep nets on mazes and pool. VPN — integrates model-free + model-based by predicting future values/rewards from abstract states; beats DQN on several Atari games with short-lookahead planning. (MuZero later built on this lineage.)
- **Relevance:** These are *value-prediction-as-representation-shaper* methods. They support the general thesis that predicting value-relevant future quantities improves the learned representation. Our next-elimination head is a (discrete, structured) future-prediction target in the same family — predicting *who* leaves next is a proxy for *who* will win, which is exactly the "which remaining player wins" feature the current sparse value head fails to learn.

### Self-Predictive Representations (SPR) — Schwarzer et al. (arXiv 2020, 2007.05929; published ICLR 2021)

- **Verification:** verified via search excerpts of OpenReview/arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.05929 (full text not fetched directly; key ablation claim cross-checked across two excerpts).
- **What it did:** Auxiliary self-supervised loss predicting *future latent states* (k steps) via a transition model, with a momentum target encoder + projection/prediction heads (BYOL-style), plus data augmentation. On Atari 100k: median human-normalized ≈0.415, ~55% relative improvement over prior SOTA in the data-efficient regime.
- **Evidence (strong ablation):** Removing the **stop-gradient / target encoder** (i.e. generating targets from the online encoder without stop-grad) caused **catastrophic collapse** — the stop-grad is what prevents representational collapse. This is a clean, isolated ablation of a *design choice*, not just the task's presence.
- **Relevance:** Directly informs the stop-grad question (Area 2). If we ever make the aux target depend on the trunk's own output (e.g. bootstrapped/self-predictive variants), the SPR result is a warning: protect against collapse with stop-grad on the target side. Our next-elimination target is an external *label* (ground-truth from the replay), not a bootstrapped self-target, so the collapse risk is lower — but the lesson on gradient flow direction still applies if we tap intermediate features.

## Area 2 — Design patterns

### Aux loss weighting (fixed coefficients)
- **UNREAL** (verified, ar5iv): hand-set λ_RP=λ_VR=1.0; λ_PC tuned by log-uniform random search over an order-of-magnitude band. Takeaway: a single tuned coefficient per aux task, swept over a log range, is the baseline practice. Relevance: start the next-elimination head with one λ, swept log-uniform; don't over-engineer.
- **OpenAI Five** (VERIFIED, Appendix D — https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1912.06680?fallback=original): the win-probability head, which predicts a sparse terminal outcome (win/loss), passes gradients to the shared LSTM trunk with a *very small weight*. Mirowski (verified): one β per auxiliary head (β_d1, β_d2 for depth, β_l for loop-closure). Takeaway: a sparse-terminal-outcome head that is *allowed* to shape the trunk is given a deliberately small trunk-gradient weight — it enriches features without letting its sparse, low-variance signal dominate the policy/value gradient. Relevance: this is a concrete starting recipe for our next-elimination head — small trunk-gradient weight first, increase only if the trunk-shaping benefit shows up in the policy/value metric.

### Annealing / scheduling the weight
- **OpenAI Five Team Spirit** (annealing schedule UNVERIFIED against primary text): annealed 0→1 over training. **AlphaStar** KL-to-SL regularization is also effectively a scheduled anchor. Relevance: a natural design is to weight the aux head higher early (shape the trunk while the value head's sparse signal is weakest) and optionally decay it so it doesn't distort the trunk late in BC. Keep this as an *option to test*, not a foregone choice.

### Stop-gradient / gradient detaching
- **OpenAI Five** (VERIFIED, Appendix D): the cleanest precedent for *selective* trunk access — among its auxiliary heads, only win-probability passes gradients to the trunk (and weakly); the others (net-worth rank, team objectives) use `stop_gradient` and learn on their own features. Pattern: **default an auxiliary head to stop_gradient (it can't degrade the trunk), and open it to the trunk only deliberately and weakly.** Relevance: our next-elimination head's whole purpose is to shape the trunk, so it should *not* be stop-gradded — but this gives the safe fallback if it causes negative transfer (Area 3): cut its trunk gradient (stop_gradient) and let it ride as a pure read-off, or reopen it at a smaller weight.
- **SPR** (verified-excerpt): stop-grad on the *target encoder* is *load-bearing* — without it, representational collapse. General pattern: detach the *target*, let gradients flow only through the *predictor*. Relevance: distinct from the OpenAI Five case — SPR's stop-grad protects against collapse when the prediction target is *self-referential* (derived from the trunk). Our next-elimination target is an external replay label, so collapse risk is low; but if we ever make a head bootstrap off the trunk's own output, detach the target side.

### Where the head taps the trunk
- **DeepNash** (verified-excerpt): each head is a *small replica of the torso* tapping the shared U-Net output, then shaping layers. **UNREAL** pixel-control taps the conv-LSTM output with a deconv head producing a spatial map. Relevance: our next-elimination head should tap the same `[B, C, H, W]` spatial embedding as the existing heads; a per-player categorical can be produced by pooling/attending over per-player-owned tiles or by a small conv→pool→linear stack. Tapping the shared embedding (vs. a deeper private stack) is what actually pushes signal into the trunk.

### Shared vs separate trunk
- The entire premise (UNREAL, AlphaStar, DeepNash, OpenAI Five) is a *shared* trunk with cheap per-task heads; this is what makes the aux task enrich the primary representation. A separate trunk would defeat the purpose. The open risk is that sharing can *hurt* (Area 3). Relevance: keep the trunk shared (required for the goal); budget for the possibility that the aux gradient degrades the policy/value and have a balancing method ready.

## Area 3 — The failure side and multi-task balancing

### Negative transfer is real and task-pairing-dependent
- **Standley et al. 2020, "Which tasks should be learned together in multi-task learning?"** (ICML) — verified at abstract/citation level https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07553 (search-confirmed; not fetched in full). Finding: grouping tasks matters; some task pairs help, others cause negative transfer; they build task-affinity heuristics to decide groupings. Relevance: don't assume an extra head is free — it can degrade the shared trunk. Measure the policy/value metric with and without the aux head (a real ablation), not just the aux head's own accuracy.

### What makes an aux task useful (RL-specific, counterintuitive)
- **"What makes useful auxiliary tasks in RL: investigating the effect of the target policy"** (arXiv 2204.00565) — abstract verified https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.00565 . Finding: an aux task's target policy does **not** need to match the main policy to help; *most* policies (even uniformly random) improved over baseline, and greedy-policy aux tasks tended to help; surprisingly the main-task policy was *less* useful than some alternatives. Relevance: encouraging — our aux head needn't be perfectly aligned with the value objective to enrich the trunk. The mere presence of a structured, learnable per-frame target tends to help representation, lowering the bar for the next-elimination head to be net-positive.

### Representation dynamics — when aux tasks shape (or fail to shape) features
- **Lyle et al. 2021, "On the Effect of Auxiliary Tasks on Representation Dynamics"** (AISTATS; arXiv 2102.13089) — abstract/venue verified https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.13089 (full PDF did not extract this pass). Finding (from abstract): connects the *spectral decomposition of the transition operator* to the representations different aux tasks induce; uses this to *select* aux tasks for sparse-reward settings. Relevance: aux tasks that are predictable from the environment's transition structure shape useful features; an elimination-order prediction is tied to game dynamics, which is the kind of task this theory says should produce useful structure. (Tagged: mechanism understood at abstract level; detailed feature-rank claims UNVERIFIED here.)
- Related, not fully read this pass: "When does Self-Prediction help? Understanding Auxiliary Tasks in RL" (arXiv 2406.17718) and "Understanding and Preventing Capacity Loss in RL" (Lyle et al.) — both relevant follow-ups on *capacity loss* / when aux/representation objectives stop helping. Flagged for deeper reading if the aux head underperforms.

### Standard multi-task balancing methods (the toolbox if a fixed λ fails)
- **Uncertainty weighting — Kendall, Gal, Cipolla 2018, "Multi-Task Learning Using Uncertainty to Weigh Losses"** (CVPR) — verified https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/html/Kendall_Multi-Task_Learning_Using_CVPR_2018_paper.html . Weights each task loss by its learned homoscedastic uncertainty (a per-task learned σ), so tasks with different scales/units self-balance instead of hand-tuned weights. Relevance: a principled drop-in if our value/policy/aux losses are on very different scales — learn the relative weight rather than sweeping it.
- **GradNorm — Chen et al. 2018** (ICML) — verified https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.02257 . Dynamically tunes per-task gradient *magnitudes* to equalize training rates, with a single asymmetry hyperparameter α; matches/beats grid search. Relevance: if the aux head's gradient dominates or is dominated, GradNorm rebalances training speed.
- **PCGrad / Gradient Surgery — Yu et al. 2020** (NeurIPS) — verified https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/file/3fe78a8acf5fda99de95303940a2420c-Paper.pdf and https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.06782 . Detects *conflicting* gradients (negative cosine similarity between task gradients) and projects one onto the normal plane of the other, removing the interfering component. Improves multi-task classification, scene understanding, and RL. Relevance: this is the direct remedy if the next-elimination gradient *conflicts* with the policy/value gradient at the trunk — a cheap, model-agnostic guard against negative transfer. A quick diagnostic before adopting it: log the cosine similarity between the aux-head trunk gradient and the policy/value trunk gradient during BC; persistent negative cosine = real conflict = candidate for PCGrad.

## Synthesis for our design

1. **Conceptual framing:** the next-elimination head is cleanest cast as a **GVF / predictive question** (Horde, Sutton 2011, verified) — predict the elimination event per frame, off-policy from replays. This makes the per-frame/off-policy nature explicit and exposes the natural denser variant ("how soon is the next elimination") if the categorical is too sparse.
2. The strongest *ablated* evidence that a dense aux head on a shared trunk helps the main RL objective is **UNREAL** (verified). **Mirowski 2017** (verified) is the closest *classification-head* analog with a clean ablation — depth + loop-closure heads on a shared A3C trunk, ablated, improved navigation. SPR (verified-excerpt) adds that *gradient-flow design* (stop-grad on targets) is load-bearing when the target is self-referential — low risk for us since our target is an external label.
3. **Most directly applicable design recipe — OpenAI Five (VERIFIED, Appendix D):** a head predicting a *sparse terminal outcome* (win/loss ≈ our placement) shapes the shared trunk with a *very small gradient weight*, while other auxiliary heads use `stop_gradient`. Start the next-elimination head at a small trunk-gradient weight; keep stop_gradient as the safe fallback if it causes negative transfer. AlphaStar's "auxiliary outputs" are *included-not-isolated* — precedent, not proof of task usefulness.
4. DeepNash validates our *architecture shape* (U-Net torso + small head replicas) but is not evidence for aux heads.
5. Negative transfer is real; the mitigation toolbox (uncertainty weighting, GradNorm, PCGrad) is mature. Plan the experiment as a *real ablation* (main-metric with/without aux) plus a gradient-conflict diagnostic (cosine between aux and policy/value trunk gradients), not just "did the aux head learn its task."
6. Encouraging RL-specific result (2204.00565, verified): aux tasks needn't be tightly aligned with the main policy to help — lowers the bar for next-elimination to be net-positive.
