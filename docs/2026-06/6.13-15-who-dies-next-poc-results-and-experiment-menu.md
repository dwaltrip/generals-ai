# who-dies-next PoC — results, baseline analysis, and the experiment menu

**Date:** 2026-06-13

**Description:** First results from the who-dies-next aux-head PoC sweep (the `next_death` variant built in [`6.13-14`](6.13-14-who-dies-next-poc-build-and-cut-corners.md), per the reframe in [`6.13-12`](6.13-12-who-dies-next-target-reframe.md) and plan in [`6.13-13`](6.13-13-who-dies-next-poc-plan.md)). Covers the model-free baseline analysis that was deferred from the build, the key insights from the discussion that followed, and a calibrated menu of next experiments. Read [`6.13-13`](6.13-13-who-dies-next-poc-plan.md) for the green-light criterion this is graded against.

## Bottom line

The head, as run, **does not clear the green light.** The criterion from [`6.13-13`](6.13-13-who-dies-next-poc-plan.md) was "clearly beats both heuristic baselines." Measured against the lowest-total-army rule (47.1% top-1 next-victim accuracy), the head reaches 41–46% — at or slightly below it on aggregate, and clearly below it in the imminent regime (deaths <10 ticks away: head ~38–42% vs rule 47.8%) where the prediction matters most. The head learns an approximation of "lowest army dies next" and adds nothing beyond it; where it deviates from that rule, it is wrong more often than the rule.

Two things are nonetheless genuine positives: the reframe delivered **legibility** (a clean, decisive read, where the time-bin head gave only "beats its soft floor by 0.02 nats"), and **do-no-harm holds** (the head doesn't degrade policy/value). The negative is a legible one, which is what the reframe was for.

This is single-seed evidence, but the conclusion is robust to that: the head trails the rule in all 4 cells and both checkpoints read, and the imminent-regime gap (~6–10 points) is larger and equally consistent.

## The sweep

`data/training/sweeps/2026-06-13-elim-head-who-dies-poc` (gitignored; this doc is the durable record). A 2×2: `lambda_elim ∈ {0.15, 0.3}` × `lambda_value ∈ {0.1, 0.3}`, all cells sub_5k / 5 epochs / seed 0 / n20, on the τ=0.6 pyramid value-head base. Head fixed to `next_death`: `Conv2d(C→8) → masked lse pool → [B,8]`, single cross-player softmax over alive players, hard CE, pool=lse, no pre-pool capacity.

## What we measured

The in-loop pass surfaces only the `next_elim` CE; everything below is computed offline from the dumped raw columns (`next_elim_probs/target/alive_mask/dt`) joined to model-free baselines. (The elim tables in `stratified_val_loss_analysis report` are the **old time-bin** head and do not read `next_death` — don't rely on them for this variant.)

**Top-1 next-victim accuracy (best cell `elim0.3-v0.1`, ep5; pattern holds across cells):**

| predictor | aggregate | imminent (dt<10) |
|---|---|---|
| lowest total army | **47.1%** | **47.8%** |
| army-delta (best window, K=25) | 44.6% | 42.2% |
| **head** | 45.7% | 41.4% |
| uniform over alive players | 28.6% | — |

- The head beats *uniform* (28.6%) but loses to the *lowest-army rule* (47.1%). Uniform is the wrong bar; the heuristic rules are the bar, because they're trivially available and the head has the inputs to compute them.
- On the ~54% of frames where head and lowest-army pick different victims, lowest-army is right more often (≈29–33% vs ≈24–27%): the head's deviations from the rule are net wrong.
- The head edges the rule only at dt≥50 — and only in this best cell — the far-horizon, few-players-alive endgame, by small margins (0.7–1.7 pts). Accuracy is high there because there are only 2–3 candidates (a base-rate effect), not because the head is skillful. So the aggregate near-tie is buoyed by an easy, low-value regime; in the contested imminent regime the rule wins clearly.

**Memorization / overfit signature (val `next_elim` CE):** train CE falls steadily in every cell (from ~1.0 down to ~0.70–0.91 by ep5, lower at higher λ_elim) while val CE stalls at ~1.5–1.7 and is non-monotone (best at ep2–3, then rises). The head's val CE is also **worse than the uniform-over-alive baseline** (1.38) despite better argmax — it ranks better than chance but is overconfident/miscalibrated on held-out frames.

**λ_elim is not a lever here (measured):** λ_elim 0.3 drives train CE lower than 0.15 but best val CE is no better (and the λ_elim 0.3 cells are noisier). More elim pressure buys training fit, not generalization, at this data scale.

**Do-no-harm holds:** value CE sits below its floor in every cell (best Δ −0.09 to −0.13 vs floor 1.406); policy top-1 71–72%, normal. No elim-OFF arm in this sweep, but the readout sweep ([`6.13-11`](6.13-11-elim-readout-sweep-results.md)) established do-no-harm at λ0.15, and the value/policy metrics here are unremarkable.

**Stage-0 corpus facts (val split):** victim-channel marginal is nearly flat (10–16% per channel; no positional/slot shortcut to memorize). Time-to-next-death is short-tailed: median 55 ticks, 11% of frames have a death <10 ticks away, 47% <50, 85% <200. Combined-oracle ceiling (lowest-army OR army-delta, per-frame best) ≈ **56–58%**.

## Key insights from the discussion

**Update (6/18):** insight #1 (failure is upstream of the head) confirmed — argmin over clean army is learnable, so ranking is not the wall; the locus is representation / input-fidelity. [6.18-9](6.18-9-elim-head-synthesis-and-direction.md) §2.1.

**1. The head's failure is not a head-capacity problem — it's upstream.** The head computes, per player `p`, `logit_p = pool(filter_p · trunk)`. If the trunk output carries each player's total army (the obs broadcasts `opp_N_army_count`, fog-independent, even for fully-fogged opponents), then a *single linear filter* with a negative weight gives `logit_p ≈ −army_p`, and the softmax recovers the lowest-army rule. So the architecture can already express the 47% baseline with a linear readout — no extra capacity needed. Its failure to even match the rule therefore points upstream of the head: either the trunk output doesn't present per-player army in a readable form (under-carved), or there aren't enough examples to fit it at 5k (data), or the gradient regime starved it. This argues against "add head capacity" as a fix and lines up with [`6.13-11`](6.13-11-elim-readout-sweep-results.md) (capacity helped the easy signal, not the hard one).

**2. Where it fails, in design terms.** The head trails the rule specifically in the **imminent, contested** regime — exactly where prediction needs *local combat dynamics* (who's losing a fight now), not just the army leaderboard. The three predictive signals split: level (lowest army) — the head can see it but only matches it (under-extraction); rate (army-delta) — the head cannot see army-rate at all, only a land-rate proxy (input gap, below); local vision (losing a fight) — the genuinely hard residual.

**3. The `opp_N_army_delta` input gap is real but is a complement, not the headline lever (recalibrated).** Verified in code this session: the obs has `opp_N_land_delta` (a land *rate*) but **no** `opp_N_army_delta`; the `opp_N_army_count` scoreboard scalar is current-frame-only (not stacked in dense history); the dense-history tail carries only spatial maps (`ownership_transition`, a fog-limited `army_delta`). So the model **cannot self-derive** opponent army-rate — an explicit `opp_N_army_delta` scalar would be genuinely new information matching the ground-truth rate baseline. *But* the idea originated in [`6.13-13`](6.13-13-who-dies-next-poc-plan.md)/[`6.13-14`](6.13-14-who-dies-next-poc-build-and-cut-corners.md) gated on "Stage-0 shows rate is the stronger predictor," and Stage-0 says the opposite: level (47.1%) > rate (44.6%). The remaining case is **complementarity** (level OR rate ≈ 58% > level's 47%), which is real but weaker, with realized gain ≤ the oracle, and partially overlapping the land-rate proxy the model already has. So this is a reasonable cheap experiment, not a first move.

**4. The deeper question to sit with — HYPOTHESIS, not established.** With lowest-army at 47%, the ensemble ceiling ~58%, and army levels already in the obs (and likely encoded in the trunk, since the policy needs them), who-dies-next may be largely *"read the army leaderboard."* If so, the aux head's *unique* contribution to the trunk is only the residual beyond army-level features — the localized "who's losing a fight" signal, which is exactly the hard imminent part the head fails at. This would mean tuning the head toward the leaderboard signal makes a better *predictor* without necessarily *enriching the trunk* (the 24k-transfer bet). This is a hypothesis the elim-OFF trunk probe (below) tests directly. It connects to the Wave A meta-pattern ([`6.12-4`](6.12-4-wave-a-results-and-synthesis.md)): counting (army → who's weak) is learnable; ranking via local dynamics is the wall.

**5. Methodological lesson worth keeping.** Lift over a *uniform* baseline can look encouraging while saying little — uniform is the wrong bar. The head must beat the trivially-available heuristic rule it has the inputs for. The heuristic baselines (now scripted) are the gate, computed before calling any head "promising." Echoes the legibility theme of [`6.13-12`](6.13-12-who-dies-next-target-reframe.md).

## The experiment menu

The central uncertainty: **is the bottleneck the head readout, the trunk features, the data scale, or the target itself?** The head can express the rule linearly (insight 1), so "more head capacity" is not on this list. Diagnostics that localize the bottleneck come first because they're cheap and they tell us which fix to spend on.

### Tier 1 — Diagnostics (cheap; do before spending on a fix)

**1a. Frozen-trunk probe suite.** *(Leading candidate; design detail in the next section.)*
- **Why:** the head can represent lowest-army linearly but doesn't reach it, so the failure is upstream of the head. We need to know *where* upstream. The naive "can a probe read it" is confounded — the U-Net trunk likely preserves near-raw board info, so a strong-enough probe can recompute lowest-army regardless of whether the trunk learned it ([`6.13-11`](6.13-11-elim-readout-sweep-results.md) flagged this ambiguity). The design below defuses that with a raw-obs anchor.
- **What we'd learn:** separates (i) gradient-starvation (the deployed-shape head, trained hard on the frozen trunk, suddenly beats 47% → it was crowded out by policy/value gradients, fix is λ/training), (ii) readout-entanglement (only a fat probe reads it → richer readout warranted), (iii) data/target ceiling (trunk-probe ≈ raw-obs-probe ≈ 47% → no readout or trunk change helps; it's data or target). A separate probe of an **elim-OFF trunk** tests insight 4 — if policy+value alone already linearly carries lowest-army, the aux head is redundant on the level signal.
- **Cost:** cheap. Frozen trunk = fast; reuses `scripts/value_head_probe.py` (already a "frozen trunk + swap-in head" harness).
- **Calibration:** a frozen probe of *this* trunk can't alone separate "data lacks it" from "λ never carved it" — that's why the raw-obs anchor is load-bearing, not optional.

**1b. Predictability-conditioned evaluation.** Stratify the head-vs-baseline comparison by vision/contact and lopsidedness, not just by time-to-death (which we already have).
- **Why:** [`6.13-12`](6.13-12-who-dies-next-target-reframe.md) predicted the head might do well on a predictable subset, diluted in the aggregate. We can check whether the head beats the rule on *any* identifiable subset (e.g. frames with vision/contact on the dying player).
- **What we'd learn:** whether the head adds value in a specific regime, or trails the rule everywhere. If it never beats the rule on any natural slice, that's strong evidence it learned only the leaderboard.
- **Cost:** cheap; offline, extend the baseline script (would need contact/vision columns added to the join — they're in the obs/sim).

### Tier 2 — Inputs and readout (cheap-ish; matched to specific probe outcomes)

**2a. Add `opp_N_army_delta` input channels (7 broadcast scalars).**
- **Why:** confirmed real input gap (insight 3); ensemble ceiling says rate carries up to ~11 points of orthogonal signal over level.
- **What we'd learn:** whether the army-rate signal lifts the head toward the ~58% ceiling.
- **Cost:** low (compute Δ of the scoreboard army total from existing sim; add channels; retrain). Best folded into a larger run, not run alone.
- **Calibration:** *not* gated in by Stage-0 (level beats rate individually); a complement, partly overlapping the existing land-rate channel; does **not** address the level under-extraction, which is the primary failure.

**2b. Mean+lse pooled readout.** Give the head both a mean-pool (exact for the broadcast level signal) and the lse-pool (the localized-danger peak), concatenated into the logits.
- **Why:** who-dies-next mixes a global-level signal (lowest army → mean-exact) and a local-danger signal (under attack → lse-peak); a single lse pool may distort the level read.
- **What we'd learn:** whether the level/local mix is being lost in the single pool.
- **Cost:** low.
- **Calibration:** the representational argument (insight 1) says a linear readout works under either pool, so this is second-order — better run *after* the probe says the bottleneck is the readout, not before.

**2c. Relational / pointer-attention readout.** Cross-player interaction before pooling (AlphaStar pointer / JueWu target-attention, [`6.13-3`](6.13-3-aux-heads-applied-per-entity-hidden-info.md)/[`6.13-4`](6.13-4-aux-head-literature-synthesis.md) §2).
- **Why:** the lit pre-registered this as the upgrade path "if conv-pool underperforms on contested multi-player spots" — which is exactly where the head fails. Matched fix *if* the probe shows "signal present but entangled (only a fat probe reads it)."
- **What we'd learn:** whether modeling who-threatens-whom captures the contested-spot signal the per-channel conv-pool misses.
- **Cost:** higher (new architecture). Tier-2/3, gated on probe result 1a-(ii).

### Tier 3 — Scale and target

**3a. Larger-manifest run (10k, maybe larger).** Re-run with more data (with army-delta inputs included so the test is fair).
- **Why:** train CE falls / val stalls is the classic small-data overfit; the arc's standing question has been sub_5k-vs-scale; the head can't reach the level rule at 5k, and the ~58% ceiling says more signal exists to be learned.
- **What we'd learn:** does more data let the head reach and beat lowest-army? Yes → target good, sub_5k was the wall. Still below → the head adds nothing beyond the leaderboard, which undercuts the 24k-transfer rationale.
- **Cost:** a cloud training run (moderate).
- **Calibration:** the Tier-1 raw-obs probe at 5k partly previews this for near-zero cost (it bounds the learnable ceiling at 5k); the full run additionally tests whether the trunk+head jointly learn it under the real multi-task objective.

**3b. Back-pocket target variants — doom-binary, ordinal-CDF.** ([`6.13-12`](6.13-12-who-dies-next-target-reframe.md) §back-pocket.)
- **Why:** if the head can't beat the rule even with data + inputs + readout, the categorical who-dies-next target may itself be the limit. doom-binary keeps per-player density and is memorization-safe; ordinal-CDF keeps density *and* the full ordering.
- **What we'd learn:** whether a different target shape extracts more of the predictable signal.
- **Cost:** moderate (new target extraction + head). Tier-3, gated on Tier-1/2 failing.

### Tier 4 — Secondary / calibration

**4a. Label smoothing / early-stop.** The planned memorization mitigation ([`6.13-13`](6.13-13-who-dies-next-poc-plan.md)), unused so far.
- **Why:** val CE > uniform (overconfident); best val at ep2–3 then overfits.
- **What we'd learn / calibration:** addresses the calibration *symptom* (CE, confidence) but **not** the under-extraction (top-1 below the rule). Worth folding into another run; not a headline fix on its own.

### Explicitly deferred

**24k trunk-transfer run** — the actual bet (does the head enrich the trunk for the value head). Gated on a promising Stage-1 read, which we do not have. Not justified now; the elim-OFF trunk probe (1a) is the cheap way to inform whether it's worth running at all.

## The frozen-trunk probe — design detail (for 1a)

Three probes, equal training budget, on the frozen trunk output `[B,C,H,W]`:

1. **Deployed-shape head (`Conv→lse-pool→8`), trained hard.** Beats 47% → the real-run bottleneck was gradient-competition/λ/under-training. Still stuck → go to probe 2.
2. **Linear-pool vs. fat (multi-conv) probe.** Linear works → signal is pool-readable (deployed failure is regime). Only fat works → signal is entangled → richer readout (2c). Neither → trunk doesn't carry decodable signal at this scale.
3. **The control — same probes on the raw obs (skip the trunk).** Gives the learnable ceiling at 5k for that probe class. If fat-probe-on-raw-obs also caps ~47–50% → ~47% is the data/target ceiling, not the trunk or head. If raw-obs reaches ~58% but trunk lags → the trunk discards signal (a real finding).

Plus two sharp add-ons: a **linear probe for each player's total-army scalar** (does the trunk preserve the broadcast army channel at all), and the **elim-OFF trunk** probe (redundancy with the value head — insight 4).

Minimal conclusive first cut: **probe 1 + probe 3 at linear and fat capacity.** That trio separates gradient-starvation, readout-entanglement, and data-ceiling in one pass.
