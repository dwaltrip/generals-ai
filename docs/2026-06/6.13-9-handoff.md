**Where we landed:** the elim-head weighting sweep concluded that class weighting is a *prior knob, not a discrimination lever* — it can't lift imminent-bin precision. bin 1 `[10,20)` stays at ~chance precision even when over-predicted 15×; bin 0 `[0,10)` has real signal (8.3× base-rate precision, intrinsic) but weighting *degrades* it. So weighting is not the lever.

**The hypothesis driving this session (#2 in 6.13-8):** the imminent signal may be *in the trunk but washed out by the readout*. The head is `Conv2d(C→8·n_bins)` + masked **global-average-pool**; averaging dilutes spatially-localized evidence. The bin-0-survives / bin-1-washes pattern fits this — globally-apparent doom (bin 0) survives averaging, a local vulnerability (bin 1) doesn't.

**Goal:** discuss, then possibly implement, a **readout / head-structure tweak**. Specifics are open — candidates include footprint pooling (already pre-registered), max/attention pooling, or a richer (nonlinear) head. Pick the design in discussion against the lit; don't lock it in cold.

**Caveats:** flat imminent precision is also consistent with data-scale (#1), under-trained trunk (#3), and head-capacity (#4) — a tweak primarily tests #2/#4, not #1/#3.
