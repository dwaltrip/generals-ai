# Aux-head literature synthesis — next-elimination head

**Date:** 2026-06-13

Synthesis of a two-part literature review run to inform the first auxiliary head we're designing: a per-frame **next-elimination** head (per-player categorical — which currently-alive player is eliminated next — read off the shared U-Net trunk). The review followed the aux-head direction set in [`6.09-1`](6.09-1-value-head-direction-and-head-metrics.md), the Wave A conclusion that within-bucket value signal is signal-starved at sub_5k ([`6.12-4`](6.12-4-wave-a-results-and-synthesis.md)), and extends the value-target pointers in [`6.10-2`](6.10-2-value-target-literature-pointers.md).

Full reports (per-finding detail + per-claim verification tags) live alongside this doc:
- [`6.13-2-aux-heads-fundamentals.md`](6.13-2-aux-heads-fundamentals.md) — when aux prediction heads on a shared trunk help vs. backfire; weighting, stop-grad, balancing.
- [`6.13-3-aux-heads-applied-per-entity-hidden-info.md`](6.13-3-aux-heads-applied-per-entity-hidden-info.md) — per-player outcome prediction, per-entity head architectures, hidden-information games.

Both reports were web-grounded and each passed an independent adversarial citation audit; claims are tagged "verified against primary/secondary source" or "UNVERIFIED" in the reports. Confidence calibration below inherits those tags.

## Headline: the head has direct precedent, and the literature is encouraging

Three findings, taken together, say this head is a reasonable bet rather than a shot in the dark:

- **The exact shape exists in deployed systems.** OpenAI Five's **net-worth-rank** head predicts, verbatim, "which rank among the team (1–5) in terms of total resources collected will this hero be at the end of the game" — a per-entity end-of-game *ordering* prediction, trained value-style off the shared trunk, and actually consumed by the bot's item-buying logic (primary-verified). ToMnet's per-object "will this be consumed before episode end" head is the same structure — a per-entity future-discrete-event prediction off a shared torso, learned from pure observation (verified). Our "which alive player dies next" is that shape with objects→players, consumption→elimination.
- **Predicting other agents as an aux task measurably helps the shared trunk.** Agent-Modeling-as-Auxiliary (Hernandez-Leal 2019, verified) adds an opponent-action-prediction head sharing the conv trunk and beats vanilla A3C — the precise enrichment mechanism we're hypothesizing.
- **Aux tasks needn't be tightly aligned with the main objective to help** (arXiv 2204.00565, verified abstract): most auxiliary target policies — even random ones — improved representation over baseline. This lowers the bar for next-elimination to be net-positive even if its signal is partly shallow.

The cleanest *conceptual* framing is a **General Value Function** (Horde, Sutton 2011, verified): the cumulant is the elimination event, termination is the moment that player dies, and the prediction is read off the shared representation each frame. UNREAL — the strongest *ablated* evidence that a dense aux head on a shared trunk lifts the main RL objective — is explicitly a set of GVF-style aux tasks. Mirowski 2017 (verified) is the closest *classification-head* analog with a clean ablation (depth + loop-closure heads on a shared A3C trunk improved navigation).

## What the lit changes about the design decisions we had open

### Gradient flow and loss weight (new, and the most useful import)

The most directly applicable recipe is **OpenAI Five's** (primary-verified, Appendix D): a head predicting a sparse terminal outcome (win/loss — analogous to our placement) shapes the shared trunk with a **very small gradient weight**, while the *other* auxiliary heads use `stop_gradient` and learn on their own features.

Two concrete imports:
- **Start the next-elimination head's weight `ν` small** — its job is to enrich the trunk without its signal dominating the policy/value gradient. This sharpens the "start `ν` modest" instinct from our discussion into a recipe with precedent, and it dovetails with the Wave A damage law (gradient *magnitude* into the trunk is the lever).
- **`stop_gradient` is the safe fallback, not the default.** Our head's whole purpose is to shape the trunk, so it should *not* be detached. But if it causes negative transfer, the documented fallback is to cut its trunk gradient (let it ride as a pure read-off) or reopen it weaker — rather than abandoning it.

Annealing the aux weight (high early while the value head's sparse signal is weakest, decayed later) is an *option to test*, not a foregone choice — OpenAI Five's Team Spirit anneals 0→1, though that schedule is UNVERIFIED against primary text. SPR's stop-grad-prevents-collapse result (verified excerpt) is a warning only if we ever make a head's target *self-referential* (bootstrapped off the trunk); our target is an external replay label, so collapse risk is low.

### Head shape — conv-to-8-channels + masked pool is the lit-grounded choice

Our leading candidate (`Conv2d(C→8)` then masked spatial pool, one channel per player) maps onto the **relational-RL output reduction** (Zambaldi 2018, verified): reduce a spatial tensor to a per-feature vector by pooling over space. That spatial→per-feature pooling is the literature-backed part. This favors conv-to-channels over the GAP+linear fallback.

Two refinements I raised in discussion are **hypotheses, not transfers** (the audit caught both stated too firmly):
- **Coordinate channels** — Zambaldi appends x,y coords so permutation-invariant *attention* can recover position; a plain conv already encodes position via its receptive field, so this does *not* transfer as a literature-backed move. *Hypothesis:* explicit coord channels may still help if absolute geometry matters (corner/edge generals). Testable, not established.
- **Pooling choice** (masked-mean / soft-attention over a player's footprint vs. global pool) — motivated by our own guess that elimination evidence localizes near a player's general. A guess to A/B, not a finding.

Heavier per-entity readouts exist as the **upgrade path** if the plain conv-pool head underperforms on contested multi-player spots: AlphaStar's entity-encoder + **pointer network** (verified-secondary) and JueWu's **target attention** (LSTM query vs. per-unit keys; verified) — the latter directly usable with the 8 players' general-tiles as keys. Our masked softmax over alive players is the small-scale analog of a pointer's masked entity softmax. For 8 fixed slots the lighter conv-pool is the right v1; attention earns its complexity only if entity counts vary.

**Per-player output with a masked-alive softmax is standard:** Diplomacy uses a per-power value (softmax over the 7 players) and per-power policies (verified-secondary); OpenAI Five's per-hero rank is the same family.

### Categorical vs. time-to-elimination — a lit-backed A/B for the density question

The open fork from discussion (identity-only, piecewise-constant between eliminations, vs. a per-frame-varying timing companion) now has a template. **Terminal Prediction** (Kartal 2019, verified) is a dense per-frame "temporal closeness to terminal" regression (scalar in [0,1], interpolated targets, free from episode timing) that beat vanilla A3C on Pommerman. A **time-to-elimination regression per alive player** (fraction of game until they die) is the direct sibling of our categorical head, on the same labels. *Hypothesis (ours, not from the paper):* the regression form gives a denser/smoother gradient than the categorical — plausible but unproven (the paper compares aux-vs-no-aux, not regression-vs-categorical). Worth an explicit A/B; not assertable as a finding.

### Class imbalance — a concrete handle

UNREAL's reward-prediction head (verified) uses **class-balanced resampling** of minibatches to fight reward sparsity without biasing the policy. Our next-elimination labels are imbalanced (most frames are far from any elimination; the identity distribution skews toward weak players), so balancing/reweighting the aux minibatch is a directly transferable knob — and it connects to the value head's stratification work, where the bad-outcome buckets are thin by construction.

### Measurement discipline — beyond "did the head learn its task"

Negative transfer is real and task-pairing-dependent (Standley 2020, verified-abstract). The reports converge on a measurement plan stronger than checking the head's own accuracy:
- **A real ablation** — the policy/value metric *with vs. without* the aux head (the success criterion stays the value head's within-bucket stratified reads, per our earlier framing).
- **A gradient-conflict diagnostic** — log the cosine similarity between the aux-head trunk gradient and the policy/value trunk gradient during BC; persistent negative cosine = real conflict.
- **The balancing toolbox if a fixed `ν` fails** is mature and model-agnostic: uncertainty weighting (Kendall 2018), GradNorm, PCGrad/gradient-surgery (all verified). PCGrad is the direct remedy for gradient conflict; hold it in reserve rather than adopting preemptively.

## Adjacent and future directions surfaced

- **A second, complementary aux head: generative defogging.** DefogGAN (verified abstract) and the Synnaeve StarCraft Defogger (verified PDF) predict the *hidden state itself* (per-cell enemy ownership/army/positions) as output — distinct from our outcome-over-players head. For our fog of war, a conv encoder-decoder head predicting fogged enemy ownership / army / general-location is a candidate companion aux (the Synnaeve-style regression is the light starting point; the GAN machinery is likely overkill). *Hypothesis (ours):* it would specifically aid the elimination head by enriching opponent-state features — plausible but unproven; A/B independently. This is the inference-time-interpretable "general-location belief" head from our earlier discussion, now with precedent.
- **DeepNash is the architectural twin** (verified-secondary): a shared U-Net trunk feeding a value head + spatial heads on an imperfect-information game, with hidden state encoded as **public-belief input channels** (the distribution of what each hidden piece could be) plus stacked recent-move frames. It both validates adding another head to the U-Net torso and suggests how to feed fog-aware belief *inputs* — most of which our obs already does (last-seen ownership, time-since-seen, dense-history frames).
- **Suphx oracle guiding** (verified): train a teacher that sees hidden state, then anneal its privileged features to zero. A Phase-2 option; our BC setting already has full replay info at train time, so a privileged-teacher head is feasible if the fog net struggles. Mirrors AlphaStar's opponent-observation value baseline (training-only privileged info).

## Target extraction — confirmed cheap

Verified in the data layer this session (read-only): the full-field elimination schedule is already present, so no replay-parser work is needed.
- `sim["death_events"]` is `(timestep, slot)` for every eliminated player (winner absent) — `dataset.py` already uses it for `players_alive`. The next-eliminated player at frame `t` is the row with the smallest `timestep > t`; map its raw slot → canonical channel. The per-channel **alive mask** (for masking dead players' logits) falls out of the same array.
- `sim["capture_events"]` is `(t, captor, captured)` — the same events with the captor column, so the "by whom" variant ([`6.09-1`](6.09-1-value-head-direction-and-head-metrics.md)'s captor sibling) is also free.

Target extraction is a pure function of already-loaded sim arrays, computable in `encode_frame` (or precomputed per-game like the scoreboard).

## Open decisions carried forward

The lit narrowed some forks and left others genuinely open:

- **Head shape:** conv-to-8 + masked pool for v1 (lit-grounded); pool type (global vs. masked-mean/attention) is an A/B (hypothesis); coord channels an optional A/B (hypothesis). Pointer/target-attention is the upgrade path, not v1.
- **Target form:** categorical who-dies-next vs. time-to-elimination regression — an A/B, not yet decided. Could also run both as two cheap heads.
- **Gradient/weight:** small `ν` to start (OpenAI Five recipe); stop-grad as fallback; annealing as an option to test.
- **Staging:** the sub_5k-sanity → 24k-transfer staging from our discussion is unchanged; the value head's within-bucket stratified reads remain the transfer success criterion, and within-bucket signal is only expected to appear at 24k+.
- **Sequencing of the second head:** defogging is the strongest candidate companion, but it's a separate target-extraction and architecture effort — decide after the next-elimination head's first results.
