# Value-head synthesis: where we stand after the lever runs

Date: 2026-06-10

Interpretation of the 6.10 lever-run results and the analyses around them. All numbers and reproduction commands live in [`6.10-4`](6.10-4-value-head-levers-results.md); the failure-mode background is [`6.10-1`](6.10-1-value-head-two-failure-modes.md); the framing this work executes against is [`6.09-1`](6.09-1-value-head-direction-and-head-metrics.md). Session summary: [`6.10-6`](6.10-6-session-summary.md).

Standing caveat over everything here: one run per configuration, seed noise unquantified. Where a claim rests on a ~2σ read or a two-point pattern, that is said inline.

## The short version

The value head's recurring overfitting was not harmless — it was taxing the policy through the shared trunk, and that tax is what made dense history look bad in the 06-05 sweep. The two levers landed in 6.10-3 (soft ordinal targets τ=0.6, head-side dropout) substantially fix the harm: with value memorization tamed, n20 policy fully recovers, and the full-24k lever run posts the best policy numbers of any run to date. The head itself graduates from "broken in one of two ways" to "honest but shallow": these are the first runs to demonstrably beat the placement marginal at all, the head robustly learns survivor-counting, it holds a small real late-game signal at its val optimum (the ~2σ tier of the evidence), and it loses most of that signal if training continues past the optimum. Against 6.09-1's BC-phase bar ("do no harm, not be accurate"), do-no-harm now looks achieved; accuracy remains near floor; and the open frontier is whether the *trunk* retains what the head's readout loses.

## The three-state map

Every observed value head lands in one of three states, and we now know which knobs move it between them:

1. **Collapse to the prior** — constant output ≈ the placement marginal. Where: every n5 run, all four lever combinations. Signature: value metrics identical across different runs to ~1e-4, because a constant solution's CE is run-independent (the τ cells collapse to the *softened* prior, ~0.009 higher in hard CE). No lever tried so far escapes collapse at n5. High confidence: the 06-05 n5 head was directly shown constant (prediction-entropy std ≈ 0); the new n5 cells match it through the CE-identity signature, with direct confirmation not yet run.
2. **Fingerprint memorization** — train CE dives within epoch 1, val climbs above floor, gap > 1. Where: n20 under hard CE targets. Dropout alone does not prevent it (gap 1.22, best epoch 1). High confidence.
3. **Tamed** — at/below the global floor with state-dependent predictions. Where: n20 + τ, with dropout stacking a further improvement (sub_5k Δbest −0.049 → −0.143; gap 0.81 → 0.68); reproduced at full 24k (−0.129). The stratified decomposition of what "tamed" contains is below.

The asymmetry between states 1 and 3 is itself informative: τ unlocked n20 but not n5. The reading we hold (hypothesis): the dense-history channels supply the features capable of beating the marginal — under hard CE those features get spent on trajectory fingerprints; under τ they get spent at least partly on real signal. At n5 the head apparently has no features that beat the marginal, so it stays in the collapse basin regardless of target shaping.

## The causal chain, with each link now tested

**Memorizing head → corrupted trunk → worse policy.** This was 6.10-1's leading hypothesis; it now has three independent confirmations:

- The preregistered λ=0 discriminator landed on the corruption branch of its fork: val_policy 0.9615 at n20 (prediction: ≈0.97 corruption, ≈1.04 channels).
- The τ cells at n20 recover policy to the λ=0 level (0.9646–0.9681) *while still training the head* — taming the memorization buys nearly the whole recovery, no gradient amputation needed.
- At full 24k, the lever run beats the no-lever baseline at every matched epoch (ep4: 0.8470 vs 0.8691) en route to the best policy of any run to date (0.8347 / 74.9% top-1).

Corollary: **dense history does not hurt the policy** — the 06-05 sweep's n-ordering was the value gradient's fault, not the channels'. Whether n20 actively *helps* policy is now a clean, askable question; it was unanswerable before. The first behavioral data point is consistent with "at least no harm": the user-run 80-game heads-up eval between the lever n5 and n20 checkpoints came out dead even (38–42, paired-by-map sign test null) — though 80 games only resolves edges above ~±11 points, and 2-player games are out-of-distribution for 8-player-FFA-trained models.

## Tamed ≠ eliminated

The levers did not make the memorization small; the gap is still ~0.7 nats at both data scales — on train games the head puts ~49% mean probability on the true placement vs ~26% on val games. What changed is the memorization's character:

- **Bounded in depth.** Fully memorizing the softened labels bottoms out at hard-CE ~0.4–0.5 rather than →0, so part of the gap reduction vs the old runs (0.7 vs 1.1–1.3) is mechanical — the ceiling on memorization payoff moved. Worth stating plainly so the gap numbers aren't read as pure behavioral improvement.
- **Stable rather than compounding.** The gap drifts down across epochs (0.78 → 0.65) where every untamed n20 run's gap grew.
- **Decoupled from the trunk** — the property that matters. Per the causal-chain evidence, whatever memorization remains is no longer taxing the policy. Where it lives is unmeasured; the pyramid head's own 0.82M params are the natural suspect (a possibility 6.10-1 flagged), and that is the harmless place for it.
- **Coexisting with signal.** The small late-game signal emerges despite the residual gap, where the old regime's heads were either constant or confidently wrong on val.

A two-point observation worth tracking (hypothesis only): under the levers the gap is ~scale-invariant (0.68 at sub_5k, 0.70 at 24k), where the old regime's gaps diluted with data (e.g. direct head 1.55 → 1.15). That is consistent with the residual memorization being an *equilibrium* set by the regularizers rather than a capacity-vs-data ratio — in which case more data will not shrink it, and only different levers (head size, λ, augmentation) would.

## What the head actually knows

The stratified reads decompose the floor-beating into layers:

- **Survivor-counting + within-bucket priors** account for nearly all of it. This is real, learned, state-dependent signal (prediction entropy slides from ~1.6 at 8-alive to ~0.8–0.9 at 2-alive) — but it is the shallowest kind: knowing *how many* players remain, not *which* of them wins. The conditional floors are built to give this away for free, which is what makes the next layer visible.
- **A small real within-bucket signal at the val optimum.** At the bonus run's ep2, the head sits at-or-below its conditional floor in every mid/late bucket; the strongest single read is 3-alive at −0.087 (~2.3σ). The sub_5k cells show the same direction at their (near-optimum) ep3 — the drop cell at ~1.2–1.5σ, the nodrop cell weakly (≤0.6σ). Three near-optimum dumps pointing the same way (one strongly, one moderately, one weakly) plus one past-optimum dump not showing it is the convergent picture; no single read is decisive.
- **Known holes.** The head pays ~0.3 nats on decided games (1-alive frames, floor 0.0) — it has not learned "I am the only one left ⇒ 1st with certainty." Early-game buckets sit well above their conditional floors; how much of that is τ-smoothing tax vs absent signal is checkable from existing dumps but unchecked.

## Post-optimum dynamics: a uniform retreat toward the priors

The training dynamics, assembled from the per-epoch losses and the paired ep2↔ep6 stratified comparison:

- The generalizable component is learned fast — val optimum at ep2 even on full 24k. It is low-complexity signal present in every frame; a couple of epochs saturate it.
- Memorization happens *within* epoch 1 (~450 same-label frames per game per epoch) and then slowly un-happens: train value CE rises monotonically while the trained soft objective rises from its ep2 minimum — a genuine loss of train fit, not convergence onto the soft targets.
- Underneath a flat val aggregate, the composition shifts. Prediction entropy rises in 7 of 8 buckets from ep2 to ep6 — the head's outputs soften toward the within-bucket priors essentially everywhere — and each bucket's CE change is just the sign of what the retreating confidence was worth there: late-game real signal erodes (+0.06 paired, ~2.5σ in the two big buckets), early-game confident-wrongness washes out (−0.09 at 1.7σ in 6-alive), prior-aligned buckets don't move. (The 8-alive bucket sharpened slightly instead; unexplained, n=1.)
- A mechanism framing that organizes this (hypothesis): **the regularizers tax confidence indiscriminately** — dropout noise, weight decay, the soft-target pull, and trunk competition cannot distinguish earned confidence from memorized confidence. Early on, the head earns new signal faster than the tax collects; once the easy signal saturates, the tax compounds toward the zero-confidence equilibrium, which is exactly the bucket-prior state we observe at ep6.

Two consequences:

- **Best-value and best-policy checkpoints now diverge** (ep2 vs ep6 on the bonus run). Invisible in `epochs.jsonl` — val_value moved 1.329 → 1.354 while the late-game signal halved underneath it.
- **The extended-training question is sharper than "does val keep improving."** There remain ~0.056 nats of above-floor mass in the early/mid buckets, so more epochs plausibly *do* lower val_value — via calibration polish, while the late-game discrimination a critic needs keeps fading. If a longer run's val_value drops, the question is whether it comes from below-floor crossings late-game (real) or approach-from-above early-game (cosmetic). Current evidence leans the dissipation/equilibrium reading over a grokking-style delayed generalization, roughly 70/30 — held loosely, since it rests on one paired comparison. A bonus-run resume (+4–6 epochs, constant LR makes it clean) with per-epoch dumps would settle it.

## Scorecard against 6.09-1's bar

6.09-1 set the BC-phase bar at *do no harm, not be accurate*: the head exists to (not) corrupt the trunk that PPO inherits, and the head's own weights are cheap to retrain.

- **Do no harm: achieved**, pending the seed-noise caveat. The policy is best-ever with the head training normally, and the λ=0 run bounds how much better an amputated head could have done (≈ nothing).
- **Accuracy: near floor**, as 6.09-1 anticipated could be acceptable. The head is honest (varies with state, calibrated to bucket priors) but shallow.
- **The unresolved piece is the trunk hand-off.** The erosion finding is about the *head's readout*; whether the trunk's value-relevant features erode with it is unmeasured — and it is the trunk that PPO keeps. This reframes checkpoint selection too: if the trunk retains the features, ep6 (best policy) is the right checkpoint and the head's decay is cosmetic; if not, there is a real tension.

## Promising next lines

Ordered roughly by how much they gate, with 6.09-1's lever taxonomy as the menu. This is a discussion list, not a plan.

1. **Trunk-vs-readout probe.** Freeze the trunk at ep2 / ep6 / the λ=0 checkpoint (a natural control: its trunk trained with zero value gradient), retrain a fresh head on each, compare stratified reads. Cheap (head-only training), directly answers whether erosion matters for PPO, and informs checkpoint selection for everything downstream.
2. **Dense-history rematch at 24k with levers.** n5 vs n20, full manifest, both tamed — the project's original question (does history help the policy?), finally unconfounded. Metrics plus a powered eval (≥400 games for a 5-point edge; in-distribution FFA games rather than heads-up would remove the OOD caveat).
3. **A seed replicate of one cell.** Calibrates every n=1 comparison this arc has made; the policy deltas we now celebrate (~0.02–0.03) should be measured against actual seed noise rather than the ~0.005 cell-to-cell wobble we've eyeballed.
4. **Gradient-path levers, now reframed as tax scheduling.** 6.09-1's "modest λ" remains untried (everything so far ran λ=0.5); the erosion finding adds λ/τ/dropout *annealing* — full value pressure while the head is earning, damped once income stalls (~ep2). The bonus-run resume doubles as the cheap first experiment here.
5. **Target-side: denser per-frame targets** (the 6.09-1 aux-head menu — survival horizon, next elimination, general-location belief). Still the only lever aimed at making the signal *large* rather than safe: a per-frame-varying target both weakens the constant solution and gives the head income that doesn't stall at epoch 2. Real pipeline work; the measurement framework to judge it now exists.
6. **Input-side: symmetry augmentation.** Attacks fingerprinting orthogonally (effective game count ×8); helps every head; also real pipeline work. Lower urgency now that τ contains the damage, but it compounds with everything else.
7. **Head capacity under τ.** The shrink-the-head direction (GAP+linear, direct) is now cleanly testable — under hard CE it was confounded by the two attractors. The scale-invariant-gap observation predicts head size is one of the few levers that moves the residual gap.

The PPO-readiness checks (scalar reduction of the placement distribution, V(s) rank/monotonicity) stay deferred per 6.09-1, with one new asset: the eval harness already logs per-tick expected placement, so the monotonicity check has raw material waiting.

Instrumentation follow-ups (per-epoch dumps, panel completion, SE columns) are tracked in [`6.10-6`](6.10-6-session-summary.md).
