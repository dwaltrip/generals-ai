# Next-elimination head — Stage 0 corpus measurements

**Date:** 2026-06-13

Read-only corpus measurements that gate the next-elimination head implementation ([`6.13-5`](6.13-5-next-elimination-head-design.md)). Three questions: (1) the `death_events` column order the target extraction depends on, (2) the player-count distribution that sets the phantom-slot mask's blast radius, (3) the death-Δ distribution that validates the bin edges and produces the inverse-frequency class weights. Run over a 6000-game random sample (~8.9k perspectives, seed 0) of the local intermediate corpus (165,608 sim files).

The headline outcome: the measurements **revised the locked bin edges** from the 9-bin scheme in `6.13-5` to an 8-bin scheme, on evidence the original `6.13-5` didn't have. Rationale below.

## 1. `death_events` column order — confirmed

`sim["death_events"]` is `(timestep, slot)` — **col 0 = timestep, col 1 = slot** — rows sorted by timestep ascending, the winner absent. Verified on a concrete 8-player game (T=461): 7 rows, slot 0 the winner (absent), last death at t=459. The spec's assumption holds; `death_by_slot` indexes `de[:, 1]` → `de[:, 0]`.

## 2. Player-count distribution — phantom mask is load-bearing on ~7% of games

| players | games | % |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 113 | 1.88% |
| 5 | 68 | 1.13% |
| 6 | 85 | 1.42% |
| 7 | 173 | 2.88% |
| 8 | 5561 | 92.68% |
| **sub-8 total** | **439** | **7.32%** |

No 2–3 player games (FFA floor is 4 in this corpus). 7.3% of games start with fewer than 8 players, so the phantom-slot mask (`real_slots`) is load-bearing: without it, the 1–4 non-existent channels per sub-8 game would fall through to the winner sentinel and be mislabeled "alive, never dies" on every frame, flooding the loss with the inverse of the elimination-ordering signal. The mask is justified, not defensive.

## 3. Death-Δ distribution — and the edge revision

### The pooled marginal is the wrong lens

The pooled histogram concentrates mass in the mid/long bins and makes the short (imminent-death) bins look negligible (~1% each). This is **expected and not a reason to widen them**: each player spends the vast majority of its frames far from death and only a brief window near it, so "about to die" is rare *by construction* in the pooled count. The decision-relevant view is stratified by players-alive.

### Stratified by players-alive — the premise correction

`6.13-5` assumed "imminent eliminations cluster in the late game / endgame." The data contradicts this. Imminent-death frames (Δ<10) are rare *everywhere* and are **lowest at 2-alive** (1.9%), peaking weakly at 5–6 alive (3.9%):

```
Δ<10 mass by players-alive:   pa2  pa3  pa4  pa5  pa6  pa7  pa8
                              1.9  2.5  3.2  3.9  3.9  3.3  1.4   (%)
```

The reason: "2 players alive" names a long standoff phase (often hundreds of ticks); the death is one brief moment at its end. So the 2-alive bucket is dominated by the long/never bins, not imminent-death frames. The short bins are data-starved *regardless of stratification* — the endgame does not rescue them.

### The top bin is mostly the winner — the merge is sound

The `≥640`-merged-with-`never` top bin does heavy lifting at 2-alive (54.6% of targets), which is `6.13-5`'s "revisit if…" trigger. Decomposing it settles the question:

| players alive | top-bin % | = winner ("never") | = finite ≥640 (the conflation cost) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 54.6% | 50.0% | 4.6% |
| 3 | 37.2% | 33.3% | 3.9% |
| 4 | 28.8% | 25.0% | 3.8% |

At 2-alive the top bin is 50.0% genuine winner + 4.6% finite-loser. The winner sitting in "never" is the *signal we want* (identifying who wins = the within-bucket discrimination the value head is starved on), not a loss of it. Losers overwhelmingly die within 640 ticks and land in lower, distinguishable bins; the merge costs only the ~4% finite tail (a loser who survives 640+ ticks). So the `≥640`/`never` merge stands; splitting it stays a low-priority deferred A/B.

### Locked edges: 8 bins

`elim_bin_edges = (10, 20, 40, 80, 160, 320, 640)`, `n_bins = 8`:

```
[0,10) [10,20) [20,40) [40,80) [80,160) [160,320) [320,640) [>=640/never]
```

Changed from `6.13-5`'s original `(5,10,20,40,80,160,320,640)` by **merging the bottom two bins** (`[0,5)+[5,10) → [0,10)`). The stratified view showed `[0,5)` and `[5,10)` are each ~1% in every players-alive bucket — too starved to learn as separate classes; one `[0,10)` "imminent" class (~2.5%) is the learnable consolidation. No mid-range splits were added: subdividing the well-populated mid bins does not change the imminent-vs-`never` imbalance ratio (the lever for that is class weighting, below) and spends bins on placement-irrelevant distinctions ("dies in 100" vs "dies in 140 ticks").

Inverse-frequency `elim_bin_weights` candidates, mean-normalized (for the pre-registered class-weighting A/B if Stage 1 misses the floor — `None`/unweighted is the Stage-1 default):

- **raw inverse-freq:** `(2.60, 2.31, 1.13, 0.56, 0.34, 0.33, 0.48, 0.25)` — ~10× span.
- **sqrt inverse-freq:** `(1.77, 1.67, 1.17, 0.82, 0.64, 0.63, 0.76, 0.55)` — ~3.2× span; the gentler first choice.

Marginal entropy 1.856 nats (vs uniform-8 = 2.079) — the hard-CE value of predicting the class marginal, a proxy for the soft-marginal floor the head must beat.

### Channel 0 (self) is a distinct estimand — confirmed

The self channel puts 47.6% of its mass in the "never" bin (vs 19.4% for opponent channels), because winners contribute long all-"never" self trajectories. Confirms `6.13-5`'s note that a per-channel floor (deferred) must treat channel 0 separately rather than pooling it with channels 1–7.

## Class imbalance — the lever is weighting, not bin layout

The imbalance that bites is the rare high-value bins (imminent death, bins 0–1 ≈ 5%) swamped by the dominant `never` bin (25.4%). The ~25× imminent-vs-`never` ratio is what lets a lazy head minimize loss on the common bins while ignoring the imminent ones. Two levers address it, both already in the design:

1. **`elim_bin_weights`** (the direct remedy) — multiplies rare-bin losses up so the head can't ignore them. Pre-registered Stage-1 remedy; vectors measured above.
2. **The bottom merge** (secondary) — consolidates the starved imminent bins into one less-starved class.

Subdividing the *mid* bins does neither (it leaves the imminent-vs-`never` ratio unchanged), which is why the mid-split alternative was dropped.
