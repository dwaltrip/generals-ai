# army-regression probe — is the army leaderboard preserved in the trunk?

**Date:** 2026-06-15

**Description:** Built and ran the per-player army-regression probe (the continuous successor to the lowest-army identity probe), targeting Open 1 from [`6.14-1`](6.14-1-who-dies-next-frozen-trunk-probes.md): does multi-task trunk training keep each player's army, or wash it out? Headline: the frozen trunk preserves army **well — ~0.82 R² (linear) vs ~0.99 from raw obs**, roughly uniform across players, and **unchanged by the elimination head**. Equally important is a measurement lesson: a small/skewed eval split first produced an R² artifact that inverted the read, and only a representative eval set corrected it.

## Context

6.14-1's lowest-army *identity* probe saturated around ~0.6 (even raw obs, which carries the exact army values, couldn't be pushed past it), so it couldn't separate "the trunk preserves army cleanly" from "the trunk preserves it degraded." Army-regression replaces identity-accuracy with R², which has no such ceiling — raw obs lands ~1.0 by construction (army is a broadcast obs channel), so the raw-obs run is a calibration anchor and the trunk's R² *relative to it* answers the preservation question. The motivation is the who-dies-next result: the head does not beat the model-free "lowest-army player dies next" rule, and we want to know whether that traces to the trunk having lost the army leaderboard.

## What landed (code & tooling)

Committed on branch `who-dies-next`; suite green (178 tests).

- **`analysis/obs_utils.py` (new)** — a cleanly shared home for obs leaf-code helpers: `crop_to_board` (a **zero-copy** view of the unpadded board, and the single place the "real board is a rectangle at the top-left of the padded canvas" assumption lives), `per_player_army` (reads each player's army by cropping to the board and taking one real cell — the planes are broadcast constants, so no averaging), plus `army_totals_ch_indices` and `N_PLAYERS` moved here.
- **`probes/cross_player.py` — *partially* de-grab-bagged.** The obs-channel introspection and `N_PLAYERS` moved out to `obs_utils`. What remains (the per-player pooled-readout head zoo, the masked cross-player CE/top-1) is still grouped somewhat ad hoc; the module's own docstring keeps its provisional marker.
- **`probe_runs/army_regression_task.py`** — the new probe: a linear head (`Conv2d→8` channels, masked mean pool), masked-MSE loss over alive players, and whole-split per-player R² plus an `r2_mean` headline. Masking to alive matters: a dead player's army is ~0 and trivially predictable, which would inflate R².
- **`probe_runs/lowest_army.py`** — its target extraction now reads army through `per_player_army` (the helper's second consumer).

Only the linear head is run for this task: masked mean pool is exact for a broadcast (per-player constant) level signal, so a one-conv readout is the right instrument — capacity is not the question for army.

## The probe

Frozen-representation probe (same framework as 6.14-1): freeze a representation, cache its features over a held-out val sub-split, train the small head, read generalization. The target is the 8 per-player army scalars. Two feature sources: `raw_obs` (the anchor — army is an obs channel, so a linear readout recovers it ~perfectly) and `trunk` (the frozen trained trunk, pointed at the elim-ON and elim-OFF checkpoints).

Common config: seed 0, lr 3e-4, weight decay 1e-4, batch 256, 40 epochs, MPS. Checkpoints are 6.14-1's **ELIM_ON** (`sweeps/2026-06-14-yolo-11k-elim-head-v2/runs/2026-06-14T07-47-05Z/checkpoints/epoch_002.pt`, the 11k who-dies-next trunk) and **ELIM_OFF** (`sweeps/2026-06-11-seed-replicates/runs/2026-06-12T05-42-32Z/checkpoints/epoch_003.pt`, policy+value only); manifest `cloud_24k_sub_11k.json`.

### How it was run

```sh
# raw-obs anchor (loads the ELIM_ON checkpoint only to read its obs config)
./training/analysis/probe_runs/army_regression_task.py --source raw_obs \
  --checkpoint .../2026-06-14T07-47-05Z/checkpoints/epoch_002.pt \
  --manifest data/training/splits/cloud_24k_sub_11k.json \
  --epochs 40 --val-frac 0.5 --max-train-frames 24000 --max-val-frames 40000

# trunk (swap --source trunk; swap the checkpoint for elim-ON vs elim-OFF)
./training/analysis/probe_runs/army_regression_task.py --source trunk \
  --checkpoint .../2026-06-14T07-47-05Z/checkpoints/epoch_002.pt \
  --manifest data/training/splits/cloud_24k_sub_11k.json \
  --epochs 40 --val-frac 0.5 --max-train-frames 24000 --max-val-frames 40000
```

Each run prints a per-epoch table (train/val loss, `r2_mean`, and per-player `r2_p0..r2_p7`) and writes `summary.json` + `run.log` to `data/analysis/probes/TIMESTAMP-army_regression-SOURCE/`.

## Results

The result arrived in two stages, and the first was wrong for an instructive reason.

**Anchor.** raw obs, 40 epochs → `r2_mean` **0.994**, every player ≥ 0.97. Confirms the ~1.0 ceiling and that the head is the right instrument.

**First trunk runs (val_frac 0.2, 10k val frames) — an artifact.** ELIM_ON 0.43, ELIM_OFF 0.52, with a wild per-player pattern (one slot at R² = −1.4). The first read — "the trunk washed out army for specific players" — was wrong. A diagnostic showed the low/negative slots were the slots whose army had *collapsed variance* in that particular eval draw; since R² = 1 − MSE/Var, a tiny denominator turns a small absolute error into a large-negative score, dragging the mean down. A variance check on the full 550-game val split showed the opponent slots are statistically interchangeable — the 110-game sub-split had simply under-represented some slots' army spread.

**Representative eval (val_frac 0.5, 40k val frames).** With a representative eval set the artifact disappears:

| | anchor (raw obs) | ELIM_ON | ELIM_OFF |
|---|---|---|---|
| `r2_mean` | **0.994** | **0.803** | **0.806** |
| per-player range | ≥0.97 | ~0.66–0.92 | ~0.64–0.92 |

No negative slots; the residual per-player scatter does not even agree between ELIM_ON and ELIM_OFF, so it is noise, not structure.

**Data-scale check (2× train data: val_frac 0.3, 48k train frames).** To test whether ~0.80 is a true ceiling or just probe-budget-limited:

| | 24k train (~55 games) | 48k train (~110 games) |
|---|---|---|
| ELIM_ON | 0.803 | 0.818 |
| ELIM_OFF | 0.806 | 0.826 |

Doubling the training data lifts `r2_mean` by ~0.015–0.02 — small and clearly diminishing. The curves plateau by ~epoch 10 with a persistent train≪val gap (train ~0.01 vs val ~0.07), i.e. mild overfitting onset, not under-training. So the number is a **soft ceiling around ~0.82**, inching up slowly with data rather than rising toward the anchor.

## A measurement lesson: R² on small or skewed eval

The artifact is worth carrying forward as a standing caution for future probes.

- **R² (and any variance-normalized metric) is fragile on a small or skewed eval set.** A slice whose target variance happens to be small yields a large-negative score from a small absolute error, and that poisons the aggregate. Always sanity-check the per-group target variance of the eval set before trusting per-group R².
- **`max_frames` does not subsample games evenly.** The dataset shuffles whole games once, walks them in order (all frames of one game, then the next), and stops at the cap — so it keeps the *leading* games and none of the rest (the probe runs with no reservoir shuffle). Eval representativeness is therefore governed by `max_val_frames` (games actually cached: ~23 at 10k frames, ~92 at 40k), **not** by the `val_frac` pool size. The first runs only ever saw ~23 eval games, which is what made the variance collapse.
- **Future probes should watch for the same data skew.** When a metric depends on the spread of the target within a group (per-player, per-bucket, per-class), confirm the eval cache holds enough *games* — not just enough frames — for that spread to be representative, and prefer checking the realized per-group variance over assuming the split size is sufficient.

This also flags 6.14-1's frozen probes, which used the same small default (110-game pool / 10k frames). Those report top-1 accuracy, which is not variance-normalized and so has no R²-collapse failure mode — but the general small-game-count representativeness concern still applies, and the load-bearing who-dies-next numbers there are worth re-checking on a representative eval split.

## What's established (calibrated)

- The frozen trunk preserves army at **~0.82 R² (linear), versus ~0.99 from raw obs**, roughly uniform across players (~0.66–0.92; one slot, p2, runs a touch lower, but the anchor's p2 was also its lowest, so it is a slightly harder slot, not a trunk-specific drop).
- **ELIM_ON ≈ ELIM_OFF** (~0.82 both; OFF marginally higher, within noise) — the elimination head does not change how available army is in the trunk.
- **Mildly data-limited, with diminishing returns** — a soft ceiling near ~0.82–0.83, not a hard wall, but more probe data buys little.

**What ~0.82 means concretely.** R² = 1 − Var(residual)/Var(target), so a linear R² of 0.82 corresponds to a residual whose standard deviation is √(1−0.82) ≈ **0.42 of the army signal's own spread** (the raw-obs anchor at 0.99 has a ~0.10σ residual — near-perfect). That ~0.42σ noise floor is the relevant quantity for the who-dies-next target: identifying the *lowest*-army player is decided by close comparisons among the alive players, and when the two smallest armies are within roughly that residual of each other, the decoded ordering can flip. So "army is well-preserved" and "the decoded leaderboard misranks close calls" are not in tension — a 0.82-fidelity leaderboard is exactly one that gets the easy cases right and the near-ties wrong.

**Caveats.** This is a *linear-decodability* number — a nonlinear head might recover more, so ~0.82 is a lower bound on the trunk's army content, not a ceiling on what is present. Single seed, single config. ELIM_ON vs ELIM_OFF differ in training manifest/seed (6.14-1 Open 3), but their near-equality here makes that confound low-stakes.

## Conclusions — tentative hypotheses, not results

The live puzzle this feeds: the who-dies-next head *almost* matches the lowest-army rule but doesn't quite, let alone beat it. These are framings to test, not findings.

- **Hypothesis 1 — the gap is downstream of army representation, not upstream (well-supported, still a hypothesis).** Army is preserved at ~0.82 and the elim head does not change that, so "the trunk lost the leaderboard" is unlikely to be the explanation. The ~0.42σ residual is itself a concrete candidate mechanism: turning a slightly-noisy decoded leaderboard into a correct argmin will misrank exactly the close cases that decide the lowest-army victim. This points at 6.14-1's Open 2 — the bottleneck being the argmin/ranking step (or there being little learnable signal beyond the leaderboard in the imminent regime), rather than missing features. The evidence is now fairly strong, but it rests on a linear probe and a single config, so it stays a hypothesis.
- **Hypothesis 2 — the elim head does not enrich the trunk's army leaderboard (more tentative; not the current focus).** ELIM_ON ≈ ELIM_OFF on army suggests that, whatever value an aux elimination head or a larger (24k) run might add to the trunk, it is unlikely to come through a better army representation — it would have to route through a different channel. Noting this for later; it is not what we are chasing right now.

## Next steps (menu — rationale and what each outcome would indicate)

Pick as useful; the unifying question is *why the head can't match the lowest-army rule given army is well-preserved.*

- **Argmin / ranking probe — read the lowest-army victim straight off the trunk and compare to both the rule and the who-dies-next head.** Theory: if army is ~0.82-decodable, is the *ranking* the bottleneck? Train a head to predict the lowest-army player from the frozen trunk and measure its victim accuracy on a representative eval. If it reaches the rule but the who-dies-next head doesn't, the who-dies-next head is failing to do a leaderboard read it could in principle do (a training/target issue). If even the trunk-lowest-army readout falls short of the rule, that would indicate the ~0.42σ army noise itself caps argmin accuracy — i.e. a faithful-enough leaderboard still can't be ranked perfectly, and "almost matching the rule" may be near the achievable limit for this representation.
- **Close-call error analysis.** Theory: Hypothesis 1 predicts the head's victim errors concentrate where the two lowest armies are within ~0.42σ. Checking whether the misses actually cluster there would tie the 0.82 fidelity directly to the sub-rule gap (supports H1) or, if errors are spread evenly, point elsewhere (weakens it).
- **Nonlinear (fat) head on army-regression.** Theory: ~0.82 is linear-decodability; a 2-conv head bounds how much more army is present nonlinearly. If it recovers substantially more than 0.82, the linear number understates trunk army content and a more capable victim head has headroom; if it matches ~0.82, the trunk genuinely caps army there.
- **Re-check the load-bearing 6.14-1 who-dies-next probes on a representative eval split.** Theory: those used the same small default; even though top-1 has no R²-collapse mode, a 23–110 game eval is noisy. Re-running on a representative split would confirm (or revise) the imminent victim-accuracy numbers the who-dies-next read leans on.
- **(Parked) the aux-head / 24k-transfer program.** Hypothesis 2 bears on it, but it is not the current focus; revisit once the match-the-rule picture is clearer.
