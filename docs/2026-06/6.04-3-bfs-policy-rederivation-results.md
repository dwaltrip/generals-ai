## The fogged-city test wasn't actually discriminating (now fixed)

Line 93 is correct about the **rule**: scenario B splits 200/201 only if `city_army` comes from the last-seen 50, not the ground-truth 999. But the **test fixture** (in `test_bfs_policy.py`), as originally written, set the fogged enemy city's *current* army to 50 as well — so the current-armies array and `last_seen_armies` both held 50. The assertion would have passed even if the policy read the wrong source. The discrimination the scenario was designed to prove existed in the rule but not in the test.

Why 50 was the wrong fixture value: the `armies` array handed to `compute_known_passable` (`build_obs` → `_cat_bfs`) is the **ground-truth board** `sim["armies"][t]`, not a fog-zeroed view. Fog masking happens through the `vis` gate inside the policy (`np.where(vis, armies, last_seen_armies)`), not by blanking the array. So a realistically-fogged city carries its hidden ground truth (999) in `armies` while `last_seen_armies` holds the stale 50 — they diverge, exactly as the scenario describes. The fixture's 50 was the unrealistic part.

Fix: the fogged branch now sets the current army to 999 (`ENEMY_CITY_FOGGED_ARMY`), keeping last-seen at 50. Verified by mutation test — injecting "read current army even when fogged" fails *only* the fogged test.

## Why the visible and neutral-city cases can't (and shouldn't) discriminate

For a **visible** cell, current and last-seen are equal by construction: `step_memory` pins `last_seen_armies[vis] = armies[vis]` on every sighting. There is no realistic state where a visible city's last-seen differs from its current army, so the visible (A5/A6) and neutral-city (A3/A4) tests can't distinguish the source — and shouldn't try, since that would mean testing an unreachable state. Their job is the affordability **boundary**, which they do exercise. Only the fogged path has a real divergence to pin down.
