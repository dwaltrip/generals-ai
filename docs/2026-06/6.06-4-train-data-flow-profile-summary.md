# Training data flow — disk→GPU pipeline, measured vs. blind (`6.06-4`)

**Date:** 2026-06-06

The whole journey of the data, disk → GPU, as a linear pipeline. Legend: ✅ measured · 🔶 inferred · ❌ blind.

## A. Producer — runs in each of 8 worker processes, in parallel

The worker's job: pull 512 samples from our generator, stack them, hand the batch off.

| #   | stage                                                                                                                                           | where                    | data                                           |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **`data_load`** — read sim+meta `.npz` off the volume, DEFLATE-decompress                                                                       | worker (per game)        | ✅ ~20 µs/sample (~9 ms/game)                  |
| 2   | **`perspective_setup`** — `init_memory` + `init_bfs_cache`                                                                                      | worker (per perspective) | ✅ ~11 µs/sample                               |
| 3   | **`visibility`**                                                                                                                                | worker (per sample)      | ✅ ~22 µs                                      |
| 4   | **`step_memory`** — advance fog/history state                                                                                                   | worker                   | ✅ ~140 µs                                     |
| 5   | **build_obs**: `prelude` + `channel_build` + **`assemble`** (write channels into a per-sample buffer = **byte-pass #1**)                        | worker                   | ✅ 9 / 245 / 158 µs                            |
| 6   | **`build_mask`** + **`encode_tail`** (numpy→tensor)                                                                                             | worker                   | ✅ 36 / 29 µs                                  |
| —   | *(`encode_frame` wraps 5–6 as a reconciling reference span)*                                                                                    |                          | ✅ ~499 µs ≈ sum                               |
| 7   | `yield` sample → fetcher collects 512 into a list                                                                                               | worker                   | (loop overhead, ~0)                            |
| 8   | **`collate`** — `torch.stack` the 512 samples **directly into a shared-memory tensor** (= **byte-pass #2**, and the shm write is *inside* here) | worker (per batch)       | ✅ ~852 µs/sample ≈ **436 ms/batch**           |
| 9   | **`shm_copy`** (our pre-share probe)                                                                                                            | worker                   | ✅ ~0 (no-op — already shared)                 |
| 10  | `put` the batch (shm handles) onto the result queue                                                                                             | worker                   | ❌ not isolated (cheap; folded into `handoff`) |

And the dominant producer-side number:

| — | **worker BLOCK** — suspended, not computing | worker | ✅ **size** known (~2337 µs/sample at n20, via `handoff − collate − shm`); ❌ **cause** unknown (queue-full? waiting for an index from main? scheduler latency?) |

So: **every unit of obs-build work is densely measured.** The one producer-side blind spot is *why* the worker is blocked — we know it's idle, not what it's idle on.

## B. The handoff machinery — entirely dark

This is the chain between "worker finished a batch" and "main loop has it." With `pin_memory=True` it's three hops:

| #   | stage                                                                                                                           | where                    | data                                    |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| 11  | worker → **result queue** (cross-process IPC; passes shm handles, not bytes)                                                    | worker↔main              | ❌ **BLIND**                            |
| 12  | **pin-memory thread**: copy the shared-memory batch → page-locked memory (= **byte-pass #3**, 192→252 MiB/batch, single thread) | main process (bg thread) | ❌ **BLIND** ← prime suspect, zero data |
| 13  | pin thread → **data queue** (in-process) → main loop                                                                            | main                     | ❌ **BLIND**                            |
| —   | *in-flight capacity = `prefetch_factor × workers` = 2×8 = 16 batches ≈ ~4 s of runway*                                          | config                   | ✅ known                                |

**This whole block is uninstrumented.** It's also exactly where the bottleneck must sit — because (A) shows the workers are idle and (C) shows the GPU is idle, so the holdup is in the middle, and the middle is the one stretch with no seams.

## The shape of our knowledge, in one line

We have **dense data on the producer's work** (every obs-build step) and on the **two idle endpoints** — the worker blocking (A) and main waiting (C, `fetch_wait`). We have **almost nothing on the producer's *compute split being the cause*** (the block's cause), and **nothing at all on the entire handoff machinery in the middle (B)** — the result queue, the pin-memory thread's 252 MiB/batch copy, and the data queue. The GPU compute itself is inferred, not timed.

In short: **we've measured that both ends of the conveyor belt are idle, but we have no instrumentation on the belt itself** — and that belt (B) is where the answer has to be.
