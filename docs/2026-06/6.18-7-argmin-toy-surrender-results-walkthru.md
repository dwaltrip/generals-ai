# Argmin toy: surrender slice — findings & walkthrough

*Date: 2026-06-18. Status: findings note (Phase 0c). The step-7 read of the argmin-toy surrender slice on `cloud_24k_sub_5k` (full split, 400 epochs, lr 0.1). Confirms the encoding-sufficiency predictions of [6.18-4 §7](6.18-4-argmin-toy-surrender-design.md). Doubles as a walkthrough — what the toy is, what it measures, and how to read its grid — written to be re-read. Builds on 6.18-4 (design), 6.18-3 (grid read), and the elim-head thread (6.13-15, 6.14-1, 6.15-2).*

## TL;DR

On the trustworthy model legs, all three predicted contrasts hold:

1. **`none ≡ capture_status` — the core prod statement.** Under the `alive` target, both army-only and prod's-capture-status encodings land at **exactly 63.2%** on the surrender subpopulation — the information ceiling — and at 97.6% overall, *identical to the printed digit across all three seeds*. Prod's capture-based status channel carries **zero** discriminating power over raw army for excluding surrendered players — by construction (a captured player is already at `army==0`, so the flag is redundant with the army-zero notch) and now measured.
2. **The true alive mask removes the ceiling, linearly.** `boolean` and `categorical` reach **100%** on the surrender subpopulation with a *linear* scorer — the channel does the exclusion, so no notch and no ceiling.
3. **Flipping the target isolates surrender as the sole cause.** The same `none` model that ceilings at 63.2% under `alive` reaches **100%** under `army_pos`. The only thing that changed is whether surrendered players are valid answers.

**So what (for the elim-head):** if the elim-head's target is defined the "next DeathEvent" way (surrendered = already eliminated, must be excluded), prod's per-player status inputs **can't support it**: the status channel is capture-based (blind to surrender) and there is no alive channel, so a surrendered and a live player look identical in those inputs. (The toy tests army + capture-status — the prod-relevant status inputs; whether some *other* obs channel incidentally leaks surrender is untested.) The "next board-removal" target (surrendered still a candidate) needs no extra channel; army suffices. This is evidence for the target-definition decision deferred in 6.18-4 §10, not the decision itself.

## 1. Walkthrough — why this toy exists

### 1.1 The elim-head and the lowest-army rule

The BC agent carries an auxiliary **elim-head** that predicts **who is eliminated/captured next** among the 8 players. It isn't used to play; it rides along during training, and is valued for two reasons. First, it's a **denser** supervisory signal than the value-head: the value-head predicts the perspective player's final placement — one label per game, constant across every frame — whereas "who dies next" changes at each elimination, taking up to 7 distinct values over a game. Second, it's **legible** — there's an obvious baseline ("the lowest-army player is probably next to die"), so it's a clean lens on whether the agent's internal representation is any good.

Prior work (6.13-15, 6.14-1, 6.15-2) found the head **can't beat that lowest-army rule**, and narrowed the failure to the *ranking/exclusion* step: taking per-player armies and picking the weakest eligible one. That small, isolable mechanism is what the toy studies.

### 1.2 The toy: argmin-of-army as a stand-in

The toy strips everything else away. One frame → 8 per-player armies → **pick the index of the smallest army among the eligible players** (an argmin). Tiny models, no game trunk, no obs pipeline. The toy does **not** predict real eliminations — it computes argmin-of-army as a heuristic stand-in. What transfers to the real head is the **encoding/set question** (can the inputs support the exclusion?), not the predictive target. The toy informs the decision; it does not pre-empt it.

### 1.3 Surrender, and the three statuses

In generals.io a player can **surrender** (quit). In the sim this is specific (verified, 6.18-4 §2):

- they're marked **dead** (`alive = false`) at once — a DeathEvent fires;
- but their **tiles and army stay on the board** until someone captures them or they get neutralized.

So for a window of ticks a surrendered player is **`not alive` yet `army > 0`**. That breaks the clean `alive ≡ army>0` relationship the earlier (MVP) toy relied on. There are now **three** statuses, not two:

1. **alive** — still playing;
2. **surrendered-present** — left the game, army still on the board;
3. **eliminated** — army gone (`= 0`).

The MVP toy *filtered surrender frames out*. This slice **re-admits them** — that is the experiment. (One ~1% wrinkle: a capture-while-alive tick also briefly shows `~alive & army>0`; see the obs/alive tick seam, 6.18-6. It shifts the numbers by <1pt and is separable by run length.)

### 1.4 The two targets — the elim-head's ambiguity, made concrete

"Who dies next" is genuinely ambiguous for a surrendered player, and the ambiguity *is* the elim-head's target-definition problem:

- **"next DeathEvent" reading** — a surrendered player already had their death event → not a future candidate → **exclude**. Toy: **target = `alive`** (argmin over players still alive).
- **"next board-removal" reading** — a surrendered player is still on the board and **undefended** (they've quit, so no active defense), and stays capturable only for a fixed window before the engine **neutralizes** them (their tiles become inert deadweight you have to wade through). So they're a near-term removal *candidate* — though not necessarily a *worthwhile* capture (the general may still hold many troops; and since they make no moves they're no threat, so a costly one can be ignored and left to neutralize) → **include**. Toy: **target = `army_pos`** (argmin over players with army > 0).

Both are first-class. The toy runs the whole grid once per target.

### 1.5 The encodings — what the model sees per player

This is the axis the experiment turns on (the *status-sufficiency ladder*):

- **`none`** — army only. A surrendered player (army > 0) is indistinguishable from a live one.
- **`capture_status`** — army + **prod's actual status channel** (which opponent captured you), collapsed to a binary captured flag. The channel is **capture-based**: surrender fires a DeathEvent but *no* capture event, so it is **blind to surrender** by construction. Hypothesis: information-equivalent to `none` for this task.
- **`boolean`** — army + the **true alive/dead mask**. Lets the model exclude surrendered players.
- **`categorical`** — army + a 3-way one-hot (alive / surrendered / eliminated).

The headline question in one line: **does prod's real status channel help exclude surrendered players, or is it no better than raw army?**

### 1.6 The ceiling, and why we slice the metrics

Take `none` under target `alive`. Even a *perfect* army-only model can do no better than "pick the smallest army among `army > 0`." On surrender frames the smallest-army player sometimes *is* the surrendered one, and an army-only model has no way to know — so it picks them and is wrong. That fraction is an **information ceiling**: the best any army-only model could score. On this split it is **63.2%** on the surrender subpopulation (and **100%** under `army_pos`, where surrendered players are valid).

So we don't read the diluted overall number (surrender frames are a minority). We **slice**:

- **surrender subpopulation** (`surr`) — where the ceiling lives; the headline.
- **non-surrender mixed** (`nonsurr_mixed`) — isolates the "notch" (excluding `army==0` dead), a separate *capacity* question.
- **all** — the diluted aggregate, context only.

## 2. The grid — axes and columns

### 2.1 The models (capability ladder, and "trustworthy leg")

The models are a deliberate ladder along two directions — depth, and the equivariance spine A1 → D1 → E1 (unconstrained → permutation-equivariant → context-free shared-φ, the last being closest to the real aux head):

- **A1** — unconstrained linear (72 params). The slowest to converge; needs many epochs.
- **B1** — small MLP, one hidden layer. *Can* draw the notch, but **volatile** (hence 3 seeds).
- **D1** — equivariant linear (2 params). Fast and clean — but *linear*, so it **cannot** draw the notch.
- **E1** — per-player shared function φ. `E1_linear` (linear φ) for linearly-solvable encodings; `E1_mlp` (MLP φ) where the notch is needed.

The **trustworthy leg depends on the encoding's difficulty** — the leg that converges reliably to the achievable value:

- `none` / `capture_status` need the **notch** → read **`E1_mlp`**.
- `boolean` / `categorical` are linearly solvable → read **`D1` / `E1_linear`**.

A1 is too slow, B1 too noisy, D1 can't notch — so when reading the result, look at E1_mlp (notch encodings) and D1/E1_linear (status encodings). Other rows are *expected* failures or noise, not contradictions.

### 2.2 The metric columns

| Column | What it is |
|---|---|
| **acc_surr** | accuracy on the **surrender subpopulation** — the headline slice. |
| **ceil_surr** | the **information ceiling** on that slice — the best an army-only model could do. Read `acc_surr` against it. |
| acc_full | accuracy over **all** frames — the diluted aggregate (context). |
| acc_inset | tie-aware acc_full — credits picking *a* tied-smallest player when several are exactly equal. |
| Train / Val / Gap | cross-entropy losses; Gap = Val−Train (overfit signal). Diagnostics. |
| Best ep / Stop ep | early-stop bookkeeping; `Stop ep < 400` ⇒ early stop fired. |

`acc_surr` *above* `ceil_surr` is expected and correct for `boolean`/`categorical`: `ceil_surr` is the *army-only* ceiling, and those encodings add the alive channel, so they aren't bound by it.

## 3. Results

Trustworthy legs, `alive` target (ceiling 63.2%) and `army_pos` target (ceiling 100%):

| Encoding | Model | Target | acc_full | acc_surr | ceil_surr | reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | E1_mlp ×3 | alive | 97.6% | **63.2 / 63.2 / 63.2%** | 63.2% | at ceiling — army-only can't exclude surrendered |
| capture_status | E1_mlp ×3 | alive | 97.6% | **63.2 / 63.2 / 63.2%** | 63.2% | identical to `none` → channel adds nothing |
| boolean | D1, E1_linear | alive | 100% | **100%** | 63.2% | alive mask excludes, linearly; no ceiling |
| categorical | D1, E1_linear | alive | 100% | **100%** | 63.2% | same; the extra split buys nothing |
| none | E1_mlp ×3 | army_pos | 100% | **100%** | 100% | ceiling gone once surrendered are valid |
| capture_status | E1_mlp ×3 | army_pos | 100% | **100%** | 100% | same |

### 3.1 Three rows read end-to-end

- **`none / E1_mlp / alive` → 97.6% full, 63.2% surr, ceil 63.2%.** Carves the notch fine overall, but on surrender frames hits exactly the ceiling — army alone can't tell a surrendered player from a live one. `acc_surr == ceil_surr` reads "at the information limit," not "broken."
- **`capture_status / E1_mlp / alive` → identical to the row above, to the digit, across 3 seeds.** Adding prod's real status channel buys nothing, because it's blind to surrender. This is the core finding.
- **`none / E1_mlp / army_pos` → 100% full, 100% surr.** Same encoding and model as the first row; only the **Target** column changed, and the ceiling went 63.2% → 100%. In this controlled comparison the cap traces to surrender-exclusion alone.

### 3.2 The two load-bearing contrasts (6.18-4 §7)

- **Same model, target flipped:** `none`/E1_mlp surr 63.2% (`alive`) vs 100% (`army_pos`). Isolates surrender ambiguity as the sole cause of the ceiling.
- **Status channel removes both difficulties:** `boolean`/`categorical` reach 100% even linearly (no notch, no ceiling), vs `none`/`capture_status` which need depth for the notch and still ceiling on surrender.

## 4. What it means for the elim-head

The toy doesn't choose the elim-head's target; it prices each choice in terms of what the observation must carry:

- **If "next DeathEvent" (exclude surrendered):** prod's per-player status inputs **can't support it**. `capture_status` is blind to surrender and is information-equivalent to raw army (the `none ≡ capture_status` result); the obs schema has no alive channel. So in the inputs that encode player status, a surrendered and a live player are indistinguishable. (The toy tests army + capture-status specifically; whether another obs channel incidentally leaks surrender is untested.) Making the target cleanly learnable would mean adding a surrender-aware alive channel.
- **If "next board-removal" (include surrendered):** the ambiguity disappears; army alone suffices (the `army_pos` columns are all 100% on the trustworthy legs).

This feeds — does not settle — the target decision deferred in 6.18-4 §10.

## 5. Caveats / non-findings

- **A1 under-reads on `boolean`/`categorical`** (75.0% / 88.5% surr) — the slow unconstrained-linear convergence, cut by early-stop while still climbing (it would reach ~100%, which D1/E1_linear show directly). Cosmetic; A1 is not a trustworthy leg there.
- **B1 is volatile** across seeds (e.g. `none`/B1 surr 43.8–59.5%) — by design the least-reliable model, run with 3 seeds for exactly this reason. Don't read a single B1 cell.
- **D1 fails the notch on army-only encodings** — `none`/D1 acc_full 9.2% (worse than random: linear can't draw the notch, so it reliably picks an `army==0` dead slot). `capture_status`/D1 recovers to 69.6% full because the capture flag lets it exclude captured (army-0) players linearly — but still can't reach 100% (neutralized dead and surrendered remain).
- **Early-stop is appropriately aggressive for the notch/volatile legs and slightly too aggressive for the slow A1 cells** — converged cells (E1_mlp ~ep10) and clean linear cells (D1 at 100% by ep10, then sharpening logits to ep265–400) behave as expected; only A1-on-linear-solvable is cut short. Not load-bearing. A future run wanting a clean A1 row would raise `min_epochs`/`patience`.
- **Ceiling calibration (reconciled).** The authoritative split-specific surr ceiling is **63.2%** (irreducible error 36.8%) — measured on the exact split the run trains and evaluates on, and E1_mlp sits exactly on it. 6.18-4 §5's **~57.6%** was a different split: §5's build-step-1 fq pass filtered to the **mixed** (`n_alive<8`) population *before* `split_by_game`, while the run splits the **full** population (`population="full"`). 4 games are all-alive-only (no elimination cached), so mixed has 246 unique games vs full's 250; `split_by_game` shuffles the unique-game list with `random.Random(0)`, and a length-246 shuffle diverges entirely from a length-250 one — the held-out game sets come out nearly disjoint (symmetric difference 65), so the two surrender subpopulations are different (1,557 vs 1,497 frames) and ceiling differently. The ceiling *formula* is identical (`none_ceilings` reproduces §5's disagreement rate to the digit); only the held-out split differs. The aggregate over the whole val table (no train/val sub-split) is **53.5%** (7,437 surr frames). Quote 63.2% as the run's ceiling; 57.6% was never the right reference. Underlying gotcha: `split_by_game` is population-order-sensitive — same seed, different pre-split filtering ⇒ different holdout.
