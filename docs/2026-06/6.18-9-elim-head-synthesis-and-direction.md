# Elim-head: synthesis & direction (closing argmin-toy Part 1)

*Date: 2026-06-18. Status: synthesis & direction (Phase 0c). Closes Part 1 of the argmin-toy spike and sets the elim-head direction. Builds on 6.18-2/3/7/8 (the toy and its surrender slice), 6.13-15/6.14-1/6.15-2 (the elim-head investigation and frozen-rep probes), and 6.18-6 (the obs/alive tick seam). Intended as the single forward-looking source for this line — supersedes the §Next/§Open sections of 6.18-3/4/7/8. Companion loose-thread backlog: 6.18-10.*

## TL;DR

- The argmin toy was built to adjudicate a prior hypothesis — that the elim-head's failure lived in the argmin/ranking step — which had since been restated in places as a settled "learnability wall." It **disconfirmed** that: argmin over clean per-player army is trivially learnable, so whatever fails is *upstream* of ranking.
- That reframes the problem. The lowest-army rule is a **floor, not a target**. Performance splits into two framings: **Gap 1** — matching that army-only floor, a *representation* question — and **Gap 2** — beating it with tactical signal, which is the head's actual value. Gap 2 is **fog-bounded**: most deaths are tactical but unobservable, so the accessible signal is the always-visible leaderboard (army + land, and their trends) plus border-local vision.
- The original "head trails the rule" measurement is **confounded** by obs/target bugs (ambiguous player status, a one-tick obs/label seam), so its magnitude can't be trusted. Direction: **fix the obs, re-measure on a coherent setup (the gate), then pursue the accessible signal from an open menu of levers.**

## 1. What the spike was for, and what it settled

The BC agent's auxiliary elim-head predicts which of the 8 players is eliminated next. Prior work (6.13-15, 6.14-1, 6.15-2) found it could not beat a simple baseline — "the lowest-army player is the likeliest to die next" — and raised the hypothesis that the cause was the ranking/exclusion step (take per-player armies, pick the weakest eligible one). 6.15-2 flagged this as well-supported but still a hypothesis; a later summary (6.18-7 §1.2) restated it as a settled finding.

The argmin toy was built to test it directly: strip everything away, hand a model clean per-player armies, and ask whether it can learn the argmin. It can, trivially — on clean inputs the ranking is learnable to 100% by the permutation-equivariant and shared-φ models, and the unconstrained linear model recovers the W ≈ −I solution (6.18-2/3/7). So, as a claim about the ranking mechanism, the wall does not hold.

Part 1's result is therefore a **disconfirmation**: ranking is not the bottleneck. The toy mechanics, the encoding ladder, and the surrender slice are documented in 6.18-2/3/7/8 and are not repeated here; this note records what those results *mean* for the elim-head, which the prior docs did not step back to draw.

A note on method, because it carries forward: the toy plus the fq and probe tooling let us settle the question by isolating and measuring the mechanism rather than weighing competing prose. The same tooling is what the direction below relies on — the clean re-run's baselines and the diagnostic probes are all fq/probe consumers, already built.

## 2. What we now understand

### 2.1 There is no argmin-learnability wall

Argmin over clean per-player army is trivially learnable (6.18-2/3/7). Stated precisely, to avoid trading one overclaim for another: this is the *operation* on *clean inputs*. It does not show the real head's ranking works — it shows ranking is **not the wall**. The consequence is the load-bearing one: whatever the elim-head fails at is **upstream of ranking** — in the representation it is handed, or in the inputs themselves.

### 2.2 The lowest-army rule is a floor, not a target

The baseline uses only army totals. A model that merely matches it has shown it can extract army — nothing more. Matching the rule is a floor, a sanity check, not a measure of whether the representation is any good. The elim-head's actual value is predicting the deaths the rule *cannot* — which are tactical. (6.18-7 §1.1 framed the head's purpose as matching this baseline; that framing is superseded here.)

### 2.3 Two gaps — a lens for where the headroom is

It helps to split "beat the rule" into two questions. These are framings for reasoning about achievable performance, not a partition of the engineering work — a single fix can touch both:

- **Gap 1 — match the army floor.** The head currently trails it (roughly 41–46% vs the rule's ~47.1%, 6.13-15 — confounded regardless; see §2.4). It is failing to use even army-total information. Given §2.1 (ranking is fine), the leading explanation — well-supported but still a hypothesis — is *input fidelity*, not lost signal: the trunk preserves army, but only at ~0.82 R² linearly (vs ~0.99 from raw obs, and a linear *lower bound* — 6.15-2), and that ~0.42σ residual misranks the close calls that decide the lowest-army victim. The rule ranks over true army; the head ranks over slightly-noisy army and loses on the near-ties. (6.15-2 finds army *preserved*, with ELIM_ON ≈ ELIM_OFF — so "the trunk washed out the leaderboard" is unlikely.)
- **Gap 2 — beat the floor with tactical signal.** Its size is unmeasured. This is the head's real point.

### 2.4 Gap 1's magnitude is not trustworthy — the finding is confounded

The 41–46% figure was measured on a setup we now know was broken: player status was ambiguous (a surrendered player looked alive — 6.18-7), and the obs army channel and the alive/death target disagreed by one tick (the obs/alive tick seam — 6.18-6). Either could distort the number, and we cannot say by how much:

- If the effect is only contaminated labels/inputs on the affected frames, it is small — surrender is ~6.6% of frames (6.18-4 §5) and the seam ~1% (6.18-6).
- If the absence of any status channel degraded the learned representation more broadly, it could be larger.

We have no strong prior on which. So the honest position is that the number can't be trusted — not that the gap is "probably the bug." This is what makes a clean re-measurement the gate before any expensive representation work (§3).

### 2.5 The fog constraint bounds Gap 2 and locates its signal

Most eliminations are tactical — a stronger neighbor commits an attack, a general falls — but most of that happens in fog the perspective player cannot see. So the *accessible* signal has two parts, and which carries more is open:

- **The leaderboard** — army and land for every player, always visible — and especially its **trends**: a player whose army or land is dropping sharply is being attacked somewhere out of view. For a player entirely in fog, this is the *only* accessible signal.
- **Border-local vision** — high-quality tactical detail along the player's own borders and any scattered tiles, but only for deaths nearby.

These map onto two temporal substrates in the obs:

- the leaderboard trend would live in a **per-player global memory** that is mostly *absent* today — a confirmed gap (6.13-15: the obs carries an opponent *land*-rate but no *army*-rate, and the army-count scalar is current-frame-only, not stacked in history);
- the border-local dynamics live in the **per-tile dense-history channels**, which are present but under-revisited (an initial sweep was run, then parked when the value/elim-head work took over).

Two cheap things follow. **Land** (tile count) may be a stickier death predictor than army — a hypothesis worth a low-cost check, not a claim. And the leaderboard's appeal is that it is cheap to probe (low-dimensional, clean), not that it is the larger share.

(Light framing, held loosely: a useful second cut is *completeness* — is the signal in the obs at all — vs *extraction* — is present signal delivered to the readout. Status and leaderboard-trend are completeness; army-totals and border-local are extraction. It cuts across the two gaps rather than lining up with them, so it is a note, not the doc's spine.)

## 3. Direction

### 3.0 Sequencing

Fix correctness first (it is needed regardless), then re-measure on a coherent setup — that re-measurement is the **gate**. Don't invest in representation/extraction work until Gap 1 is shown to survive a clean setup. Two disciplines carry over from the toy: change one thing at a time (on the expensive real-model runs too), and keep bug-fixes (correctness) separate from enhancements (new signal) so any change stays attributable.

### 3.A Fix the obs — correctness (do now)

This is target-agnostic and therefore the true unblocked starting point; nothing here waits on the target decision.

- The obs/alive **tick seam** — align the obs snapshot and the death/alive target to the same within-tick moment (banners at `obs.py:348`, `obs.py:389`, `elim_targets.py:77`; 6.18-6).
- **Player-status disambiguation** — give the obs a way to tell a surrendered player from a live one. A binary alive flag suffices for the elim-head; a 3-way (alive / surrendered / eliminated) channel only if the policy/value heads want it.

### 3.B Coherent target + clean re-run — the gate

- Make the **incumbent** target reading coherent — consistent surrender handling within that reading, plus a seam-aligned label. This is not the DeathEvent-vs-board-removal decision (that defers — §3.X).
- Run 1 = bug-fixed obs + coherent target, no Gap-2 enhancements. Hold the target fixed and vary only the obs, so it is a clean A/B against the original.
- Add baselines beyond lowest-army: **lowest-land, fastest-land-loss, trend-aware**. Cheap, and one of them may turn out to be a stronger floor than lowest-army.
- The read is binary: **does Gap 1 survive a correct setup?** If the head now matches the floor, the confound explained it (and which fix mattered is a cheap follow-up ablation). If it still trails, Gap 1 is real and representation work is earned.

### 3.C The enhancement landscape — an open menu

Post-gate. A landscape, not a sequence — mostly Gap-2 signal, plus the Gap-1 representation levers where they overlap. It is not yet clear which moves the needle most, so the list stays open and we pick by signal-per-cost and by what the gate and the baselines reveal.

- **Global / leaderboard signal** (per-player, the fog half — mostly absent):
  - *temporal:* army-delta and land-delta (the simplest rung); lagged army/land (e.g. t−5 / −10 / −25); summaries (EMA, drawdown-from-peak, frames-since-peak, growth rate).
  - *derived-static:* ordinal rank rather than raw totals; army normalized by total board army (game-phase invariant); army-per-land density (bottled-up vs spread); players-alive / game stage.
  - *design axes:* explicit-derived features vs raw lagged history; temporal resolution (per-tick is redundant, so sparse or windowed).
- **Local / dense-history signal** (per-tile, the border-local half — present, under-revisited): revisit the history depth N; reconsider what the per-tile history encodes (army over time, ownership flips, recently-attacked).
- **Extraction / architecture** (help the model use signal already present — overlaps Gap 1): an elim-head-specific pooling or attention over players (cross-player comparison is what the head needs and convs don't do naturally); an auxiliary army-reconstruction loss to push the trunk to retain army (cheap to pre-test in the toy — §3.D).
- **Head-training levers** (lower priority given the upstream diagnosis — tuning the head won't help if the input lacks the signal): loss weighting, hard-slice targeting in the imminent dt<10 regime, confidence ramp (6.13-x).

How to choose: let Chunk B and the baselines point. If the land or trend floor beats the army floor, prioritize the land/trend channels; if Gap 1 survives the clean re-run, the extraction levers rise.

### 3.D Toy Part 2 — gated, optional

Only if the re-run says it is worth it. Candidates, roughly in order of cheapness:

- **Real-label-on-army-totals** — keep the vector toy, swap the argmin target for the real next-death from the replay; measures how much of real death is predictable from army totals alone (the floor's true ceiling).
- **Temporal-vector toy** — per-player army+land over a history window → real next-death; cheap, and high-fidelity for the fog half (the dominant accessible signal there is a clean low-dimensional vector).
- **Auxiliary army-reconstruction** — does forcing the trunk to retain army (a recon aux loss) push fidelity past the ~0.82 residual? A fix that would port directly to prod. (Better-motivated than "reproduce contention-attenuation" — 6.15-2 found adding the elim head left army fidelity unchanged.)
- **Layerwise army-R² localizer** — probe army-recoverability per layer to locate where it is lost.
- **Spatial / extraction ladder + model menu** — broadcast and raw-tile layouts, prod-shaped trunk vs DeepSets, and so on.

Fidelity caveat: the toy's value — cheap and interpretable — erodes as it approaches prod, and erodes for both properties at once. Past that point, instrument the real model directly (the probe tooling exists). The tell that a rung has gone too far: it degrades and the cause can't be attributed to a single axis.

### 3.X Parked decisions and gated work

- **Elim-head target definition** (next-DeathEvent vs next-board-removal) — deferred; it can follow the obs fix, and Run 1 uses the incumbent reading regardless. The encoding-sufficiency evidence is in 6.18-7/8; the choice is yours.
- **The Gap-1 representation work** — improving the trunk's army fidelity past the ~0.82 residual — is gated behind the Chunk B re-measurement; earned only if the gap survives.
- The **order of enhancement (3.C) vs extraction work** is left open; the gate's result informs it.
