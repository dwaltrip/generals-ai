# Session note — the writing-quality diagnosis and the draft-doc skill

*Date: 2026-07-04. The record of a session-long discussion: why agent-written docs keep coming out dense and mis-weighted, a process designed against it, the new `draft-doc` skill, and the v1→v2 rewrite experiment that trialed it. Written for pickup on 2026-07-05 — the CLAUDE.md edits are decided-in-spirit but not yet shaped, and the open items are in §6.*

## 1. The trigger

Daniel reviewed the chunk-1 progress summary (v1) and found the content decent but the prose stacked, dense, and mis-weighted — four examples pulled from §1 alone without reading further. The standing question underneath: slow per-sentence iteration with the agent works, but it demands constant vigilance. Is the failure a fundamental limitation of the model?

## 2. The diagnosis

The failures sit at the sentence-planning level — facts per sentence, choice of grammatical subject, ordering by significance — not at the word level.

The existing global CLAUDE.md "direct sentence structure" section condemns exactly these in its headline principle ("one fact per clause"). The precise gap: its operational machinery — the enumerated tells and the "parses correctly on first pass" test — targets *ambiguity*, sentences that misparse. These failures are *overload*: sentences that parse fine but carry too much or weight it wrong. The model reliably executes the checkable tells, not the principle, so a faithful pass certifies the prose at issue.

Three forces behind the overload register:

- **The completeness instinct.** Dropping a fact feels like a catchable error; a stacked clause doesn't. Under simultaneous pressure to be complete and concise, "all facts, tighter syntax" wins over "fewer facts, plainer sentences".
- **In-context contagion.** Imitating dense source material just read is a stronger pull than a style instruction stated once. This predicts the failure is worst right after deep immersion in the old docs — and explains why the repo's existing prose keeps re-infecting new prose.
- **The density prior.** The model's background sense of "polished technical writing" is dense; appositives and dash-asides read as mastery in the training distribution.

Supporting evidence from this same session: two of Daniel's four examples were introduced or worsened by the agent's own *fix* passes, and a sub-agent reviewer blessed v1's writing quality after reading the same dense sources — it inherited the same blind spot.

On "fundamental limitation": partial. The capability exists — guided sentence-by-sentence iteration produces the wanted register. What is unreliable is invoking it unprompted across a long document. Rules move the average, but they don't survive contact with dense sources. The implication drawn: change the generation setup, not the rule count.

## 3. The CLAUDE.md amendment — drafted, then parked

A concrete amendment to the "direct sentence structure" section was drafted in chat and parked by Daniel's call, to first see what the process alone buys. Its parts:

- **Overload tells** (scannable, like the existing ambiguity tells):
  - an insertion — apposition, dash pair, parenthetical — splitting subject from verb
  - more than one inserted aside per sentence
  - a bonus clause tacked on after the sentence's point has landed
  - two substantial announcements sharing one sentence via "and"
  - "plus"/"alongside" gluing facts of unequal significance
  - a grammatical subject that isn't what the sentence is about
- **A replacement test:** each sentence makes one point, and the main clause is that point. Spoken check: could you say it aloud to a colleague without re-planning midway?
- **A repair menu, in priority order:** (1) drop the minor fact if it has a home elsewhere, (2) demote it to its own smaller sentence or a table, (3) only then restructure. The forbidden default: keeping every fact and re-punctuating.
- **Daniel's reframe, load-bearing for everything after:** expansion is diagnostic, not bloat. Density is how the significance judgment gets *skipped* — unpacking a dense sentence exposes the low-value content the compression was hiding, and a document that grows longer after a de-densifying pass skipped the judgment again.
- Also on the table: an exemplar before/after pair (imitation is the strong channel, so feed it the right target), and naming ambiguity vs. overload as distinct failure classes.

## 4. The process, and the draft-doc skill

The process: **frame → gather → organize & filter → draft**, now encoded in `.claude/skills/draft-doc/SKILL.md`. It separates collecting, judging, and expressing, so each happens where it is performable — facts get judged on a list, where each stands alone, not in finished prose, where every sentence reads as necessary. It targets the completeness and contagion forces. It does *not* touch the density prior — the register still needs its own fix (§3), which is why the amendment is parked rather than dropped.

Design decisions, most of them Daniel's:

- **The frame step is first and not skippable** — purpose is what makes significance measurable at the filter step.
- **Two tmp artifacts** (`<slug>-facts.md`, `<slug>-outline.md`), so triage is a visible transformation with an input and an output, and the phase separation is physical.
- **Both review checkpoints are optional** (after frame, after outline), judged on two axes: document complexity/stakes and how collaborative the session is. High on either → ask at kickoff which checkpoints the user wants.
- **Steps are dominant focuses, not gates.** The agent proposed gate-style mechanisms — a verbatim-atoms rule, fact numbering for auditability — and Daniel rejected them: accuracy doesn't compose through gates, synthesis creates new claims no atom-check covered, and verification happens on the final draft. Worth recording that this was the same instinct as the tells-over-principle failure in §2 — substituting checkable mechanism for judgment.

Deferred variants, for later iteration if the simple version falls short:

- Subagent blind-drafting, or a fresh-session handoff — stronger source isolation than same-session discipline.
- A pre-outline "organized-facts" step for messy fact sets. A leeway clause in step 3 covers it for now.
- Detector tooling extending `scripts/one_offs/dump_prose_semi.py` toward structural-density tells.

## 5. The experiments

**Run 1 — the skill doc itself.** The process drafted its own SKILL.md. It worked, but as a test it was weak: the "sources" were the live conversation, so the contagion force was barely present.

**Run 2 — the v2 summary, renovation mode.** v1 was decomposed into a facts doc (~90 atoms plus a supplemental gather for the revised frame), re-outlined, and re-drafted as `7.04-4-ckpt-config-progress-summary-v2.md`. Findings:

- **The frame revision mattered materially.** Widening the frame from planner-handoff to durable-narrative exposed a *selection* gap in v1, not just a weighting one: v1 had no "why this refactor" content, and omitted the unrealized-payoff fact (nothing reads the stored config yet) entirely.
- **Renovation mode has an anchoring hazard.** The first outline justified its structure by reference to v1 ("as v1's — it worked") — inherited rather than derived. Daniel caught it. When the facts doc is decomposed *from* an existing document, the old structure rides along in the atom ordering, and the outline step needs an explicit fresh-composition discipline. This is a candidate skill amendment.
- **The checkpoints did real work twice.** Beyond the anchoring catch, the second outline still needed an altitude correction from Daniel (a per-finding review table doesn't belong in a narrative doc; review notes folded into a process section instead).
- **Outcome.** v2 came out at ~95 lines against v1's ~135 and Daniel judged it a large improvement. The finishing passes on v2 produced five one-line fixes; on v1 the equivalent passes had produced structural rewrites across five commits. Modest reading: the process front-loaded the judgment, so the repair layer had little left to do. Caveats: heightened in-session awareness, v1 anchoring, and a single data point — Task B is the cold trial.

Daniel's assessment of the run: the frame and outline checkpoints were the standout. That matches the intended labor shift — from post-hoc prose vigilance to two cheap reviews at the points where the judgment actually happens.

Note: the v1 summary doc has been deleted and replaced by the v2 doc.
