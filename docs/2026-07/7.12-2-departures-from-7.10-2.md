# Appendix — departures from the 7.10-2 design

*Date: 2026-07-12. Companion to the [v2 overview (7.12-1)](7.12-1-behavior-goldens-first-cut-v2-overview.md). A full walkthrough of [7.10-2](7.10-2-behavior-goldens-first-cut-design.md) confirmed its fundamentals and changed a handful of things. This appendix records each departure and its reasoning. Journey framing lives here deliberately, so the overview can read clean.*

*Update 2026-07-20: a naming pass renamed the E1/E2/E3 effect classes (now "impacts") and related goldens vocabulary (channel-class names and "surface" labels). See [7.20-3](7.20-3-goldens-naming-reference.md).*

## 1. Design changes

**Layer-1 goldens move from the deferred list into the cut, sequenced near the end of the build.** 7.10-2 §2 deferred the layer-1 golden (re-derive each fixture npz from its source, compare) on no-structure-without-a-consumer grounds. The walkthrough found the consumer: the fixture-schema-migration two-step was the design's one remaining piece of remembered discipline, and a per-fixture re-derive test converts it into a machine-prompted event. When only layer-1 fires, the sim or parser changed. When only layer-2 fires, the obs or targets code changed. When both fire, a schema migration is in progress and the two-step applies. The tests live in the sim/parser test tier (they need the Rust build, which the golden tier deliberately avoids), and a few fixtures suffice because they guard shared code, not per-fixture data. Two build-time items: each covered fixture's source replay gets committed alongside its npz (a small size cost), and parser determinism on a fixed replay should be verified once.

**Committed toy fixtures are removed.** This is a leaning rather than a hard rule. 7.10-2 §3 kept one or two scripted toys as debugging aids for fires inside real games, where correct cell values can't be derived by inspection. Two observations dissolved the need. First, coverage was never the toys' job: 7.10-2's own realism argument already demoted them to debug-only. Second, the debugging value doesn't require committed fixtures. A throwaway scripted game can be authored on demand at fire time through the produce-fixtures command, and it needs no blessed references: its job is supplying first-principles correct values derived from the script, and any old values needed for comparison are already available in the real fixtures' frozen references and in git. Committing toys in advance was structure without a consumer. The removal also deletes pre-build item 4 (the toy meta synthesizer) and the toy provenance-stamping schema obligation. The call reverses cheaply if early fires turn out to need scripted ground truth regularly.

**The freeze-time asserts shrink to a lightweight soft commitment.** The surviving version is small: each fixture declares event-class tags, and fixture production checks them through a tag-to-predicate dict over the npz event arrays, a few dozen lines in total. It may still be trimmed or dropped during the build. The walkthrough's assessment: the authoring-time value (the selection was correct, not just intended) is largely replaceable by careful manual verification. The unique value concentrates at schema-migration regeneration, the one moment layer-1 comparison is suspended because the npz is expected to change. One presentation note for future docs: the mechanism reads heavier on the page than it is in code.

**The "stub" becomes the boundary record, and its destination is decided.** The rename is because "stub" names the artifact by its least important property, that it starts unfilled. The completed record is the durable artifact everything downstream reads, and the lifecycle language follows: regen *opens* a record, the author *completes* it, and the blocking test refuses to pass while any record is open. Completed records accumulate in a standalone append-only boundaries ledger, which resolves 7.10-2's open question 1. The reasoning: boundaries are events (chronological, consulted at comparison time), while support status is derived state, so route-1's eventual policy doc is a downstream view computed from the ledger rather than its container. A standalone ledger also avoids re-coupling the build to route-1, and it has seed content waiting: the three known May–June boundaries, whose retroactive record has been queued since [7.08-1](7.08-1-confound-boundaries-survey.md).

**Boundary-record automation is staged.** The class-first field structure stays (the record leads with the effect class, which determines the required follow-ups), because as template text it costs nothing, and it is the defense against boilerplate answers during the elim arc, when fires will be frequent. What gets deferred is the machinery: pre-filling the class from which golden fired (the framework's surface table makes this mechanical) and inferring gatedness from the fire pattern (a new entry failing as unblessed means gated, existing references moving means ungated). That layer waits for demonstrated need, and it slots cleanly into the regen tool's template-writing step later. Three judgments can never be automated and stay human: the sign of an E2 (break vs repair), the depth of an E3 (ruler vs task), and the support disposition.

**A guard rule for config-point scope is added.** Goldens guard code paths, and config points are the addressing scheme. Enum-like knobs (obs dtype, elim variant, the status-channel gate) guard every occupied value, because each value selects a different branch. Scalar knobs guard per regime, with declared boundaries: for dense_history_n the regimes are 0 and greater than 0, and a second point inside a guarded regime adds little because its code is already guarded at the sibling point. The rule is codified minimally as declared guard rules beside the registry, and it is the curation rule route-1 inherits. It also reframes 7.10-2 §5's manual-occupancy limitation: the structural cases already have automatic forcing (the elim registry parametrization and the gated-channel-groups structural test), so what slips through the manual gap is mostly the parametric category, which needs little guarding (dtype occupancy is also manual, but its two occupied values are both guarded already).

**Budgets are reframed by the project horizon.** The project has roughly six months of remaining work (stated this session). 7.10-2 §4's growth framing (expected re-blesses per year, 20 to 30 fixtures over the project's life) drops out, and the budgets become sanity checks rather than binding constraints. The size-and-runtime probe demotes from a gating decision to a cheap first measurement, though it still runs first, and the size-budget test still ships, keeping ceilings encoded as tests. The repo size ceiling itself has a little give if that becomes the pragmatic choice.

## 2. Open questions resolved

7.10-2 §11 listed four open questions. All four now have dispositions.

1. **Record destination**: the standalone boundaries ledger (§1 above).
2. **Sequencing vs the elim arc**: the cut is built first, at least some version of it.
3. **Legacy-point support horizon**: confirmed as non-blocking. Over-guarding is the right posture, and the eventual call stays with route-1.
4. **Capture-seam intent**: documented as the known convention for now, revisited after the first cut. The note folds into the memory.py banner rewrite (pre-build item 1, same file), so the intent call no longer waits for first bless. The cost asymmetry decides it: documenting costs a comment, while fixing would be an ungated E2 boundary for every existing checkpoint, spent on a subtle semantic in about 1% of frames (capture ticks, an estimate carried from 7.09-1) with no evidence of harm.

## 3. Corrections and bookkeeping

- **Correction to 7.10-2 §5**: "dense_history_n occupies only 5 (the N-sweep never ran)" is wrong. Checkpoints trained at n=20 exist. Under the guard rule they need no additional golden (same regime as the guarded n=5), but whether they are supported goes on the route-1 list.
- **New planned follow-up** (a separate task, not blocking the cut): unify the two npz writers, `write_sim_output` in replay-parser and `write_eval_game` in game-runner. The walkthrough verified they are near-identical: the payload dicts match except for the provenance keys, the event-packing helpers are copy-pasted, and both read StaticMap, because the May 30 refactor that split StaticMap out of GameStatic removed the type obstacle. After unification, layer-1 guards one writer instead of two, and every npz producer shares one schema including provenance keys.
- **Pre-build list**: four items become three. The toy meta helper is deleted with the toys, and item 1 (the 6.18 trail repair and stale-96 sweep) now also carries the capture-seam documentation.
- **Presentation decisions applied in 7.12-1**:
  - The meta-principle is stated once, with the frozen-fixture and explicit-config rules as its two legs.
  - A guarded-vs-unguarded table replaces facts scattered across sections.
  - The event-labeling of frozen frames is stated explicitly (it answers the localization half of the debuggability question that motivated the toys).
  - Fire reports gain an event-context annotation as a build item.
  - The per-frame-hashes question in the stats layer is carried as a small open item.
  - Future goldens docs lean toward smaller, more focused pieces rather than one doc covering everything.
