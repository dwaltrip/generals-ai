# Confounds framework: application scratchpad

*Date: 2026-07-21. Status: unpolished capture doc, feeding a future pass that folds application guidance into [confounds-framework.md](../confounds-framework.md).*

This doc captures the application-level material from the 2026-07-21 confounds session: litmus tests, distinctions, edge cases, and worked examples that go beyond what the framework doc currently states. The material serves three reader goals:

- understand a potentially-confounding code change along its impact-relevant characteristics,
- assess what the change means for existing artifacts (checkpoints, recorded numbers) and for cross-boundary comparisons in specific workflows (analysis, model eval, training curves), and
- decide what introducing the change requires (gating, boundary records, data regeneration, re-measurement).

The impact is what matters: the classifications and litmus tests are tools for understanding a situation well enough to assess the change's impact properly.

The doc is deliberately unpolished: content is captured comprehensively rather than ranked or structured for presentation. Each entry is written to stand alone for a reader without the session's context. The session's open items and restructure planning live in [7.21-2](7.21-2-confounds-open-items.md).

## 1. General tools (apply to any change)

**The tests are the authority; the rows are precomputed answers.** When a change doesn't match a row cleanly, run the three tests on the change directly, and union the impacts across every surface the change touches (the union rule in the doc's §1). Forcing a change into a single row is the failure mode this rule prevents.

**The four-way discriminator.** For sorting any change quickly:

- The mapping from game state to scored moves changed → `contract-break` or `contract-repair` (a subject change).
- How we score or aggregate changed → `metrics-change` (the ruler).
- The game being played changed, in its rules or in the information it exposes → `mechanics-change` (the world).
- Only training changed → `recipe-change`.

**The mirror test, two formulations.** The framework doc's Artifacts section states the first: does training have a counterpart that this inference-side code must stay consistent with? Code with such a counterpart is part of the subject. Code without one is part of the apparatus. The equivalent "own play" formulation: could you apply the change to every checkpoint, old and new, and still call each one's resulting play its own? Yes means apparatus. No means subject.

**The function-vs-distribution line, and the frozen-checkpoint litmus.**

A `contract-break` means the *mapping* changed: state to scored moves computes differently, or outputs are decoded differently. A `mechanics-change` means the *distribution* changed: which states occur. Test 2 is about the mapping only.

*Note: The previous line is load-bearing and is currently stated nowhere in the framework doc. The doc's contract-break bullet actively blurs it: "inputs, computation, or output decoding they never trained with" reads as if it also covers distribution shift, since a world change likewise puts old checkpoints in states they never trained with. The guidance pass should reword the bullet so it names the mapping (something like "input construction that differs from training"), alongside stating the line itself.*

The operational test for Test 2 comes in two forms:

- Model-internal form: does the change alter what a frozen checkpoint computes in eval mode?
- Same-state form (for sim changes): hand a frozen checkpoint the same game situation before and after the change. Same obs tensor and same scored moves means no contract impact. Different means contract territory.

## 2. The output end: decode and selection

**Decode is subject.** It must invert the target encoding fixed at training time: which output slot means which move is baked into the weights by how the targets were encoded.

**Decode never executes during training.** The loss consumes encoded targets directly, so a decode change carries no `recipe-change`. This is the reason decode belongs in the live-inference-path row (– training, ✓ live runtime), and the reason it has no shared-path twin the way obs code does. The row's numbers cell (the information conditional) never fires for decode, since a decode change doesn't alter what information the model sees.

**Selection is apparatus.** BC training never chooses a move: the cross-entropy loss consumes the model's whole scored distribution against the single demonstrated move. No training step exists for a selection policy to drift out of sync with.

**A contrast pair that makes the split concrete.** Swap the decode's direction ordering and every old checkpoint immediately plays nonsense, because its outputs are misread: the contract is broken. Change sampling temperature from 1.0 to 0.5 and every checkpoint plays sharper, with each one's play still its own: nothing is broken, and only recorded-number comparability across the boundary is affected.

**Edge cases, sorted the same way by both formulations.** Invalid-move masking at inference is apparatus (no training counterpart, applies uniformly). Argmax tie-breaking is apparatus. The stale-live-obs fix (incident 2 in the framework doc's §4) is subject, because the live obs path has a mirror: the training obs path.

**The target-analogy split.** Changing target *values or semantics* (e.g. the elim tick-seam fix, incident 3) has no runtime mirror, so it carries no contract impact. Changing the policy target *encoding* (the slot-to-move convention) has the decode as its runtime mirror, so it is a contract-break. The intuition "label changes feel like they corrupt old checkpoints" is calibrated on the second kind, while the framework's labels row describes the first.

**PPO caveat.** "Training never selects" is a BC property, not a universal one. Under PPO, training samples actions to generate its own data: the behavior policy's temperature becomes part of the recipe, and selection acquires a training-side counterpart (a train-vs-deploy selection mismatch becomes a real consistency question). The mirror test itself stays valid. Its answer for selection is regime-dependent.

## 3. Model-internal changes

**The three-way split.**

- Alters weight shapes → the model-shape row: `recipe-change` plus a loud `contract-break` (old checkpoints fail at load).
- Same shapes, but the eval-time forward computation differs → the forward-semantics row: `recipe-change` plus a *silent* `contract-break`. This is the most hazardous class in practice, since the old checkpoint loads fine and quietly plays below strength.
- Same shapes, train-mode only → the training-only row: `recipe-change` alone.

**Shape preservation does not imply recipe-only.** The split between classes (2) and (3) is by eval-time effect, not by shape.

**Why class (2) carries both impacts.** Forward-pass code executes during training (shaping gradients, hence `recipe-change`) and at inference (shaping what a frozen checkpoint computes, hence `contract-break`). Training-only code shapes only the first. That asymmetry is the entire difference between the two classes.

**Dropout is the tempting misfile.** It is the identity in eval mode and parameter-free, so both rate changes and adding or removing dropout modules are training-only (`recipe-change` alone). It is the training-only change most tempting to misfile into forward semantics, because it lives inside the model's `forward()`.

**More sorting examples.** Batch-norm epsilon → forward semantics (it is in the eval-time computation). Batch-norm momentum → training-only (it shapes the learned running stats, with no eval-time change). Activation swap (ReLU → GELU) → forward semantics. Weight-initialization scheme → training-only (it executes once, at the start of training, inside the model).

**The zero-impact class.** Train-time execution changes that don't systematically change the weights (gradient checkpointing, and by the doc's own exclusion, logging and dump cadence) carry no impacts at all. Test 1's "systematically, not as run-to-run noise" clause excludes them.

**Practitioner-taxonomy warning.** The rows are defined by the three tests, not by practitioner vocabulary. "Adding dropout" is colloquially a model or regularization change, but the tests file it with learning rate. Row names are mnemonics for typical members: the membership rule is behavioral. (The doc's row name already states this — renamed to "Training-only code/config" 7.21.)

**A known coverage edge.** An inference-only forward change (int8 quantization for deployment, a compile or fusion step that isn't numerically exact) would have the profile (– training, ✓ runtime, – numbers): a contract-break with no recipe-change, which no table row produces today. This project currently does none of these. The doc's closing extension hook is the designated response if one appears.

## 4. Sim-core and world changes

**The two frames, at two precision levels.** A sim-core change can do two different things. Intuitive phrasing: change how the game unfolds, or change how the game state gets presented to the model. Precise phrasing: change the *trajectories* (which states occur) or the *semantics* (what the stored state fields mean).

**The mirror test resolves the input end.** State-field semantics are mirrored: the live sim's state format must stay consistent with the parsed states that training obs were built from. Dynamics have no mirror: training data is a sample of the world, not a convention the runtime must match, so the world can move without anything becoming inconsistent. This is the same test that sorts decode from selection, so the subject/apparatus boundary at both ends of the runtime path is decided by one test.

**Trajectories-kind changes carry no contract impact.** The frozen-checkpoint litmus passes: same situation in, same scored moves out. What they do instead is leave old checkpoints *out of domain*.

**Out of domain, unpacked.** The checkpoint's function is bit-identical. What degraded is the fit between the unchanged function and the moved world. The reason this is not a contract impact: a broken function corrupts attribution, because the measured play is not the model's own play and no measurement can unmix ability from mismatch. A world shift keeps attribution honest: the measured play is exactly the model's own play, at a game it wasn't trained for, like a human player after a rules patch. The framework polices measurement validity, not model performance.

**Where out-of-domain lives in the framework: captions, not cures.** Verdict row 1 is clean "including the recipe change's effect". Row 2 is "valid (today's game)". Row 6 captions "which game era". Nothing needs curing because nothing is lying: old numbers remain true statements about a game that no longer exists. They lose comparability, not validity.

**The three consequences of one world change.** Recorded numbers stop comparing across it (`mechanics-change`). Models trained after it learn a different game (`recipe-change`). Models trained before it are mismatched to the new world (out of domain). The first two are impact labels. The third is not a third impact: it is what the world change means for frozen checkpoints, and the verdict table's captions carry it.

**Semantics-kind changes route through the obs rows.** A semantics-kind sim change is really an input-construction change, even though the edited code lives in sim-core: the obs code reads the reinterpreted fields, so the same true situation produces different obs for every existing checkpoint. It routes through the obs rows via the union rule. The game row's cells (runtime –) give the wrong answer for it.

**The full map, with clocks.** Sim-core output is consumed in two places, and each has its own boundary clock:

| | Live sim (clock: the commit) | Parsed data (clock: the regeneration) |
|---|---|---|
| **Trajectories** | `mechanics-change` on gameplay-eval numbers, immediate | `recipe-change` (later runs train on corrected data), and the `mechanics-change` now reaches curves and analysis numbers |
| **Semantics** | `contract-break` for every existing checkpoint, immediate | newly trained models are back in contract after regen plus retrain, and the `recipe-change` lands |

(A condensed version of the trajectories row landed in the doc's game-changes bullet 7.21 as a quick fix; the guidance pass should supersede it with this fuller treatment.)

**Why the mechanics-change reaches curves and analysis numbers late.** Their pipelines contain the sim through materialized artifacts: the parsed val data and dumps are the world half of their apparatus, sitting in storage. Regeneration swaps world A for world B inside those pipelines. It is the same impact as the live-sim case, delivered late because this part of the world is materialized on disk rather than computed live.

**The semantics-window breach.**

Between a semantics-kind commit and the regeneration, the live and training sides disagree for everyone: even a model trained inside the window trains on old-semantics obs and is served new-semantics obs.

- A semantics change makes the shared path's two halves change at different times.
- Practical guidance: don't ship a semantics-kind sim change ungated, and if one must land anyway, regenerate immediately to close the window.

The framework doc is currently missing proper treatment of this.

## 5. Worked example: the A-vs-B sim-fix scenario

**Setup.** Fix a trajectories-kind sim bug, regenerate the intermediate data, retrain with an identical config. Checkpoint A trained before the fix and regen, checkpoint B after.

**A fresh A-vs-B eval is clean.** The verdict comes from row 1 of the verdict table (old vs. new checkpoint, both run now): the recipe column says clean including the recipe change's effect, and the mechanics column says clean. It is *not* an instance of the doc's "breaks neither contract" paragraph: the sim fix does break the measurement contract for recorded numbers. The fresh eval records new numbers on both sides and simply never triggers it.

**The recorded-number contrast.** B's fresh win rate against A's *old recorded* win rate is incomparable (`mechanics-change`). Same change, different activity, different verdict: a compact demonstration of why classification takes both steps (impacts, then activity).

**How to read the fresh gap.** With the config held identical, the measured gap is the data correction's effect plus run-to-run training noise. It cannot be read as B "learning better" in any generic sense. A's out-of-domain disadvantage is part of the measured quantity, not a distortion of it, and symmetry (both models play the same new world) is what keeps the comparison fair.

## 6. Routing notes, and a note for the synthesis pass

**New obs channels are necessarily shared-path.** The model can only use a channel that the training data also builds, so a channel addition never routes through the live-inference-path row.

**Genre note for the synthesis pass.** The framework doc's labels-row walkthrough ("the row easiest to misread") is the prototype shape for guidance entries: name the trap, walk the cells against the tests, and resolve the misleading intuition. New guidance entries should take its shape.
