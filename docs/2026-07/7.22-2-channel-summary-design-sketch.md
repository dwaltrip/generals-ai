# Channel-summary report — design sketch

*Date: 2026-07-22. This doc settles the "channel-summary contents" pre-build item opened in [7.15-2 §8](7.15-2-goldens-first-cut-scope-revision.md): the per-channel stat recipes, the report's format and layout rules, and the principles behind them. It extends the reference design of 7.15-2 §2 and departs from it in two named ways (markdown instead of JSON, and a five-group class record instead of the four-class census). The artifact keeps its [7.20-3](7.20-3-goldens-naming-reference.md) name with one clarifying extension: we call it the channel summary report, or "the report" in context. A writer-design session is expected before the build and may revise details here.*

## Intro

The channel summary report is a reference artifact of the behavior goldens: a small markdown file that the regen tool writes at bless time, one per `(fixture, config point)`. It is committed to git alongside the other artifacts (the hash references and the targets references). Its diff provides a human-readable overview and sanity check for a re-bless commit.

Boundary-inducing behavior changes are detected by the frame and per-channel hashes, which the test suite compares byte-exactly against their blessed values. The report complements those artifacts with summary information that can be scanned once a golden has fired: which channels changed and roughly how they moved.

The design targets two main use cases. The primary one is bless-time review of an intentional behavior change, where the reviewer reads the report's diff to confirm that the observed changes match the intended ones. The second is an accidental fire, where the report is the only standing magnitude information. Here, it gives a first view of what may have caused the fire and helps decide next steps (including a possible exact rerun).

## 1. Governing rules

The recipes were selected against rules inherited from 7.15-2, restated here as the local decision criteria.

- **Detection is fully carried by the frame hashes.** A stat is never justified by what it might catch. Every stat needs a legibility case: it makes a bless diff readable, or it helps orient debugging after a fire.
- **The bless-commit reviewer is the primary consumer.** The design test used throughout: when an intentional change is blessed, does the report's diff let the reviewer confirm that the change's sign, scope, and rough size match intent?
- **Fire-time magnitude information is coarse by design.** Tick-level and cell-level questions belong to the frame hashes and the rerun recipe. The dropped dense stats layer (7.15-2 §5) sets a limit the report respects: whole-game aggregates only, no per-tick or time-windowed stats. Whole-game temporal aggregates such as flip counts are aggregates rather than time series, and stay within the limit.

Two standing facts shape what the stats can be:

- The fixture is frozen, so the report is recomputed only at re-bless. A stat's delta is therefore pure behavior signal, and the design question for each stat is whether its delta is interpretable, not whether the stat is stable.
- The report and the hash references are written by the same regen pass from one computation, so informational duplication between them cannot drift.

## 2. Principles

Four principles govern the recipes, and future recipe changes should honor them.

- **Stats are functions of the emitted channel bytes only** (the same domain the hashes cover). Anything computed from the fixture's inputs is frozen by construction: it can never move across a re-bless, so it is fixture documentation, not a stat. The example that produced the rule: "how many cells did player X take" is not computable from the transition channels, because the encoder keys on the older owner and never encodes the gainer.
- **Structure is observational, and assumptions are demoted to annotations.** The tables record what the channels did. Our beliefs about what the values mean live in legend lines, where a wrong belief can only mislabel a correct count, never misfile one. Assumptions are kept only where being wrong is visible in the output and harmless to the data.
- **Expected set plus overflow, for categorical values.** Count columns cover the expected value set (the layout stays stable, and a zero count is itself informative). Observed out-of-set values get appended columns with a `?` marker. Nothing is dropped and nothing crashes: an out-of-set value diffs as a new header plus a populated column.
- **Point masses plus moments, for continuous values.** Exact-value counts for the point-mass values (0 and -1), distribution stats over everything else. The two patterns are the same idea at different depths: categorical channels get a full value histogram, continuous channels get a two-bucket histogram plus moments.

One property follows from these principles taken together: the writer carries a small set of hand-maintained assumptions (the channel-to-table classification map, the expected value sets, the legends), and every one of them fails visibly rather than silently. To keep the property complete, the writer fails loudly on a channel its classification map does not cover: no guessed recipe, no silent skip. How that failure is detected and surfaced is worked out at build time.

## 3. The channel map

The default config point emits 110 channels: 86 base, 10 dense-history at n=5, and 14 player-status. The legacy point emits 96 (no player-status group). Two axes place every channel: its class (7.20-3) and its layout. A *spatial* channel has real per-cell structure, and a *broadcast* channel holds one value repeated over the whole board. Recipes are per (class × layout) cell.

| class | spatial | broadcast | total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| binary | 30 | 21 | 51 |
| category-encoded-exact | 5 | 7 | 12 |
| int-scaled-by-divisor | 0 | 24 | 24 |
| log-scaled | 16 | 0 | 16 |
| inactive | 0 | 7 | 7 |
| **total** | **51** | **59** | **110** |

The two empty class cells are structural: every int-scaled channel is a scoreboard-derived scalar, and every log-scaled channel is a per-cell magnitude.

One deviation from the docs' census: 7.20-1 counted the 7 zero-stub `city_inference` channels as binary (giving binary 58). This report records them as `inactive` instead, because a constant-zero channel has no behavior to classify (§5.6).

Properties the recipes rely on:

- The 28 step channels (21 binary broadcast flags, 7 `captured_by`) are step functions, today with at most one transition per walk.
- The dense-history channels (5 `ownership_transition`, 5 `army_delta`) are lagged copies of one signal each: the encoder computes each (t, t-1) pair once, and the five lag channels re-read the same stored arrays at different offsets. Whole-game stats of the five rows are near-identical, differing only by edge truncation at the walk's start and end.
- The 30 binary masks split into cumulative (once-known-stays-known: `mountains`, `cities`, `generals`, `historically_seen`, the 7 `opp_N_has_seen`) and fluctuating (`fog_cells`, the 8 ownership masks, the 9 `last_seen_owner` one-hots). `structures_in_fog` is expected to unlatch when a structure resolves, to be verified at build (it affects only how its losses column is read).
- The 16 log-scaled channels have four value structures:

| sub-group | channels | -1 present? | what zero means | signed? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| visibility-masked magnitude | `army_magnitude` (1) | no | fog, or a visible empty cell | no |
| memory scalars | `last_seen_armies`, `turns_since_seen` (2) | yes (never-seen) | a real value (seen-empty cell, currently-visible cell) | no |
| BFS distances | `bfs_self_general`, `bfs_opp_1..7` (8) | yes (unreachable, unrevealed, or general unknown) | a real value (the general's own cell) | no |
| signed deltas | `army_delta_t-1..5` (5) | no | not-both-observed, or observed no change | yes |

- Values are not bounded by 1: `timestep` reaches about 2.0 under the T ≤ 2000 eligibility gate, army counts exceed 1 late-game, and `land_delta` is signed. Column widths therefore need the range sweep (§8).

## 4. The report's format

**Markdown, one report per (fixture, config point).** 7.15-2 specified JSON on the grounds that it diffs and deltas well in git. We moved off JSON after tracing the actual consumers: the machine path (the checker's comparisons, regen's printed fire extent) runs on the hash references, not on the report, and the report's one real consumer is a human reading a git diff. Markdown tables serve that consumer better: one row per channel, so a change diffs as exactly the changed rows, no repeated key names, and the file renders as actual tables. If a future tool ever wants the stats programmatically, it parses the strictly formatted markdown, or a machine-format twin is added at that point (the standard revival-trigger shape).

**Layout: a provenance header, then seven tables.**

- The header records the hash algorithm and digest size, the canonical serialization (C-order bytes in the emitted dtype), and the unpadded-region stats rule (below). It exists for the record rather than for the machine: the checker uses a code constant, and the recorded copy answers "which algorithm produced these hashes" for a future investigation.
- One table per recipe. The class record is table membership: each heading carries the full class and layout name once, and no row carries a class label. A reclassification diffs as a row deleted from one table and added to another: a loud change that matches the significance of the event.

**Shared columns.**

- Every table carries the channel's hash (16 hex chars), a display duplicate of the `[C]` reference that cannot drift from it, because both come from the same regen pass. The case that justifies it: a mid-walk change can leave whole-game aggregates visually identical while the hash flips, and the reviewer still sees which channels changed in the same diff.
- Spatial tables carry `com`, the whole-game center of mass of active cells, formatted `(r̄, c̄)` in board coordinates (rationale in §5.7).

**Alignment and printing.**

- Column widths are fixed constants in the writer, never derived from content. Content-derived widths are what pollute diffs: one wide value re-pads a whole column and touches every row. Buffers are sized by an empirical range sweep at build time.
- Numeric print formats are fixed (for example, four decimals), so numeric columns cannot overflow by construction. A variable-width cell that exceeds its buffer (a long transitions list) mis-aligns its own row only, and neighbors do not re-pad.
- Fixed rounding also gives the right churn behavior: sub-print-precision drift is invisible in the stats while the hash column still flips. That split is exactly the signature the class record predicts for legitimate environment churn, which can only occur in the log-scaled rows.
- A legend line above a table carries its semantic annotations (a value-to-meaning map, or the per-sub-group reading of `mean`).
- An empty stats domain prints a dash, never NaN. The known case is an all-sentinel BFS channel for a general the perspective never discovers.

**Padding.** The hashes cover the padded emitted bytes ("hash what the config emits" extends to shape), so detection has no padding blind spot. Stats are computed over the unpadded H×W board region only, declared once in the header. The zero counts force this rule (they are not padding-immune), and it is adopted uniformly across the recipes so there is a single rule.

**Config points share recipes.** Recipes are class-driven, so the legacy point reuses them wholesale: it differs only in channel set (96) and emitted dtype (fp32).

## 5. The seven tables

Each recipe below gives its channels, columns, a mockup row (plausible values, not real output), and the recipe-local reasoning. Reviewer-facing reading patterns are collected in §6.

### 5.1 Step traces — broadcast flags and codes (28 channels)

The 21 binary flags (`opp_N_contacted`, player-status) and the 7 `opp_N_captured_by` codes. Columns: `initial`, `transitions` (a list of tick→value entries).

```markdown
| channel            | hash             | initial | transitions      |
|--------------------|------------------|---------|------------------|
| opp_3_is_alive     | e77d10f38b52c6a0 | 1       | 842→0            |
| opp_3_captured_by  | 2b94d1c07f6e8a43 | 0       | 842→5            |
```

For a step signal with few transitions, the initial value plus the transition list is lossless at two or three numbers, which beats any aggregate. The transitions field is a list rather than a single slot because "at most one transition per walk" is a fact about current behavior, not a schema guarantee: if a latch-once channel ever re-flips, the list records it as a second entry that stands out in the diff, instead of forcing the writer to error or to keep only the first. The list is capped (first N entries plus a total count) against pathological cases. The cap is never reached today.

### 5.2 Numeric broadcast (24 channels)

The 8 army counts, 8 land counts, `timestep`, and 7 `land_delta`. Columns: `min`, `mean`, `max`, `final`, in emitted units, computed from the broadcast scalar (one cell of the board region), fixed decimals.

```markdown
| channel            | hash             | min     | mean    | max     | final   |
|--------------------|------------------|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| self_army_count    | 71afc5d2e0934b18 | 0.0010  | 0.1982  | 0.4551  | 0.4121  |
```

`min` is the weakest of the four but cheap, and it is needed for the signed `land_delta` (the largest loss). Percentiles (p10/p90) were considered and deferred: no reviewer question was named that the four stats miss at the sanity-check level, and they are a two-column addition later if a real diff leaves us wanting.

### 5.3 Transition histograms (5 channels)

The `ownership_transition_t-1..5` channels. Columns: one count per expected nonzero value (headers are the raw values), any overflow columns, and `com`. A legend line maps values to meanings.

```markdown
Legend: -1 = self lost · 0.5 = neutral lost · 1+k/8 = loss by canonical opp k

| channel                  | hash             | -1  | 0.5 | 1.125 | 1.25 | 1.375 | 1.5 | 1.625 | 1.75 | 1.875 | com          |
|--------------------------|------------------|-----|-----|-------|------|-------|-----|-------|------|-------|--------------|
| ownership_transition_t-1 | 3fa8c91d22e07b56 | 41  | 388 | 12    | 96   | 30    | 0   | 55    | 8    | 0     | (14.2, 17.8) |
```

Each count is the number of cells in that category, summed over the whole walk. The 0 bucket is deliberately absent: it is the overwhelming bulk (almost every cell, every frame, is "no change"), it carries no reviewable signal, and dropping it makes every reported count padding-immune. Per-opponent columns are kept rather than a lumped `opp_lost` because attribution is what makes an encoding change readable: a recode diffs as counts migrating between columns. The five rows are near-identical by the lagged-copy structure (§3). That redundancy is left visible as a standing invariant rather than deduplicated away.

### 5.4 Binary masks (30 channels)

The spatial masks: `fog_cells`, ownership, map knowledge, `last_seen_owner`, `historically_seen`, `opp_N_has_seen`. Columns: `mean_nnz`, `final_nnz`, `gains`, `losses`, `com`.

```markdown
| channel            | hash             | mean_nnz | final_nnz | gains | losses | com          |
|--------------------|------------------|----------|-----------|-------|--------|--------------|
| historically_seen  | 4c80be2217aa9035 | 214.6    | 407       | 382   | 0      | (15.1, 16.4) |
```

`mean_nnz` is the per-frame average count of 1-cells (the main magnitude stat), and `final_nnz` the end state. `gains` and `losses` are whole-game counts of 0→1 and 1→0 cell flips, split into two columns rather than one flips total: cumulative masks then display `losses = 0` as a free monotonicity check, read off the output rather than assumed by the writer. The expected value set is {0, 1}, so a "binary" channel emitting anything else spawns an overflow column, which is the observational class check.

### 5.5 Log-scaled (16 channels)

Columns: `n(-1)`, `n(0)`, `n_active`, `min`, `mean`, `max`, `com`. The stats domain is uniform across all 16 rows: cells not equal to -1 and not equal to 0.

```markdown
Legend: mean is over active cells (armies where present, staleness where stale, distance where reachable, delta where changed)

| channel            | hash             | n(-1)   | n(0)    | n_active | min     | mean    | max     | com          |
|--------------------|------------------|---------|---------|----------|---------|---------|---------|--------------|
| bfs_opp_2_general  | 0d62e8b1a4c97f55 | 191204  | 601     | 214471   | 0.6931  | 2.8410  | 3.9120  | (16.0, 15.2) |
```

`n(-1)` and `n(0)` are exact-value counts, not "sentinel" counts: the writer makes no claim about what the values mean (that lives in the legend), and rows without a -1 convention simply read `n(-1) = 0`, a column that doubles as an anomaly display if a -1 ever appears where none should. The uniform domain rule costs a little: `mean` reads slightly differently per sub-group (the legend sentence covers it), and zero is overloaded for `army_magnitude` and `army_delta`, so a zero↔nonzero shift shows in `n_active` rather than in the moments. One of the three counts is derivable from the fixed per-fixture total. All three are printed anyway, because making a reviewer do subtraction across a diff is a false economy.

### 5.6 Inactive (7 channels)

The zero-stub `opp_N_city_inference` group. Columns: `n_nonzero`, `min`, `max`, computed over all board-region cells (no domain rule, since there is no expected structure).

```markdown
| channel               | hash             | n_nonzero | min     | max     |
|-----------------------|------------------|-----------|---------|---------|
| opp_1_city_inference  | b3e91f0a77c24d68 | 0         | 0.0000  | 0.0000  |
```

Quarantining the stubs dissolves their classification question: a constant-zero channel has no behavior to classify, and the honest record is "inactive". Classification happens at activation, when there is behavior to classify from, and the reclassification (rows migrating to a real table) rides the same commit as the behavior change and its boundary record. The columns cover the leak case: `n_nonzero` says how widespread, `min` and `max` say the sign and scale of whatever appeared.

### 5.7 The `com` column (shared by the spatial tables)

Counts and moments are blind to one realistic bug class: multiset-preserving spatial errors, such as a transposed reshape, an index offset, or a padding-placement change. Every value survives, every count is exact and identical, and only positions move. `com` (whole-game center of mass of the active cells) is the one whole-game aggregate that sees this class, and because averaging is linear the signatures survive aggregation exactly: a transpose diffs as the two coordinates swapping, an offset as both shifting by the offset.

"Active" means the cells in each table's stats domain: the 1-cells for masks, the nonzero cells for the histograms, and the non-point-mass cells for the log table.

The trade: the class is hash-detected regardless, and the rerun recipe diagnoses it on demand in about 0.2s. `com` adds orientation (the diff itself shows the change is positional), and with it the report's remaining blind spot narrows to timing-only changes (§6).

## 6. Reading the report

This section collects the reading patterns the recipes were designed around. The goldens workflow doc (planned in 7.15-2 §4) is the long-term home for fire-reading guidance and should absorb this material. It is recorded here because this is where it was derived.

**Fire signatures.**

- The first read of an accidental fire is breadth: which tables changed. Cross-channel patterns (fog plus ownership plus transitions) usually point at the responsible subsystem faster than any single row.
- Hashes flipped broadly while stats stay quiet: a serialization- or padding-layer change (stats never see the padding).
- Hash flips confined to log-scaled rows with stats quiet: the environment-churn signature. Any flip outside the log table is a behavior change.
- Counts move while magnitudes hold (or the reverse): the two axes separate rule changes from encoding changes. The BFS rows are the clean example: a passability-policy change moves `n(-1)` and `n_active` with magnitudes stable, and a distance-encoding change does the opposite.
- A row whose hash flipped but whose aggregates are identical: a timing or positional change. `com` separates the two (positional moves it, timing does not).
- Worked examples from project history: a 6.18-style tick-seam change diffs as one step-trace row, `842→0` becoming `843→0`. A divisor change scales all four numeric-broadcast stats by the ratio, uniformly across the affected rows. A transition-encoding recode diffs as histogram counts migrating between columns.

**Free invariants** (all read off the output, none assumed by the writer):

- The five `ownership_transition` rows and the five `army_delta` rows are each near-identical. Divergence beyond edge effects means the lag-buffer logic itself broke.
- `losses = 0` on cumulative masks. A nonzero value there is a monotonicity violation, visible directly in the diff.
- The nine `last_seen_owner` rows' nnz roughly partitions `historically_seen`'s nnz.
- `turns_since_seen`'s derivable zero count approximates the visible-cell count, cross-checking `fog_cells`' `mean_nnz`.
- `n(-1) = 0` on log rows with no -1 convention.
- No overflow columns anywhere means every observed value sat in its expected set.
- All-zero inactive rows mean the stubs are still inactive.
- An all-dash BFS row documents that the fixture never discovers that general, which doubles as fixture-selection information.

**Accepted blind spots.**

- Timing-only changes (same value multiset, different ticks): every aggregate including `com` is identical, and only the hashes flip. The rerun recipe owns the diagnosis.
- Zero overloading in `army_magnitude` and `army_delta` (§5.5).
- The report inherits 7.15-2's marginal attribution model: a fire yields the changed tick set and the changed channel set, not the joint pairing. The stats are whole-game per-channel aggregates, so they are channel-marginals too. Overlapping-cause fires stay the rerun recipe's case.

## 7. Decision record

The decisions whose reasoning sets reusable patterns, compressed. Chat-level alternatives that merely lost on taste are omitted.

- **Semantic histogram headers were rejected** (columns named `self` / `neutral` / `opp1..7`). Two failure modes: the writer would own a value-to-meaning map that can drift silently, leaving correct counts under stale labels, and out-of-set values have nowhere to go. Raw-value headers with a legend line keep the counts unconditionally correct and give novel values a visible landing place.
- **A sentinel-count column was rejected for the log table.** It would put per-row semantics (with the match value varying by sub-group) back into structure. Exact-value counts (`n(-1)`, `n(0)`) dissolve the problem and unify with the histogram pattern.
- **A gainer-keyed transition count was rejected**, which produced the emitted-bytes principle (§2): the stat wasn't computable from the channel, and a sim-derived version could never move across a re-bless.
- **`com` is justified positionally, not as a magnitude stat.** Judged on magnitude it is marginal. Asking which bug class the counts provably miss named the multiset-preserving class (§5.7) and gave `com` its real job.
- **Aligned columns were kept** after the diff-churn objection was traced to content-derived widths specifically. Fixed-constant widths remove the churn while keeping the report readable raw, which serves the secondary use cases.
- **`city_inference` left the census's binary bucket for the inactive quarantine**, on the grounds that structure should describe what is rather than what is planned.
- **Percentiles were deferred twice** (numeric broadcast, log-scaled), each time for the same reason: no named reviewer question that the kept stats miss.

## 8. Build-time items

Deferred mechanics, listed here so the build session doesn't have to rediscover them. None of them block drafting the writer.

- The value-range sweep over eligibility-gated fixtures that sizes the column-width buffers.
- The hash algorithm pick (blake2b vs sha256), recorded in the provenance header either way.
- Machine-side packaging of the `[T]`/`[C]` hash references (likely a small npz beside the other references). The report's hash column is a display duplicate.
- Report file naming and sub-path under `packages/training/tests/goldens/`.
- Flip counting starts at the second walked frame (the first has no predecessor).
- Dash rendering for empty stats domains.
- Verifying whether `structures_in_fog` unlatches (affects only how its `losses` column is read).
- The exact mechanism by which the writer detects and reports an unclassified channel (§2).

## 9. Out of scope

- **The targets diffability gap.** The report is obs-side. Targets references have no diffable summary artifact, so targets bless-review rests on regen's printed extent plus the boundary record's fields. The elim arc will fire targets goldens frequently, so this may deserve attention later. It is observed here, not solved, and the existing docs already accept it.
- **Time-windowed or phase stats**, considered early and rejected: they would creep back over the dropped-dense-layer boundary, and tick localization already belongs to the frame hashes.
- **The walk-seam discussion**, the other open pre-build item (7.15-2 §8).
