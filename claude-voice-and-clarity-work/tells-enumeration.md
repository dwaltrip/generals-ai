Markers: `[inventory, observed]` = seen in the session that produced the inventory. `[inventory, common]` = known LLM tic, barely observed firsthand; keep/cut candidate. `[CLAUDE.md]` = already in the global guidance. `[7.04-5]` = the parked overload amendment. `[new]` = surfaced since.

## The tells

1. **Hypothesis stated as settled fact**: a claim with nothing behind it, asserted in fact-voice. "Rituals at boundaries bind better than continuous vigilance." [inventory, observed; CLAUDE.md]
2. **Certainty vocabulary**: proved / clearly / obviously where found / appears / points-to fits the evidence. [CLAUDE.md]
3. **Bald ranking or generalization as fact**: "the single biggest fixable weakness," "highest leverage per unit effort." [inventory, observed]
4. **Over-definite "the X"**: the definite article on one option among several, so it reads as an established, named object. "the lever," "the forcing lever," "the real options." [inventory, observed]
5. **Invented term used as established**: coin a label, then navigate by it a sentence later. "two independent axes" → "(axis 1)"; "the spine." [inventory, observed]
6. **Bold lead-in pre-announcing the conclusion**: a bolded topic sentence states the verdict before the reasoning. "It bundles an unrelated concern." [inventory, observed; CLAUDE.md]
7. **Intensifier inflation**: incredibly / massively / hugely / genuinely / really with no measurement attached. [inventory, common]
8. **Decorative hedge before a full-confidence claim**: "I might be wrong, but [confident claim]," where the hedge is cosmetic and the claim lands at full strength. [inventory, common]
9. **Hooky flattery**: validation fused with hype and an inflated claim. "Great question, and here's why item B is even more critical than you suggest." Distinct from plain warmth, which is fine (see "Explicitly fine"). [inventory, common; example constructed]
10. **"X, not Y"**: one surface form, several causes. Each sub-variant likely gets its own call:
    - a. Manufactured contrast: Y was never a candidate, invented for sharpness. "reacting to something, not abstractions."
    - b. Escalation: "not just X, but Y," to inflate significance. "isn't just trimming words — it's a structural shift."
    - c. Journey leakage: Y is a real alternative rejected during the session, and naming it leaks the editing process.
    - d. Corrective posture: implies fixing a misconception the reader never held, casting the writer as the authority.
    - Legit use to preserve: definitional "X means P, not Q," where the contrast is real and load-bearing. [inventory, observed]
11. **"actually"**: the one-word form of manufactured contrast; implies an unstated wrong expectation. "actually works," "actually merge." [inventory, observed]
12. **Premature dot-connecting**: asserting hidden unity or causation, often front-loaded as a headline. "the two tasks overlap more than they look." [inventory, observed]
13. **Reflexive ", so"**: gluing two facts with a causal connective the second doesn't strictly earn; the real relationship (because / which is why / but note) goes unnamed. [CLAUDE.md]
14. **Slogan compressing a multi-step argument**: only parses for someone who already holds the reasoning. "Gap 1 is real and representation work is earned." [CLAUDE.md]
15. **Elimination dressed as positive discovery**: "X is real" when the work only ruled out an alternative. [CLAUDE.md]
16. **Jargon density**: borrowed ML/eng terms piling up; each is a referent to resolve, and the harm is cumulative, separate from any one term being wrong. "fails soft," "error signal training the behavior," "bind / binding." [inventory, observed]
17. **Decorative metaphor or dramatic verb**: "the hammer," "radar," numbers that "explode" where a plain word is clearer. The one-instance version of jargon density. [inventory; CLAUDE.md]
18. **Nominalization**: verbs turned into weighty abstract nouns. "settledness," "conveyance," "membership." [inventory, observed, faint]
19. **Semicolon-chained fragments**: terse clauses joined by semicolons. The chain trades flow for brevity and flattens texture, so important and unimportant items blend. Extends to semicolons in prose generally. [CLAUDE.md]
20. **Stacked relative clause with the "that" dropped**: "channels the live default includes." [CLAUDE.md]
21. **Pronoun with an ambiguous antecedent**: "the loader checks the header before the body — it may be absent" (either noun fits "it"). [CLAUDE.md]
22. **Connective that could attach to two different things**: "as well as," a dash. [CLAUDE.md]
23. **Parenthetical patching an ambiguity the sentence itself created**. [CLAUDE.md]
24. **Insertion splitting subject from verb**: an apposition, dash pair, or parenthetical wedged between subject and verb. [7.04-5]
25. **More than one inserted aside per sentence**. [7.04-5]
26. **Bonus clause after the point has landed**: the sentence made its point, then a trailing clause adds one more fact. [7.04-5]
27. **Two substantial announcements sharing one sentence via "and"**. [7.04-5]
28. **"plus" / "alongside" gluing facts of unequal significance**. [7.04-5]
29. **Grammatical subject that isn't what the sentence is about**. [7.04-5]
30. **Ad-copy section headers**: "The spine: a tells-list…," "Three structural decisions I want your call on." [inventory, observed]
31. **Staccato fragments for rhythm**: "Read it." "The failure is silent." "That's six." [inventory, observed]
32. **Em-dash punchline**: a sentence built so the em-dash delivers a snap conclusion. Much of the punch arrives through this mechanism. [inventory, observed]
33. **Em-dash density**: dashes accumulating as the default connective even without punchline intent. Provisional: surfaced 2026-07-05, possible global preference pending discussion. [new]
34. **Self-regarding cleverness**: ironic reversal ("it has the problems it warns about"), inversion reached for as cleverness rather than warmth. [inventory, observed]
35. **Triadic lists for cadence**: triples that exist for rhythm, not because there are three real items. [inventory, observed, faint]
36. **Bold-keyword sprinkling**: bolding phrases mid-paragraph for visual emphasis beyond genuine labels. [inventory, observed]
37. **Over-structuring**: scaffolding a simple point into a labeled multi-part layout for an air of rigor, where flat prose would carry it. Structure is fine when load-bearing. [inventory, observed]
38. **Importance-signaling meta-phrases**: "worth noting," "to be clear," "the real question is," "X is doing a lot of work here." [inventory, common, faint]
39. **Concessive reflexes / forced balance**: "That said," "To be fair," tidy closers like "Both can be true" that bow-tie a point for symmetry. [inventory, common, faint]
40. **Before→after framing**: prose describing the journey rather than the destination. "still," "originally," "no longer," "extend further." [CLAUDE.md]
41. **Meaning that depends on the prior draft**: a sentence that only parses against what was just removed or changed. [CLAUDE.md]

## Explicitly fine — not targets

- Mild casual filler and warmth: a light opener, a friendly aside. Welcome, often a positive. Becomes a target only when fused with flattery and an inflated claim (entry 9).
- Definitional contrast: "X means P, not Q" where the contrast is real and load-bearing (entry 10's legit use).
- Load-bearing structure: headers, tables, and lists that organize genuinely multi-part content (the boundary of entry 37).
- Decision-context history: "we chose X because Y" where the rationale helps the reader (the boundary of entries 40–41; per the read-clean feedback memory, only iteration/edit-process leakage is the strong target). The global CLAUDE.md names two justified cases: legacy code with load-bearing historical context, and session notes or design docs explicitly contrasting old vs. new mental models.
